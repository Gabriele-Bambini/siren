## 1. Introduction

CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screens measure the effect of repressing individual candidate enhancers on the expression of nearby genes [Fulco 2019; Gasperini 2019; Nasser 2021]. Their pooled data now underpin supervised enhancer–gene predictors such as ENCODE-rE2G, trained and evaluated on a combined table of 10,356 tested K562 element–gene pairs with leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) cross-validation [Gschwind 2026]. Two observations from that literature frame the present study. First, genomic distance to the transcription start site (TSS) is a strong baseline on CRISPR benchmarks [Fulco 2019; Gasperini 2019; Nasser 2021; Moore 2020], so any added feature must be judged against it with uncertainty that respects the dependence between pairs. Second, sequence-to-function models such as Enformer predict distal enhancer effects poorly when probed by in-silico perturbation [Karollus 2023], while recent models report improvements when their gradient scores are added to rE2G [Avsec 2026].

We began from an internal v1.0 analysis of the same table that reported (i) a calibrated distance-plus-ATAC gradient-boosted baseline, (ii) a collapse of transfer to other cell types and screens, (iii) a negative result for Enformer silencing scores, and (iv) a set of failed method variants. A line-by-line audit of that release (`supplementary/AUDIT_v1.md`) found implementation and design problems that bear directly on (ii) and (iii), including an output-bin offset in the Enformer scoring code, an unverified track slice, a bootstrap interval inconsistent with its point estimate, and K562 chromatin features applied to other cell types. This manuscript therefore (a) re-derives all baseline results under LOCO with cluster-aware inference, (b) re-tests transfer with locus-disjoint sources and within-context ceilings, (c) re-runs the Enformer counterfactual with the corrected geometry, track indices and a fidelity control, and (d) documents the audit as a worked example of how such errors arise and are caught. The analyses were pre-specified in a git-timestamped plan (`supplementary/PREREG_v2.md`), and every number below is regenerated by `hpc/run_all.sh` and machine-checked against the text.

We make no claim of a new predictor. Our contribution is a rebuildable, uncertainty-aware reassessment on public data and an account of what the v1.0 conclusions do and do not survive.

## 4. Methods

### 4.1 Data
We used the public EPCrisprBenchmark combined K562 training table [Gschwind 2026] (columns `EffectSize`, `Significant`, `Regulated`, `ValidConnection`, `distanceToTSS`, `Reference`) and its held-out table of five cell types, restricted to valid connections. The table pools FlowFISH, Perturb-seq and TAP-seq screens; we label each pair with its originating screen from the `Reference` field (Gasperini 2019, Fulco-derived FlowFISH, Schraivogel 2020, other). Following the benchmark convention the primary positive class is `Regulated` (a significant decrease in expression; prevalence {{e1/prevalence_reg|.4f}}, n = {{e1/n_regulated|,}} of {{e1/n_pairs|,}} pairs); the v1.0 label `Significant` (any direction, n = {{e1/n_significant|,}}) is reported as secondary (amendment A6). K562 accessibility features come from ENCODE IDR-ranked ATAC peaks (ENCSR956DNB).

### 4.2 Models and features
`logd` = log10(|distance to TSS| + 1). Baselines: M0, the negative `logd` (no fitting); M1, LightGBM on `logd`; M2, LightGBM on `logd` plus K562 ATAC overlap and distance-to-peak; M3, M2 plus the benchmark's per-pair activity annotations (H3K27ac, DHS, CTCF, H3K27me3, H3K4me1 signals and chromatin category). LightGBM 4.6.0 used fixed hyperparameters (300 trees, learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, minimum 40 samples per leaf, seed 0, deterministic mode) with no tuning in primary analyses. Predictors regress the signed effect size; the score for `Regulated` is the negated prediction (predicted repression strength) and, for `Significant`, its absolute value.

### 4.3 Evaluation and inference
Out-of-fold predictions come from leave-one-chromosome-out folds (chromosomes with fewer than 100 pairs share one fold; all training chromosomes differ from the test chromosome, so no halo filter is needed). The primary metric is area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC), with AUROC, precision at the top 2% and 5%, and Spearman correlations of the score with |effect| (`rho_abs`) and of the signed prediction with the signed effect (`rho_signed`) as secondary metrics; the v1.0 manuscript described its correlations as magnitude correlations while computing the signed form, so both are now named. Pairs are not independent: we link pairs that share a gene or whose elements lie within 25 kb on the same chromosome and take connected components as clusters. Confidence intervals are 95% percentile intervals from a cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 12345) that resamples whole clusters and recomputes every model on the same resample, so differences are paired. Families of comparisons are corrected with the Holm procedure. Decision rules (benefit, equivalence within ±0.02 AUPRC, inconclusive) were fixed in `PREREG_v2.md` before any v2 result was computed.

### 4.4 Transfer analyses
Transfer was assessed (i) between screens within the training table (leave-one-screen-out) and (ii) from the K562 training table to the held-out cell types. In every transfer, source pairs sharing a cluster with any target pair were removed before fitting (locus-disjoint transfer, amendment A7); naive transfer is reported as secondary. Each target also receives a native ceiling from within-target cross-validation (within-screen LOCO, or five-fold cluster-grouped cross-validation) with identical features. Targets with fewer than 15 `Regulated` pairs or fewer than 20 clusters are labelled not estimable. K562 ATAC features are used only for K562 targets; other cell types use the cell-type-matched benchmark annotations.

### 4.5 Enformer in-silico silencing
Using the ported Enformer weights `EleutherAI/enformer-official-rough` with `enformer-pytorch` 0.8.12, each eligible pair (element fully inside a 196,608-bp window centred on the TSS; minus-strand genes reverse-complemented) was scored as the change in predicted CAGE at the TSS output bin ±1 when the element is replaced by a GC-matched shuffle (three seeds derived from a hash of the element coordinates). The output covers the central 896 bins × 128 bp of the input, so output bins were indexed after removing 40,960 bp of cropping on each side. The primary score uses the two K562 CAGE tracks (indices 4828 and 5111 in the Basenji/Enformer targets file, SHA-256 recorded in the repository); secondary scores use the mean over all 638 CAGE tracks and the K562 DNase tracks. A null control shuffles an equal-length window containing no benchmark element, and a fidelity control checks that K562 CAGE ranks in the top decile at K562-marker promoters (amendment A8). The primary test is the LOCO change in AUPRC when −Δ is added to M2 on the eligible population (amendment A1).

### 4.6 Additional analyses
Variant methods (LambdaRank, a two-stage classifier-times-regressor, uncertainty-penalised re-ranking, nested-cross-validated hyperparameter search, gene-level covariates) were compared with M2 under the same LOCO folds with paired cluster bootstrap (E6). A sign-composition summary (E5) reports the fraction of significant pairs that are decreases in each population, with Wilson intervals.

### 4.7 Reproducibility and deviations from the plan
All experiments run from `hpc/run_all.sh` under a pinned environment (`hpc/requirements.lock.txt`, `hpc/setup_env.sh`) on the Bocconi HPC cluster (SLURM; one NVIDIA A100 slice for Enformer). Results are written to `results/v2/*.json`, flattened into `results/claims.json` and substituted into this manuscript by `scripts/claims.py`, which fails if a number is typed by hand, a status annotation disagrees with the results, or a banned term appears. Amendments to the pre-specified plan (A1–A8) were made before the corresponding results were computed; each is listed with its rationale in `supplementary/PREREG_v2.md`. The unit-test suite and a synthetic end-to-end demo run in continuous integration.
