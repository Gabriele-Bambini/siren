## 1. Introduction

CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screens measure the effect of repressing individual candidate enhancers on the expression of nearby genes [Fulco 2019; Gasperini 2019; Nasser 2021]. Their pooled data now underpin supervised enhancer–gene predictors such as ENCODE-rE2G, trained and evaluated on a combined table of 10,356 tested K562 element–gene pairs with leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) cross-validation [Gschwind 2026]. Two observations from that literature frame the present study. First, genomic distance to the transcription start site (TSS) is a competitive baseline on CRISPR benchmarks and has long been used as a comparator [Fulco 2019; Gasperini 2019; Nasser 2021; Moore 2020], so any added feature must be judged against it with uncertainty that respects the dependence between pairs. Second, sequence-to-function models such as Enformer [Avsec 2021] predict distal enhancer effects poorly when probed by in-silico perturbation [Karollus 2023], while recent models report improvements when their scores are added to rE2G [Avsec 2026]. The benchmark's source publication already evaluates Enformer on this table, scoring only pairs closer than the model window and setting the others to zero [Gschwind 2026].

We began from an internal v1.0 analysis of the same table that reported (i) a calibrated distance-plus-ATAC gradient-boosted baseline, (ii) a collapse of transfer to other cell types and screens, (iii) a negative result for Enformer silencing scores, and (iv) a set of failed method variants. A line-by-line audit of that release (`supplementary/AUDIT_v1.md`) found implementation and design problems that bear directly on (ii) and (iii), including an output-bin offset in the Enformer scoring code, an unverified track slice, a correlation helper that aligned pandas series on index labels, a bootstrap interval inconsistent with its point estimate, and K562 chromatin features applied to other cell types. This manuscript therefore (a) re-runs the Enformer counterfactual with the corrected geometry, track indices and a fidelity control, asks whether the score adds information to distance and accessibility, and tests the answer in a confirmation pre-specified before any held-out score was computed, (b) re-derives all baseline results under LOCO with cluster-aware inference, (c) re-tests transfer with locus-disjoint sources and within-target references, and (d) documents the audit as a worked example of how such errors arise and are caught. The analyses were pre-specified in a git-timestamped plan (`supplementary/PREREG_v2.md`), and every number in the Abstract, Results and Discussion is regenerated by `hpc/run_all.sh` and substituted into the text by script.

We make no claim of a new predictor. Our contribution is a rebuildable, uncertainty-aware reassessment on public data and an account of what the v1.0 conclusions do and do not survive.

## 3. Discussion

**Main finding.** A sequence-model silencing score adds information to distance and accessibility for nearby enhancer–gene pairs. In LOCO evaluation inside the Enformer window the corrected K562-CAGE score improved a logistic baseline, the gain persisted when the promoter signal, screen power and source screen were controlled, and it was confirmed in a test pre-specified before any held-out score was computed (gain {{e8/primary/C1_LR1-LR0/delta_auprc|.3f}}, interval {{e8/primary/C1_LR1-LR0/ci95/0|.3f}} to {{e8/primary/C1_LR1-LR0/ci95/1|.3f}}), with supervised models refitted on locus-disjoint pairs. The effect is moderate and imprecise (the held-out set has {{e8/n_reg}} positives), the score alone was not detectably better than raw distance there, and the discovery-set gain over a gradient-boosted regression partly reflects that regression's weakness. The direction is in line with reports that sequence models are usable but weak for distal enhancers [Karollus 2023], with the published Enformer evaluation on this table [Gschwind 2026] and, in spirit, with the improvement reported when scores from a different model are added to rE2G [Avsec 2026]; the contribution here is the audited, pre-specified evidence and its confirmation, not the direction. The earlier internal null result is not supported: its headline value was an artefact of a misaligned correlation (reproduced exactly in the bridge analysis), its features carried an output-indexing error and mixed assay tracks, and its design (a small sampled set) cannot settle the question. The population is limited to pairs inside the model window, a minority of the table where distance already ranks well; part of the signal is gene or promoter context (null-window control); the weights are a community port checked only by a rank-based control; and only K562 could be tested. We therefore regard the result as evidence of complementary information, not of a deployable predictor.

**Baselines.** On the public K562 table, a ranking by log-distance that involves no training is the baseline to beat: it reproduces the published AUPRC, and neither a gradient-boosted regression on distance and accessibility nor the benchmark's annotations improved on it detectably under LOCO with cluster-aware intervals; the regression was lower. Logistic regression moved in the right direction without being distinguishable from it. Published supervised predictors that combine activity and contact reach much higher AUPRC ({{v1/lit_rE2G_auprc|.2f}} for rE2G), so the simple models studied here should not be read as a ceiling.

**Methodological points.** (i) A single held-out chromosome gave the opposite ordering of M2 and distance to the pooled LOCO result. (ii) Constant-width conformal intervals attain nominal coverage only on average while under-covering the pairs that matter; Mondrian intervals repair this, a known technique applied here to a new table. (iii) The one-sidedness of significant effects varies by population, so any constant-predictor baseline must be computed on the evaluated population. (iv) Pairs from different screens test the same loci, so cross-screen transfer needs locus-disjoint sources, which are largely unavailable.

**Limitations.** The study uses one cell type for training and one public table; its labels depend on screen power, and "not regulated" is not "no effect". The effective sample is the number of independent loci, far smaller than the number of pairs, which is why several contexts were not estimable; the held-out K562 slice tests cross-screen rather than cross-cell-type transfer. Accessibility comes from one ENCODE experiment. The Enformer confirmation covers one cell type (K562) and a small number of positives, and the held-out set shares genes with the training table, so it is independent by screen and not by locus. Enformer analyses use a community port of the published weights, checked with a rank-based fidelity control but not compared numerically with the original implementation, one scoring rule, and the window-eligible subset only; the null windows were chosen from a fixed list of offsets, so distance matching is approximate. The pre-specification lives in the repository's own git history, as one author's commits made within a single working day without an external timestamp; eight of the first eighteen amendments were made after results had been seen (classifiers, post-hoc Enformer contrasts, the bridge and sensitivity analyses, extra labels and status labels), and amendments A11 to A19 record each, with whether results had been seen. Confidence intervals condition on fixed out-of-fold predictions (models are not refitted inside the bootstrap), so they omit training variance, and several rest on few clusters. Published rE2G and activity-by-contact values are quoted from the source's Supplementary Table 3. The conformal selection analysis is exploratory and its labels are relative to benchmark calls, not to ground truth.

**Recommendations.** Report an unfitted distance baseline next to any new predictor; evaluate with every chromosome held out in turn and cluster-aware intervals; state which positive class is used; avoid selecting a winner on the test set; report conditional as well as marginal coverage, in line with reporting recommendations for machine learning in biology [Walsh 2021; Kapoor 2024].

## 4. Methods

### 4.1 Data
We used the public EPCrisprBenchmark combined K562 training table [Gschwind 2026] (columns `EffectSize`, `Significant`, `Regulated`, `ValidConnection`, `distanceToTSS`, `Reference`) and its held-out table of five cell types, restricted to valid connections. The table pools FlowFISH, Perturb-seq and TAP-seq screens; we label each pair with its originating screen from the `Reference` field (Gasperini 2019, Fulco-derived FlowFISH, Schraivogel 2020 [Schraivogel 2020], other). Following the benchmark convention the primary positive class is `Regulated` (a significant decrease in expression); the v1.0 label `Significant` (any direction) is secondary (amendment A6). K562 accessibility features come from ENCODE IDR-ranked ATAC peaks (ENCSR956DNB).

### 4.2 Models and features
`logd` = log10(|distance to TSS| + 1). Baselines: M0, the negative `logd` (no fitting); M1, LightGBM [Ke 2017] on `logd`; M2, LightGBM on `logd` plus K562 ATAC overlap and distance-to-peak; M3, M2 plus the benchmark's per-pair activity annotations (H3K27ac, DHS, CTCF, H3K27me3, H3K4me1 signals, ubiquitous-expression flag and chromatin category). LightGBM 4.6.0 used fixed hyperparameters (300 trees, learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, minimum 40 samples per leaf, seed 0, deterministic mode) with no tuning in primary analyses. Predictors regress the signed effect size; the score for `Regulated` is the negated prediction (predicted repression strength) and, for `Significant`, its absolute value. Classifiers (E7, post hoc) were a standardised logistic regression and a LightGBM classifier with monotone constraints (distance decreasing, accessibility overlap increasing, distance-to-peak decreasing).

### 4.3 Evaluation and inference
Out-of-fold predictions come from leave-one-chromosome-out folds (chromosomes with fewer than 100 pairs share one fold; all training chromosomes differ from the test chromosome, so no halo filter is needed). The primary metric is area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC), with AUROC, precision at the top 2% (ties at the cut-off count their expected hit rate), precision at the threshold giving 70% recall, the Spearman correlation of the magnitude score with |effect| and of the signed prediction with the signed effect as secondary metrics. Pairs are not independent: we link pairs that share a gene or whose elements lie within 25 kb on the same chromosome and take connected components as clusters. Confidence intervals are 95% percentile intervals from a cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 12345) that resamples whole clusters of fixed out-of-fold predictions and recomputes each metric on the same resample, so differences are paired; models are not refitted inside the bootstrap. Families of comparisons are corrected with the Holm procedure. Decision rules were fixed in `PREREG_v2.md` before any v2 result was computed: "adds over distance" in E1 and E7 requires a Holm-adjusted p below 5% and a gain of at least 0.01; BENEFIT in E3 and E6 requires an interval above zero, a gain of at least 0.02 and, within a family, Holm-adjusted p below 5%; equivalence means an interval within ±0.02; otherwise a comparison is inconclusive. Later amendments (A11 and A14 to A19; A20 was fixed before its results) tightened or added labels and analyses after results were seen and are disclosed as such. Intervals condition on the out-of-fold predictions, which are not refitted inside the bootstrap.

### 4.4 Transfer analyses
Transfer was assessed (i) between screens within the training table (leave-one-screen-out) and (ii) from the K562 training table to the held-out cell types. In every transfer, source pairs sharing a cluster with any target pair were removed before fitting (locus-disjoint transfer, amendment A7); naive transfer is reported as secondary. Each target also receives a native reference from within-target cross-validation (within-screen LOCO, or five-fold cluster-grouped cross-validation) with identical features, and the unfitted-distance ranking in the target. Targets with fewer than 15 `Regulated` pairs or fewer than 20 clusters are labelled not estimable. K562 ATAC features are used only for K562 targets; other cell types use the cell-type-matched benchmark annotations.

### 4.5 Enformer in-silico silencing
Using the ported Enformer weights `EleutherAI/enformer-official-rough` with `enformer-pytorch` 0.8.12 [Avsec 2021], following the shuffle-and-read-out-CAGE-at-the-TSS design of Karollus et al. [Karollus 2023] (who shuffled 2 kb windows and averaged 25 shuffles), each eligible pair (element fully inside a 196,608-bp window centred on the TSS; minus-strand genes reverse-complemented) was scored as the change in predicted CAGE at the TSS output bin ±1 when the element is replaced by a GC-matched shuffle (three seeds derived from a hash of the element coordinates). The output covers the central 896 bins × 128 bp of the input, so output bins were indexed after removing 40,960 bp of cropping on each side. The primary score uses the two K562 CAGE tracks (indices 4828 and 5111 in the Basenji/Enformer targets file, SHA-256 recorded in the repository); secondary scores use the mean over all 638 CAGE tracks and the K562 DNase tracks. A null control shuffles an equal-length window containing no benchmark element, chosen from a fixed list of offsets, so distance matching is approximate. A fidelity control checks that K562 CAGE ranks in the top decile at K562-marker promoters (amendment A8). The primary test is the LOCO change in AUPRC when −Δ is added to M2 on the eligible population (amendment A1). Post-hoc contrasts, the bridge to the v1.0 design and the random-shuffle sensitivity analysis are described in amendments A15 to A17. The finding was then tested in a pre-specified confirmation (E8, amendment A20) on the K562 slice of the held-out table, scored with identical code and tracks (`--table heldout --cell-type K562`), with logistic regressions fitted on training-table in-window pairs that share no cluster with any held-out pair; the claim policy (confirmed iff adding the score to the logistic baseline meets the BENEFIT rule) was fixed beforehand. The plan was committed before the scoring job was submitted (commit 15e0533, tag `prereg-v2-e8`; the cluster's accounting records the submission time of the scoring job).

### 4.6 Additional analyses
Variant methods (LambdaRank, a two-stage classifier-times-regressor in two orientations, uncertainty-penalised re-ranking, nested-cross-validated hyperparameter search with chromosome-grouped inner folds, gene-level covariates) were compared with M2 under the same LOCO folds with paired cluster bootstrap and Holm correction over the six variants (E6, primary label only). Conformal analyses (E4) used a cluster-grouped 25% calibration split inside each training set, split-conformal constant-width and per-decile (Mondrian) absolute-residual intervals [Papadopoulos 2002; Angelopoulos 2023], and conformal p-values from non-regulated calibration pairs with Benjamini–Hochberg selection [Jin 2023]. A sign-composition summary (E5) reports the fraction of significant pairs that are decreases in each population, with Wilson intervals.

### 4.7 Reproducibility, review and deviations from the plan
All experiments run from `hpc/run_all.sh` under a pinned environment (`hpc/requirements.lock.txt`, `hpc/setup_env.sh`) on the Bocconi HPC cluster (SLURM; one NVIDIA A100 slice for Enformer). Results are written to `results/v2/*.json`, flattened into `results/claims.json` and substituted into this manuscript by `scripts/claims.py`, which checks that numbers in the Abstract, Results and Discussion are substituted rather than typed (numbers in the Introduction, Methods and tables of constants are exempt), and fails if a status annotation disagrees with the results or uses a forbidden verb, a pipeline step failed, `claims.json` is stale, or a banned term appears. Three independent AI reviews (code, then two of the draft) produced fixes recorded as amendments A14, A16 and A19; under the pre-specified cap of three review rounds no further round was run. Amendments to the pre-specified plan (A1 to A20) are listed with their rationale and with whether results had been seen in `supplementary/PREREG_v2.md`. Scores for the training table were produced by the pipeline at an earlier commit; the scoring script was later extended with options (`--table`, `--cell-type`) that leave its default behaviour unchanged, which was not re-verified by re-running. The unit-test suite and a synthetic end-to-end demo run in continuous integration.
