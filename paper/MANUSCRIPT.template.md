## 1. Introduction

CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screens measure the effect of repressing individual candidate enhancers on the expression of nearby genes [Fulco 2019; Gasperini 2019; Nasser 2021]. Their pooled data now underpin supervised enhancer–gene predictors such as ENCODE-rE2G, trained and evaluated on a combined table of 10,356 tested K562 element–gene pairs with leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) cross-validation [Gschwind 2026]. Two observations from that literature frame the present study. First, genomic distance to the transcription start site (TSS) is a competitive baseline on CRISPR benchmarks and has long been used as a comparator [Fulco 2019; Gasperini 2019; Nasser 2021; Moore 2020], so any added feature must be judged against it with uncertainty that respects the dependence between pairs. Second, sequence-to-function models such as Enformer [Avsec 2021] predict distal enhancer effects poorly when probed by in-silico perturbation [Karollus 2023], while recent models report improvements when their scores are added to rE2G [Avsec 2026]. The benchmark's source publication already evaluates Enformer on this table, scoring only pairs closer than the model window and setting the others to zero [Gschwind 2026].

We began from an internal v1.0 analysis of the same table that reported (i) a calibrated distance-plus-ATAC gradient-boosted baseline, (ii) a collapse of transfer to other cell types and screens, (iii) a negative result for Enformer silencing scores, and (iv) a set of failed method variants. A line-by-line audit of that release (`supplementary/AUDIT_v1.md`) found implementation and design problems that bear directly on (ii) and (iii), including an output-bin offset in the Enformer scoring code, an unverified track slice, a bootstrap interval inconsistent with its point estimate, and K562 chromatin features applied to other cell types. This manuscript therefore (a) re-derives all baseline results under LOCO with cluster-aware inference, (b) re-tests transfer with locus-disjoint sources and within-context ceilings, (c) re-runs the Enformer counterfactual with the corrected geometry, track indices and a fidelity control, and (d) documents the audit as a worked example of how such errors arise and are caught. The analyses were pre-specified in a git-timestamped plan (`supplementary/PREREG_v2.md`), and every number below is regenerated by `hpc/run_all.sh` and machine-checked against the text.

We make no claim of a new predictor. Our contribution is a rebuildable, uncertainty-aware reassessment on public data and an account of what the v1.0 conclusions do and do not survive.

## 3. Discussion

**What the data support.** On the public K562 table, a ranking by log-distance that involves no training is the baseline to beat: it reproduces the published AUPRC, and neither a gradient-boosted regression on distance and accessibility nor the benchmark's annotations improved on it detectably under LOCO with cluster-aware intervals; the regression was lower. Logistic regression moved in the right direction without being distinguishable from it. Published supervised predictors that combine activity and contact reach much higher AUPRC ({{v1/lit_rE2G_auprc|.2f}} for rE2G), so the simple models studied here should not be read as a ceiling.

**Sequence-model silencing scores carry information for nearby elements, but its size depends on the baseline.** After correcting the indexing and track errors of v1.0, the Enformer score for K562 CAGE improved on a distance-plus-accessibility regression (the pre-specified comparison). Against unfitted distance the regression-based combination was not detectably better, whereas the score alone and a logistic model that includes it were, in post hoc contrasts; the score alone was as good as that logistic model, so the information appears to sit mostly in the score. The bridge analysis shows that, under the v1.0 design, the corrected score changes the increment from strongly negative to the value reported above, so the v1.0 null result is not supported; it does not establish how large the effect is in general. This agrees with reports that sequence models are usable but weak for distal enhancers [Karollus 2023] and that adding model-derived scores to rE2G helps [Avsec 2026]. The population is limited to pairs inside the model window, a minority of the table where distance already ranks well, the null-window control shows that part of the signal is gene or promoter context, and the weights are a community port checked only by a rank-based control, so we regard this as evidence of complementary information, not of a deployable predictor.

**Methodological points.** (i) A single held-out chromosome gave the opposite ordering of M2 and distance to the pooled LOCO result. (ii) Constant-width conformal intervals attain nominal coverage only on average while under-covering the pairs that matter; Mondrian intervals repair this, a known technique applied here to a new table. (iii) The one-sidedness of significant effects varies by population, so any constant-predictor baseline must be computed on the evaluated population. (iv) Pairs from different screens test the same loci, so cross-screen transfer needs locus-disjoint sources, which are largely unavailable.

**Limitations.** The study uses one cell type for training and one public table; its labels depend on screen power, and "not regulated" is not "no effect". The effective sample is the number of independent loci, far smaller than the number of pairs, which is why several contexts were not estimable; the held-out K562 slice tests cross-screen rather than cross-cell-type transfer. Accessibility comes from one ENCODE experiment. Enformer analyses use a community port of the published weights, checked with a rank-based fidelity control but not compared numerically with the original implementation, one scoring rule, and the window-eligible subset only; the null windows were chosen from a fixed list of offsets, so distance matching is approximate. The pre-specification lives in the repository's own git history: the plan, its amendments and the results were produced within one day without an external timestamp, and several analyses (classifiers, post-hoc Enformer contrasts, the bridge and sensitivity analyses, extra labels) were added after earlier results were seen; amendments A11 to A17 record each, with whether results had been seen. Published rE2G and activity-by-contact values are quoted from the source's Supplementary Table 3. The conformal selection analysis is exploratory and its labels are relative to benchmark calls, not to ground truth.

**Recommendations.** Report an unfitted distance baseline next to any new predictor; evaluate with every chromosome held out in turn and cluster-aware intervals; state which positive class is used; avoid selecting a winner on the test set; report conditional as well as marginal coverage, in line with reporting recommendations for machine learning in biology [Walsh 2021; Kapoor 2024].

## 4. Methods

### 4.1 Data
We used the public EPCrisprBenchmark combined K562 training table [Gschwind 2026] (columns `EffectSize`, `Significant`, `Regulated`, `ValidConnection`, `distanceToTSS`, `Reference`) and its held-out table of five cell types, restricted to valid connections. The table pools FlowFISH, Perturb-seq and TAP-seq screens; we label each pair with its originating screen from the `Reference` field (Gasperini 2019, Fulco-derived FlowFISH, Schraivogel 2020, other). Following the benchmark convention the primary positive class is `Regulated` (a significant decrease in expression); the v1.0 label `Significant` (any direction) is secondary (amendment A6). K562 accessibility features come from ENCODE IDR-ranked ATAC peaks (ENCSR956DNB).

### 4.2 Models and features
`logd` = log10(|distance to TSS| + 1). Baselines: M0, the negative `logd` (no fitting); M1, LightGBM [Ke 2017] on `logd`; M2, LightGBM on `logd` plus K562 ATAC overlap and distance-to-peak; M3, M2 plus the benchmark's per-pair activity annotations (H3K27ac, DHS, CTCF, H3K27me3, H3K4me1 signals, ubiquitous-expression flag and chromatin category). LightGBM 4.6.0 used fixed hyperparameters (300 trees, learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, minimum 40 samples per leaf, seed 0, deterministic mode) with no tuning in primary analyses. Predictors regress the signed effect size; the score for `Regulated` is the negated prediction (predicted repression strength) and, for `Significant`, its absolute value. Classifiers (E7, post hoc) were a standardised logistic regression and a LightGBM classifier with monotone constraints (distance decreasing, accessibility overlap increasing, distance-to-peak decreasing).

### 4.3 Evaluation and inference
Out-of-fold predictions come from leave-one-chromosome-out folds (chromosomes with fewer than 100 pairs share one fold; all training chromosomes differ from the test chromosome, so no halo filter is needed). The primary metric is area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC), with AUROC, precision at the top 2% (ties at the cut-off count their expected hit rate), precision at the threshold giving 70% recall, the Spearman correlation of the magnitude score with |effect| and of the signed prediction with the signed effect as secondary metrics. Pairs are not independent: we link pairs that share a gene or whose elements lie within 25 kb on the same chromosome and take connected components as clusters. Confidence intervals are 95% percentile intervals from a cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 12345) that resamples whole clusters of fixed out-of-fold predictions and recomputes each metric on the same resample, so differences are paired; models are not refitted inside the bootstrap. Families of comparisons are corrected with the Holm procedure. Decision rules were fixed in `PREREG_v2.md` before any v2 result was computed: "adds over distance" in E1 and E7 requires a Holm-adjusted p below 5% and a gain of at least 0.01; BENEFIT in E3 and E6 requires an interval above zero, a gain of at least 0.02 and, within a family, Holm-adjusted p below 5%; equivalence means an interval within ±0.02; otherwise a comparison is inconclusive. Later amendments (A11, A14 to A16) tightened or added labels after results were seen and are disclosed as such.

### 4.4 Transfer analyses
Transfer was assessed (i) between screens within the training table (leave-one-screen-out) and (ii) from the K562 training table to the held-out cell types. In every transfer, source pairs sharing a cluster with any target pair were removed before fitting (locus-disjoint transfer, amendment A7); naive transfer is reported as secondary. Each target also receives a native ceiling from within-target cross-validation (within-screen LOCO, or five-fold cluster-grouped cross-validation) with identical features, and the unfitted-distance ranking in the target. Targets with fewer than 15 `Regulated` pairs or fewer than 20 clusters are labelled not estimable. K562 ATAC features are used only for K562 targets; other cell types use the cell-type-matched benchmark annotations.

### 4.5 Enformer in-silico silencing
Using the ported Enformer weights `EleutherAI/enformer-official-rough` with `enformer-pytorch` 0.8.12 [Avsec 2021], each eligible pair (element fully inside a 196,608-bp window centred on the TSS; minus-strand genes reverse-complemented) was scored as the change in predicted CAGE at the TSS output bin ±1 when the element is replaced by a GC-matched shuffle (three seeds derived from a hash of the element coordinates). The output covers the central 896 bins × 128 bp of the input, so output bins were indexed after removing 40,960 bp of cropping on each side. The primary score uses the two K562 CAGE tracks (indices 4828 and 5111 in the Basenji/Enformer targets file, SHA-256 recorded in the repository); secondary scores use the mean over all 638 CAGE tracks and the K562 DNase tracks. A null control shuffles an equal-length window containing no benchmark element, chosen from a fixed list of offsets, so distance matching is approximate. A fidelity control checks that K562 CAGE ranks in the top decile at K562-marker promoters (amendment A8). The primary test is the LOCO change in AUPRC when −Δ is added to M2 on the eligible population (amendment A1). Post-hoc contrasts, the bridge to the v1.0 design and the random-shuffle sensitivity analysis are described in amendments A15 to A17.

### 4.6 Additional analyses
Variant methods (LambdaRank, a two-stage classifier-times-regressor in two orientations, uncertainty-penalised re-ranking, nested-cross-validated hyperparameter search with chromosome-grouped inner folds, gene-level covariates) were compared with M2 under the same LOCO folds with paired cluster bootstrap and Holm correction over the six variants (E6, primary label only). Conformal analyses (E4) used a cluster-grouped 25% calibration split inside each training set, split-conformal constant-width and per-decile (Mondrian) absolute-residual intervals [Papadopoulos 2002; Angelopoulos 2023], and conformal p-values from non-regulated calibration pairs with Benjamini–Hochberg selection [Jin 2023]. A sign-composition summary (E5) reports the fraction of significant pairs that are decreases in each population, with Wilson intervals.

### 4.7 Reproducibility, review and deviations from the plan
All experiments run from `hpc/run_all.sh` under a pinned environment (`hpc/requirements.lock.txt`, `hpc/setup_env.sh`) on the Bocconi HPC cluster (SLURM; one NVIDIA A100 slice for Enformer). Results are written to `results/v2/*.json`, flattened into `results/claims.json` and substituted into this manuscript by `scripts/claims.py`, which fails if a number is typed by hand, a status annotation disagrees with the results or uses a forbidden verb, a pipeline step failed, `claims.json` is stale, or a banned term appears. Two independent AI reviews (one of the code before the final results were frozen, one of the draft) produced fixes recorded as amendments A14 and A16. Amendments to the pre-specified plan (A1 to A17) are listed with their rationale and with whether results had been seen in `supplementary/PREREG_v2.md`. The unit-test suite and a synthetic end-to-end demo run in continuous integration.
