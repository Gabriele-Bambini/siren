## Abstract

**Background.** Supervised predictors of CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) enhancer–gene effects are trained and judged on a public table of 10,356 tested K562 element–gene pairs (471 significant decreases). An earlier internal analysis of this table reported a distance-plus-accessibility model as a calibrated reference, a collapse of transfer across cell types, and no information in Enformer silencing scores.

**Methods.** We audited that analysis, pre-specified a replacement plan in a git-committed file, and re-evaluated everything with leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) folds, cluster-aware bootstrap intervals, locus-disjoint transfer and a corrected Enformer implementation. Amendments made after seeing results are listed and labelled post hoc.

**Results.** Ranking pairs by negative log-distance without any fitting reached a pooled AUPRC of 0.438, in agreement with the published value of 0.436.

## 1. Introduction

CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screens measure the effect of repressing individual candidate enhancers on the expression of nearby genes [Fulco 2019; Gasperini 2019; Nasser 2021]. Their pooled data now underpin supervised enhancer–gene predictors such as ENCODE-rE2G, trained and evaluated on a combined table of 10,356 tested K562 element–gene pairs with leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) cross-validation [Gschwind 2026]. Two observations from that literature frame the present study. First, genomic distance to the transcription start site (TSS) is a competitive baseline on CRISPR benchmarks and has long been used as a comparator [Fulco 2019; Gasperini 2019; Nasser 2021; Moore 2020], so any added feature must be judged against it with uncertainty that respects the dependence between pairs. Second, sequence-to-function models such as Enformer [Avsec 2021] predict distal enhancer effects poorly when probed by in-silico perturbation [Karollus 2023], while recent models report improvements when their scores are added to rE2G [Avsec 2026]. The benchmark's source publication already evaluates Enformer on this table, scoring only pairs closer than the model window and setting the others to zero [Gschwind 2026].

We began from an internal v1.0 analysis of the same table that reported (i) a calibrated distance-plus-ATAC gradient-boosted baseline, (ii) a collapse of transfer to other cell types and screens, (iii) a negative result for Enformer silencing scores, and (iv) a set of failed method variants. A line-by-line audit of that release (`supplementary/AUDIT_v1.md`) found implementation and design problems that bear directly on (ii) and (iii), including an output-bin offset in the Enformer scoring code, an unverified track slice, a correlation helper that aligned pandas series on index labels, a bootstrap interval inconsistent with its point estimate, and K562 chromatin features applied to other cell types. This manuscript therefore (a) re-derives all baseline results under LOCO with cluster-aware inference, (b) re-tests transfer with locus-disjoint sources and within-context ceilings, (c) re-runs the Enformer counterfactual with the corrected geometry, track indices and a fidelity control, and (d) documents the audit as a worked example of how such errors arise and are caught. The analyses were pre-specified in a git-timestamped plan (`supplementary/PREREG_v2.md`), and every number in the Abstract, Results and Discussion is regenerated by `hpc/run_all.sh` and substituted into the text by script.

We make no claim of a new predictor. Our contribution is a rebuildable, uncertainty-aware reassessment on public data and an account of what the v1.0 conclusions do and do not survive.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 Unfitted distance reproduces the published baseline
Ranking the 10,356 pairs by negative log-distance (M0), with no fitting, gives a pooled AUPRC of 0.438 for the `Regulated` label (471 positives, prevalence 0.045). The benchmark's source publication reports 0.436 for the same baseline [Gschwind 2026], a difference of 0.0018; precision at the threshold giving seventy percent recall is 0.316 here against 0.316 there. Because distance involves no training, this agreement tests the label definition and the pair set rather than any model. For context, the same publication reports 0.662 for ENCODE-rE2G and 0.613 for the activity-by-contact model; we do not re-implement them.

**A single held-out chromosome can mislead.** The v1.0 analysis used chr19 alone, which holds 43 of the positives. On that chromosome M2 scored 0.441 against 0.407 for M0, the reverse of the pooled ordering. Per-screen breakdowns (exploratory, not in the pre-specified plan) show distance ahead of M2 in Gasperini-derived pairs (0.485 against 0.451) and in Fulco-derived pairs (0.383 against 0.367).

**Correlations are task-dependent.** The Spearman correlation between the score and |effect| was 0.164 for M2 and 0.132 for negative log-distance, whereas the signed correlation of M2 was 0.099. The v1.0 text called its (signed) correlation a magnitude correlation; both are now named, and neither measures the prioritisation task that AUPRC does.

The TRANSFERS label means that the transfer interval overlaps the native interval and exceeds the floor; it is not an equivalence test, and it says nothing about whether the native reference is itself a good model.

The held-out K562 slice (118 positives in 152 clusters) is the same cell type probed by other screens, so it tests cross-screen rather than cross-cell-type transfer. The distance-only model trained on the training table transferred with lift 4.53 (interval 3.38 to 6.21) against a native reference of 4.28 (interval 3.29 to 5.79) and unfitted distance in the target of 6.00; the paired difference between transfer and unfitted distance had interval -2.64 to -0.37. The native reference is itself below unfitted distance here, so the label shows only that nothing is lost relative to a model trained in the target. The model with cell-type-matched annotations gave 7.29 against 7.46.
<!-- claim: e2/contexts/4/feature_sets/FA/decision status=TRANSFERS -->
WTC11 is the only estimable target from another cell type (the threshold of twenty clusters was fixed after the cluster counts of the targets were known, before any model result) (15 positives in 22 clusters): the transferred lift was 33.7 (interval 12.4 to 81.7) and the native reference 12.1 (interval 6.0 to 29.4), with wide intervals because the target has few independent loci and a very low prevalence that inflates lifts. HCT116, GM12878 and Jurkat did not meet the estimability criteria (5, 9 and 5 clusters); their descriptive transferred lifts were 3.5, 3.2 and 5.4, all above one but without intervals.

The Fulco-derived and Schraivogel pairs form only 6 and 17 clusters and are not estimable. The v1.0 cross-context collapse is therefore not reproduced where it can be tested, and it is untestable for three of the five held-out cell types.

### 2.5 Corrected Enformer silencing scores add signal for nearby elements
**Fidelity control.** At K562-marker promoters the mean of the two K562 CAGE tracks had a median rank at the 0.99 percentile among all CAGE tracks; 7 of 9 markers were above the pre-set threshold (HBG2 at the 0.41 and LMO2 at the 0.60 percentile were below it), so the ported weights and track indices pass the pre-set criterion, which cannot by itself separate K562 from related hematopoietic tracks.

**Primary result.** The eligible population is the 1,750 pairs whose element lies inside the TSS-centred window (380 positives, 529 clusters). In this population the regression baseline M2 trained on the window scored 0.436, below unfitted distance (0.497).
<!-- claim: e3/decision/status status=BENEFIT -->
Adding the negated K562 CAGE silencing score to that model raised LOCO AUPRC to 0.512, a gain of 0.076 (interval 0.042 to 0.109); the pre-specified rule labels this BENEFIT. The all-track CAGE variant did not reach that level, whereas the K562 DNase variant did (0.090; Holm-adjusted p = 0.001). On the full table, where the score is missing outside the window, the change was 0.013 (interval -0.028 to 0.061), and the full-table AUPRC of the augmented model (0.402) remained below unfitted distance (0.438).

**Against stronger baselines (post hoc, exploratory).** Because M2 is weaker than unfitted distance, we compared against stronger baselines (amendments A15 and A16).
<!-- claim: e3/contrasts/M2E-dist/status status=INCONCLUSIVE -->
The gradient-boosted regression with the score was not detectably better than unfitted distance (0.015; Holm-adjusted p = 0.591), so most of the primary gain reflects the weakness of that comparator.
<!-- claim: e3/contrasts/nd-dist/status status=BENEFIT -->
The silencing score alone (AUPRC 0.600) exceeded negative log-distance (0.497) by 0.103 (interval 0.028 to 0.169; Holm-adjusted p = 0.008).
<!-- claim: e3/contrasts/LR1-LR0/status status=BENEFIT -->
Adding it to a logistic model on distance and accessibility raised AUPRC from 0.520 to 0.594 (0.074; Holm-adjusted p = 0.002), and the score alone (0.600) was at least as good as that logistic model (0.594) by point estimate, but a direct test of the logistic model against the score alone gave a difference of -0.006 (interval -0.050 to 0.041), so distance and accessibility add little once the score is present. The gradient-boosted regression trained on the whole table and scored in the window reached 0.466; the windowed model with the score exceeded it by 0.047 (Holm-adjusted p = 0.037).

**Is it only promoter strength, or distance?** Adding the reference K562 CAGE signal at the promoter to the logistic baseline and then the silencing score changed AUPRC by 0.065 (interval 0.033 to 0.096). Restricted to pairs farther than ten kilobases from the TSS (1531 pairs, 251 positives), the score alone differed from distance by 0.188 (interval 0.102 to 0.270).

Controlling in addition for the promoter signal, screen power and the source screen, adding the score to a logistic baseline changed AUPRC from 0.596 to 0.641 (0.045; interval 0.020 to 0.069; Holm-adjusted p = 0.001); observed expression was not available as a covariate.

**Element specificity.** The score was more negative in positives than in other pairs (decrease in 0.79 against 0.58); its partial rank correlation with the repression magnitude, given distance and accessibility, was 0.14. In 298 pairs with a non-element window, the element score had AUPRC 0.565 against 0.318 for the null window (difference 0.247, interval 0.109 to 0.382; prevalence 0.211). The element delta was below the null delta in 0.57 of pairs, so part of the signal reflects gene or promoter context rather than the element sequence. The three scrambles gave AUPRC 0.598, 0.573 and 0.590. A composition-preserving random shuffle (amendment A17) correlated with the GC-matched delta at 0.83 and gave AUPRC 0.564 for the score alone; the logistic gain was 0.059 against 0.074 for the GC-matched shuffle, and the score alone was lower with the random shuffle by -0.037 (interval -0.066 to -0.009).

**Comparison with the published evaluation and with v1.0.** The benchmark's publication reports AUPRC 0.493 for Enformer when only pairs inside the model window are scored and the rest are set to zero [Gschwind 2026]. The zero-filled version of our score on the whole table gave 0.461 (interval 0.390 to 0.539) against 0.438 for unfitted distance (difference 0.023, interval -0.044 to 0.085), in the same direction as the published values (0.493 against 0.436) though not detectably different from distance. To separate the effect of each fix from the change of design, we re-ran the v1.0 design (the same 300 pairs, split and model; 293 have a corrected score). Replicating the v1.0 statistic verbatim reproduces its published increment (-0.275) as -0.275. That statistic aligned two pandas series on index labels instead of positions (audit defect D19); the same predictions scored position-aligned give -0.039. With the corrected score on the same pairs and aligned correlations the increment is -0.132 (interval -0.343 to 0.095, including zero), against -0.032 for the buggy score on that subset. The v1.0 headline value was thus an alignment artefact. The corrected score does not produce a positive increment in the v1.0 design, but that design differs from the full analysis in sample (about 114 test pairs), metric and population, and the contribution of the indexing and track fixes was not ablated separately, so the bridge neither confirms nor refutes a null in the v1.0 design.

### 2.6 Constant-width conformal intervals are calibrated only on average
Split-conformal intervals (nominal level ninety percent) with one width for all pairs covered 0.882 of held-out effects overall, below nominal (range across folds 0.83 to 0.95), and coverage fell to 0.56 in the top decile of predicted effect and to 0.57 in the nearest decile of distance, exactly where prioritisation operates. Intervals with one quantile per decile of the predicted magnitude (Mondrian) reduced the largest deviation from nominal coverage from 0.34 to 0.02 at similar mean width (0.127 against 0.131).
<!-- claim: e4/selection/alpha_0.1/status status=INCONCLUSIVE -->
Conformal selection at a target false-discovery rate of ten percent chose 35 pairs, 32 of them positives (false-discovery proportion 0.09, upper bound 0.20; power 0.07), selecting nothing in 11 of 18 folds.
<!-- claim: e4/selection/alpha_0.2/status status=INCONCLUSIVE -->
At twenty percent it chose 63 pairs with false-discovery proportion 0.21 (upper bound 0.31; power 0.11). Both are classified INCONCLUSIVE. A top-k rule that takes the same number of pairs per fold has an identical false-discovery proportion by construction (the ranking is the same), so the analysis shows what fraction an adaptive cut-off would choose, not an advantage over top-k.

### 2.7 One-sidedness of significant effects depends on the population
Among 546 significant pairs, 0.863 are decreases (Wilson interval 0.831 to 0.889), so a constant "repression" call would be right that often. The fraction is 0.741 on chr19, 0.634 among Fulco-derived pairs, 0.896 among Gasperini-derived pairs and 0.948 inside the Enformer window. The v1.0 figure of 0.967 applied to a sampled subset, not to the table.

Relative to M2 (AUPRC 0.389), LambdaRank changed AUPRC by -0.080, uncertainty-penalised re-ranking by -0.166 and gene-level covariates by -0.044. The two-stage product changed it by 0.014 (0.012 for the repression-oriented version) and nested hyper-parameter search by 0.021.

## 3. Discussion

**What the data support.** On the public K562 table, a ranking by log-distance that involves no training is the baseline to beat: it reproduces the published AUPRC, and neither a gradient-boosted regression on distance and accessibility nor the benchmark's annotations improved on it detectably under LOCO with cluster-aware intervals; the regression was lower. Logistic regression moved in the right direction without being distinguishable from it. Published supervised predictors that combine activity and contact reach much higher AUPRC (0.66 for rE2G), so the simple models studied here should not be read as a ceiling.

**Sequence-model silencing scores carry information for nearby elements, but how much depends on the baseline.** After correcting the indexing and track errors of v1.0, the Enformer score for K562 CAGE improved on a distance-plus-accessibility regression (the pre-specified comparison). Against unfitted distance the regression-based combination was not detectably better, whereas the score alone and a logistic model that includes it were, in post hoc contrasts; the score alone was as good as that logistic model, so the information appears to sit mostly in the score. The v1.0 headline value was an artefact of a misaligned correlation (reproduced exactly in the bridge analysis), and its features carried an output-indexing error and mixed assay tracks; its design (a small sampled set) cannot settle the question, and the bridge does not separate the contribution of each fix. The pre-specified analysis on the full eligible population is what finds a gain, and it does not establish how large the effect is in general. This is in line with reports that sequence models are usable but weak for distal enhancers [Karollus 2023], with the published Enformer evaluation on this table [Gschwind 2026], and, in spirit, with the improvement reported when scores from a different model are added to rE2G [Avsec 2026]. The population is limited to pairs inside the model window, a minority of the table where distance already ranks well, the null-window control shows that part of the signal is gene or promoter context, and the weights are a community port checked only by a rank-based control, so we regard this as evidence of complementary information, not of a deployable predictor.

**Methodological points.** (i) A single held-out chromosome gave the opposite ordering of M2 and distance to the pooled LOCO result. (ii) Constant-width conformal intervals attain nominal coverage only on average while under-covering the pairs that matter; Mondrian intervals repair this, a known technique applied here to a new table. (iii) The one-sidedness of significant effects varies by population, so any constant-predictor baseline must be computed on the evaluated population. (iv) Pairs from different screens test the same loci, so cross-screen transfer needs locus-disjoint sources, which are largely unavailable.

**Limitations.** The study uses one cell type for training and one public table; its labels depend on screen power, and "not regulated" is not "no effect". The effective sample is the number of independent loci, far smaller than the number of pairs, which is why several contexts were not estimable; the held-out K562 slice tests cross-screen rather than cross-cell-type transfer. Accessibility comes from one ENCODE experiment. Enformer analyses use a community port of the published weights, checked with a rank-based fidelity control but not compared numerically with the original implementation, one scoring rule, and the window-eligible subset only; the null windows were chosen from a fixed list of offsets, so distance matching is approximate. The pre-specification lives in the repository's own git history, as one author's commits made within a single working day without an external timestamp; eight of the first eighteen amendments were made after results had been seen (classifiers, post-hoc Enformer contrasts, the bridge and sensitivity analyses, extra labels and status labels), and amendments A11 to A19 record each, with whether results had been seen. Confidence intervals condition on fixed out-of-fold predictions (models are not refitted inside the bootstrap), so they omit training variance, and several rest on few clusters. Published rE2G and activity-by-contact values are quoted from the source's Supplementary Table 3. The conformal selection analysis is exploratory and its labels are relative to benchmark calls, not to ground truth.

**Recommendations.** Report an unfitted distance baseline next to any new predictor; evaluate with every chromosome held out in turn and cluster-aware intervals; state which positive class is used; avoid selecting a winner on the test set; report conditional as well as marginal coverage, in line with reporting recommendations for machine learning in biology [Walsh 2021; Kapoor 2024].

## 4. Methods

### 4.1 Data
We used the public EPCrisprBenchmark combined K562 training table [Gschwind 2026] (columns `EffectSize`, `Significant`, `Regulated`, `ValidConnection`, `distanceToTSS`, `Reference`) and its held-out table of five cell types, restricted to valid connections. The table pools FlowFISH, Perturb-seq and TAP-seq screens; we label each pair with its originating screen from the `Reference` field (Gasperini 2019, Fulco-derived FlowFISH, Schraivogel 2020 [Schraivogel 2020], other). Following the benchmark convention the primary positive class is `Regulated` (a significant decrease in expression); the v1.0 label `Significant` (any direction) is secondary (amendment A6). K562 accessibility features come from ENCODE IDR-ranked ATAC peaks (ENCSR956DNB).

### 4.2 Models and features
`logd` = log10(|distance to TSS| + 1). Baselines: M0, the negative `logd` (no fitting); M1, LightGBM [Ke 2017] on `logd`; M2, LightGBM on `logd` plus K562 ATAC overlap and distance-to-peak; M3, M2 plus the benchmark's per-pair activity annotations (H3K27ac, DHS, CTCF, H3K27me3, H3K4me1 signals, ubiquitous-expression flag and chromatin category). LightGBM 4.6.0 used fixed hyperparameters (300 trees, learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, minimum 40 samples per leaf, seed 0, deterministic mode) with no tuning in primary analyses. Predictors regress the signed effect size; the score for `Regulated` is the negated prediction (predicted repression strength) and, for `Significant`, its absolute value. Classifiers (E7, post hoc) were a standardised logistic regression and a LightGBM classifier with monotone constraints (distance decreasing, accessibility overlap increasing, distance-to-peak decreasing).

### 4.3 Evaluation and inference
Out-of-fold predictions come from leave-one-chromosome-out folds (chromosomes with fewer than 100 pairs share one fold; all training chromosomes differ from the test chromosome, so no halo filter is needed). The primary metric is area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC), with AUROC, precision at the top 2% (ties at the cut-off count their expected hit rate), precision at the threshold giving 70% recall, the Spearman correlation of the magnitude score with |effect| and of the signed prediction with the signed effect as secondary metrics. Pairs are not independent: we link pairs that share a gene or whose elements lie within 25 kb on the same chromosome and take connected components as clusters. Confidence intervals are 95% percentile intervals from a cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 12345) that resamples whole clusters of fixed out-of-fold predictions and recomputes each metric on the same resample, so differences are paired; models are not refitted inside the bootstrap. Families of comparisons are corrected with the Holm procedure. Decision rules were fixed in `PREREG_v2.md` before any v2 result was computed: "adds over distance" in E1 and E7 requires a Holm-adjusted p below 5% and a gain of at least 0.01; BENEFIT in E3 and E6 requires an interval above zero, a gain of at least 0.02 and, within a family, Holm-adjusted p below 5%; equivalence means an interval within ±0.02; otherwise a comparison is inconclusive. Later amendments (A11 and A14 to A19) tightened or added labels and analyses after results were seen and are disclosed as such. Intervals condition on the out-of-fold predictions, which are not refitted inside the bootstrap.

### 4.4 Transfer analyses
Transfer was assessed (i) between screens within the training table (leave-one-screen-out) and (ii) from the K562 training table to the held-out cell types. In every transfer, source pairs sharing a cluster with any target pair were removed before fitting (locus-disjoint transfer, amendment A7); naive transfer is reported as secondary. Each target also receives a native reference from within-target cross-validation (within-screen LOCO, or five-fold cluster-grouped cross-validation) with identical features, and the unfitted-distance ranking in the target. Targets with fewer than 15 `Regulated` pairs or fewer than 20 clusters are labelled not estimable. K562 ATAC features are used only for K562 targets; other cell types use the cell-type-matched benchmark annotations.

### 4.5 Enformer in-silico silencing
Using the ported Enformer weights `EleutherAI/enformer-official-rough` with `enformer-pytorch` 0.8.12 [Avsec 2021], following the shuffle-and-read-out-CAGE-at-the-TSS design of Karollus et al. [Karollus 2023] (who shuffled 2 kb windows and averaged 25 shuffles), each eligible pair (element fully inside a 196,608-bp window centred on the TSS; minus-strand genes reverse-complemented) was scored as the change in predicted CAGE at the TSS output bin ±1 when the element is replaced by a GC-matched shuffle (three seeds derived from a hash of the element coordinates). The output covers the central 896 bins × 128 bp of the input, so output bins were indexed after removing 40,960 bp of cropping on each side. The primary score uses the two K562 CAGE tracks (indices 4828 and 5111 in the Basenji/Enformer targets file, SHA-256 recorded in the repository); secondary scores use the mean over all 638 CAGE tracks and the K562 DNase tracks. A null control shuffles an equal-length window containing no benchmark element, chosen from a fixed list of offsets, so distance matching is approximate. A fidelity control checks that K562 CAGE ranks in the top decile at K562-marker promoters (amendment A8). The primary test is the LOCO change in AUPRC when −Δ is added to M2 on the eligible population (amendment A1). Post-hoc contrasts, the bridge to the v1.0 design and the random-shuffle sensitivity analysis are described in amendments A15 to A17.

### 4.6 Additional analyses
Variant methods (LambdaRank, a two-stage classifier-times-regressor in two orientations, uncertainty-penalised re-ranking, nested-cross-validated hyperparameter search with chromosome-grouped inner folds, gene-level covariates) were compared with M2 under the same LOCO folds with paired cluster bootstrap and Holm correction over the six variants (E6, primary label only). Conformal analyses (E4) used a cluster-grouped 25% calibration split inside each training set, split-conformal constant-width and per-decile (Mondrian) absolute-residual intervals [Papadopoulos 2002; Angelopoulos 2023], and conformal p-values from non-regulated calibration pairs with Benjamini–Hochberg selection [Jin 2023]. A sign-composition summary (E5) reports the fraction of significant pairs that are decreases in each population, with Wilson intervals.

### 4.7 Reproducibility, review and deviations from the plan

Results are written to `results/v2/*.json`, flattened into `results/claims.json` and substituted into this manuscript by `scripts/claims.py`, which checks that numbers in the Abstract, Results and Discussion are substituted rather than typed (numbers in the Introduction, Methods and tables of constants are exempt), and fails if a status annotation disagrees with the results or uses a forbidden verb, a pipeline step failed, `claims.json` is stale, or a banned term appears.
