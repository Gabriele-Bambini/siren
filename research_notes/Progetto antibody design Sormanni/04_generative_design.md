## Q1. Which methods exist and how do they work (inputs, what is generated, training data, filters)

### Takeaway
Three technical families dominate. (1) Diffusion/flow generative models conditioned on target plus epitope (RFdiffusion/RFantibody, RFdiffusion3, BoltzGen, and apparently Latent-X2, Chai-2, Nabla JAM, Origin-1, whose internals are only partly disclosed). (2) "Hallucination"/predictor-inversion methods that optimise a sequence by gradient descent through a frozen structure predictor (BindCraft, Germinal, mBER, BoltzDesign1, TorchCraft). (3) Inverse-folding and language-model components used for CDR sequence design and ranking (ProteinMPNN, IgDesign, AbMPNN, IgLM, AbLang2, ESM2). Nearly all pipelines end with a co-folding structure predictor (RoseTTAFold2, AF2-Multimer, AF3, Boltz, Chai-1, Protenix) as the in-silico filter. Open-source: RFantibody, BoltzGen, BindCraft, Germinal (code open, licence unverified). Closed/partner-access: Chai-2, Nabla JAM, Latent-X/X2, AlphaProteo, Origin-1, GeoFlow-V3, IsoDDE.

### Cited Findings

**A. Academic, open-source antibody-specific methods**

- **RFantibody / "RFdiffusion for antibodies" (Bennett, Watson, Baker lab).** Nature 649(8095):183-193, online 5 Nov 2025, print January 2026. A fine-tuned RFdiffusion network is paired with yeast-display screening to generate VHHs, scFvs and full antibodies aimed at user-specified epitopes. The authors state that vanilla RFdiffusion cannot design antibodies de novo because binders built on regular helical/strand interactions do not suit antibody loops. — [PubMed 41193805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41193805/); [IPD news](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/11/rfantibody-in-nature); [GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/ai-designed-antibodies-achieve-atomic-precision-to-enhance-drug-discovery/)
- **RFantibody pipeline (inputs/outputs/filter).** Three parts: antibody-finetuned RFdiffusion for backbone design; ProteinMPNN for sequence design; antibody-finetuned RoseTTAFold2 for in-silico filtering. The RFdiffusion variant requires a framework structure in "HLT" format (conversion script from Chothia-annotated PDBs, SAbDab suggested as the source). The repo ships the frameworks used in the preprint: a nanobody framework and an scFv framework (hu-4D5-8_Fv). The model is fine-tuned to design CDRs only, not frameworks or constant regions; the tutorial advises trying several frameworks per target. The tutorial also says AlphaFold3 or RosettaFold3 can be used to predict/evaluate designs, with an AF3 "binding oracle" recommended in a lab slide deck. — [GitHub RosettaCommons/RFantibody](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFantibody); [tutorial PDF](https://meilerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/rfantibody_tutorial.pdf)
- **RFantibody licensing.** Released freely for academic, personal and commercial use. — [IPD news](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/11/rfantibody-in-nature)
- **RFdiffusion3 (RFD3).** All-atom generative model in which atoms, not residues, are the diffused units. IPD reports it is about 10x faster than RFdiffusion2 and matched or outperformed prior tools on binding and enzyme-design computational benchmarks. Open-sourced (training code and weights) through the RosettaCommons "Foundry" repo in December 2025, together with RFdiffusion3NA (nucleic acids) and RosettaFold3. Paper: bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.09.18.676967 (Butcher, Krishna et al.; Baker senior author); GEN notes it was not peer reviewed at the time. — [IPD](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/12/rfdiffusion3-now-available); [RosettaCommons](https://rosettacommons.org/2025/12/22/rfdiffusion3-is-now-available-in-foundry/); [GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/rfdiffusion3-now-open-source-designs-dna-binders-and-advanced-enzymes); [PMC12458353](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12458353/)
- **Germinal (Mille-Fragoso et al., Hie and Gao labs; Arc Institute/Stanford).** Designs nanobodies and scFvs against user-specified epitope residues. Co-optimises structure and sequence by pairing AlphaFold-Multimer gradients with an antibody language model (abstract names IgLM). GitHub describes the pipeline as hallucination via ColabDesign, selective CDR sequence redesign with AbMPNN, then co-folding with a structure predictor as filter; README mentions Protenix and AbLang integrations under development. A third-party platform says AbLang2 is the main language-model guide and Chai-1 the final co-folding filter (this conflicts with the abstract's IgLM, so the exact LM is unresolved). Code and protocols open-source; one metadata site lists the paper as Nature Biotechnology 2026 (low-confidence, third-party). — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.09.19.677421](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421.full.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/SantiagoMille/germinal); [Tamarind docs](https://docs.tamarind.bio/tools/germinal); [Arc news](https://arcinstitute.org/news/building-germinal-for-ai-designed-antibody-molecules)
- **mBER.** VHH design: builds a nanobody template with NanoBodyBuilder2, optimises binder sequence against AlphaFold-Multimer losses guided by ESM2 or AbLang2 priors, designs variable regions while keeping framework conserved (per vendor docs). Title: "Controllable de novo antibody design with million-scale experimental screening". — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.09.26.678877](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.26.678877.full.pdf); [Tamarind docs](https://docs.tamarind.bio/tools/mber)
- **BoltzGen (MIT; Boltz team).** All-atom generative model for proteins and peptides unifying design and structure prediction; covers binder modalities from nanobodies to disulfide-bonded peptides and targets from disordered proteins to small molecules. Open source under MIT licence with weights, training and inference code. — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.11.20.689494](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494.full.pdf); [PMC12697729](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12697729/); [MIT Jameel Clinic](https://jclinic.mit.edu/research-project/boltzgen)
- **BoltzDesign1 and TorchCraft (predictor inversion).** BoltzDesign1 (bioRxiv April 2025) inverts Boltz-1 using only its Pairformer and confidence modules. TorchCraft (arXiv 2609.19770, September 2026) optimises sequence logits through a frozen all-atom predictor (pretrained AF3 weights, implemented in "TorchFold"), covering minibinders, framework-conditioned VHHs, cyclic peptides and ligand-binding proteins; no peer review found. — [BoltzDesign1 bioRxiv](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.06.647261v1); [TorchCraft arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19770)
- **BindCraft (Pacesa et al., EPFL).** AlphaFold2-based hallucination pipeline for de novo protein (minibinder) binder design, Nature 646:483-492, October 2025; headline 10-100% experimental success, nanomolar affinities without high-throughput screening; MIT licence. Not antibody-specific. — [RosettaCommons](https://rosettacommons.org/2025/09/25/one-shot-design-of-functional-protein-binders-with-bindcraft/); [EPFL news](https://news.epfl.ch/news/an-open-source-ai-platform-to-democratize-protei-2/)
- **IgDesign (Absci, bioRxiv 2023).** Inverse-folding model (built on LM-Design) that redesigns HCDR3 or HCDR1-3 given an antigen-antibody framework structure; first inverse-folding model validated in vitro for antibody binder design (8 antigens, SPR). This is redesign for antigens with known complexes, not zero-prior de novo design. — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2023.12.08.570889](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.08.570889.full.pdf); [Tamarind docs](https://docs.tamarind.bio/tools/igdesign)
- **tFold workflow (Wu et al., Nature Communications 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-67361-9).** Combines antibody structure prediction (tFold-Ab), antibody-antigen complex modelling (tFold-Ag), structure-guided virtual screening and de novo epitope-specific design; antibodies against influenza HA, PD-1, PD-L1 and SARS-CoV-2 RBD with nanomolar affinity by phage display + SPR (per Twist listing; full text not seen). — [Twist listing](https://twistbioscience.com/resources/publication/de-novo-design-epitope-specific-antibodies-structure-driven-computational); [Nature Comms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67361-9)
- **Benchmarks of older generative models.** "Benchmarking Generative Models for Antibody Design" (NeurIPS 2024) used experimental data from seven datasets; found that log-likelihood scores of generative models correlate well with measured binding affinity and found no evidence that pAE and ipTM are suitable for ranking antibody sequence designs. IgFlow ("Flow Matching for De Novo Antibody Design") appeared at NeurIPS 2024. — [NeurIPS 2024 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/102849); [IgFlow listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/102525)
- **2026 academic/ML-venue items (computational only, as far as found).** Proteo-R1 (ICML 2026): a multimodal LM identifies functionally important residues and passes them as hard constraints to a diffusion co-design model; results are computational (antibody redesign RMSD/lDDT), no wet lab found. MultiAbRank (ICML 2026) proposes a multi-objective benchmark for de novo antibody design, arguing existing evaluation relies on reconstruction metrics or structure-confidence proxies. An Amazon "agent-guided" nanobody design study (ICML 2026) used mBER, RFantibody and IgGM against a novel cancer target (no numbers retrieved). — [Proteo-R1 PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/wu26bq.html); [Proteo-R1 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02937); [MultiAbRank](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/71767); [agent-guided nanobody](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/70825)

**C. Pre-generative "epitope-targeted" antibody design (relevant to positioning for a Cambridge/Sormanni-type project)**

- Sormanni, Aprile, Vendruscolo (PNAS 2015) designed antibodies against specific epitopes within intrinsically disordered proteins using a "cascade" method that assembles complementary peptides from beta-strand fragments found in the PDB and grafts them onto a single-domain antibody scaffold. A later fragment-based method (Science Advances 2022) designs CDR loops for pre-selected epitopes; six single-domain antibodies were designed and tested on three antigens including SARS-CoV-2 RBD, and the procedure runs on a laptop. Sormanni received the RSC Norman Heatley Award in 2025; a November 2025 talk covered tailoring binding to predetermined epitopes and simultaneous optimisation of multiple biophysical traits. — [BLOPIG blog](https://www.blopig.com/blog/?p=2842); [RSC prize page](https://www.rsc.org/standards-and-recognition/prizes/winners/dr-pietro-sormanni)

### Inferences
- **Beginner mental model (synthesis, not a sourced claim).** Think of a design pipeline as: (i) a generator proposes antibody loops (and often their docking pose) around a chosen epitope, either by diffusion/flow (denoising a cloud of atoms toward a plausible complex) or by hallucination (nudging a sequence by gradient descent until a frozen predictor "believes" it binds); (ii) an inverse-folding model such as ProteinMPNN, or a language model, writes or scores the amino-acid sequence; (iii) a co-folding predictor (RF2, AF2/AF3, Boltz, Chai-1, Protenix) re-predicts the complex and designs are ranked by interface confidence (ipAE, ipTM, ipSAE) plus extras (language-model likelihood, developability); (iv) tens to hundreds of the top designs are made and tested (yeast display, BLI, SPR). The big 2025 shifts were scale of the in-silico search, better co-folding filters, and moving from minibinders to antibody formats.
- Two design philosophies exist for antibody formats: keep a fixed human/camelid framework and design only CDRs (RFantibody, Germinal, mBER) versus all-atom models that may generate more of the molecule (BoltzGen; Latent-X2 takes an optional framework). Which CDRs each industry model designs is mostly undisclosed in what I could retrieve.
- Open reproducible baselines for a PhD project are RFantibody, BoltzGen, Germinal, BindCraft/mBER; the best-performing claims (Chai-2, JAM-2, Latent-X2, Origin-1, Protenix-v2) are not independently reproducible today.

## Q2. Experimental validation: success rates, targets, affinities, formats, epitope accuracy, novelty

### Takeaway
By 2025-2026 several groups report double-digit per-design hit rates for VHH/scFv/IgG formats against many novel targets with one round of at most a few dozen designs per target (Chai-2 16% over 52 targets; JAM-2 39% VHH-Fc and 18% mAb across 16 targets; Protenix-v2 up to 48% per epitope; Galux 10.5%; Germinal 4-22% over 4 targets; BoltzGen 6 of 9 novel targets with nanobodies). Affinities range from hundreds of nM (open academic tools) to pM-nM (industry claims). Numbers are not comparable across papers because target sets, design budgets, assays and the definition of "hit" differ, and nearly all industry results are preprints or press releases.

### Cited Findings

**Academic / open tools**

- **RFantibody (Nature 2026).** Fine-tuned RFdiffusion + yeast display. The 2024 preprint screened about 9000 designed VHHs against RSV site III and influenza haemagglutinin; the best VHHs bound with 1.4 uM (RSV site III) and 78 nM (HA). In the Nature version, initial designs had modest affinity (tens to hundreds of nM Kd); affinity maturation with OrthoRep gave single-digit nM binders that kept the intended epitope selectivity. Cryo-EM confirmed the designed binding pose for VHHs to influenza HA and Clostridium difficile toxin B (TcdB), with a high-resolution structure of the HA VHH confirming the designed CDRs. De novo scFvs (designed heavy- and light-chain CDRs) were reported against TcdB and a PHOX2B peptide-MHC complex. Related PDB entries: 9NFU, 9NH7. An overall per-design hit rate for nM binders was not found. — [bioRxiv 2024.03.14.585103](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103.full.pdf); [PubMed 41193805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41193805/); [HyperAI summary](https://hyper.ai/en/news/38253); [RCSB 9NFU](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9NFU)
- **Germinal (bioRxiv Sept 2025; 4 targets).** Experimental success 4-22% with 43-101 designs tested per antigen (a secondary summary lists 101 PD-L1, 46 IL-3, 43 IL-20, 52 BHRF1 designs). Best binders approximately 170 nM (PD-L1), 560 nM (IL-3), 190 nM (IL-20), 140 nM (BHRF1); Arc describes the best nanobody as 140 nM. Designs expressed well in mammalian cells. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421.full.pdf); [Arc news](https://arcinstitute.org/news/building-germinal-for-ai-designed-antibody-molecules); [Decoding Bio summary](https://decodingbio.substack.com/p/biobyte-133-reversing-the-game-of); [BioPharmaTrend](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/news/generative-ai-framework-produces-functional-nanobodies-with-reduced-screening-1404/)
- **BoltzGen (bioRxiv Nov 2025).** Eight wet-lab campaigns covering 26 targets overall. Nanobodies against 9 novel targets (no protein with >30% sequence identity in a bound context anywhere in the PDB): with 15 or fewer designs per target, nM binders for 66% of those targets (6 of 9, my arithmetic). The nanobody campaign was run by Adaptyv Bio. Authors state that some data are confidential at collaborators' request, additional validation is ongoing, and that high affinity alone does not establish therapeutic efficacy. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494.full.pdf); [PMC12697729](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12697729/); [MIT Jameel Clinic](https://jclinic.mit.edu/research-project/boltzgen)
- **mBER (bioRxiv Sept 2025).** Per Tamarind's documentation of the paper: over 1 million VHH designs screened against 145 targets; 65 of 145 targets (45%) showed significantly elevated on-design hit rates (Fisher's exact test; also stated in a search summary of the preprint); after filtering for high-confidence designs, hit rates for specific epitopes up to 38%. Vendor-documentation source; definitions need checking in the paper. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.26.678877.full.pdf); [Tamarind](https://docs.tamarind.bio/tools/mber)
- **BindCraft (Nature 2025; minibinders).** 10-100% experimental success across targets (cell-surface receptors, allergens, de novo proteins, CRISPR-Cas9); functional tests included reducing IgE binding to a birch allergen in patient-derived samples, modulating Cas9 editing and lowering cytotoxicity of a foodborne bacterial enterotoxin. — [RosettaCommons](https://rosettacommons.org/2025/09/25/one-shot-design-of-functional-protein-binders-with-bindcraft/)
- **IgDesign (Absci; redesign for 8 antigens with known complexes).** HCDR3 design beat a SAbDab HCDR3 baseline on 8/8 antigens; HCDR1-3 design on 7/8. Example per-antigen rates: 30.6% (22/72) HCDR3 design for one antigen versus 6.3% HCDR1-3 and 1.6% baseline; IL36R 27.9% (19/68) HCDR3 and 15.6% HCDR1-3. Baseline binding rate was 0% for IL36R, TSLP, IL17A, FXI, ACVR2B and TNFRSF9. Tamarind reports 1437 antibodies screened and 278 binders, about 19% overall (my calculation). — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.08.570889.full.pdf); [Tamarind](https://docs.tamarind.bio/tools/igdesign)
- **tFold (Nature Comms 2025).** Nanomolar affinities by phage display + SPR for antibodies against Flu A HA, PD-1, PD-L1, SARS-CoV-2 RBD (numbers of designs not retrieved). — [Twist listing](https://twistbioscience.com/resources/publication/de-novo-design-epitope-specific-antibodies-structure-driven-computational)
- **Other 2025-2026 preprints with little retrievable detail:** "De Novo Computational Design of VHH Nanobodies Against LGR5" ([bioRxiv 10.64898/2025.12.02.691880](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.02.691880.full.pdf)); an AIRI iterative 4-1BB agonist nanobody design study whose screening round did not yield a high-affinity in-vitro binder per a search summary ([AIRI](https://airi.net/ru/articles/an-iterative-strategy-to-design-4-1bb-agonist-nanobodies-de-novo-with-generative-ai-models)); TorchCraft: measured binding for minibinders and VHHs across four targets in each format with AF3 weights, no post hoc redesign ([arXiv 2609.19770](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19770)).

**Industry / company-reported**

- **Chai-2 technical report (30 June 2025).** At most 20 designs per target against 52 targets; 16% hit rate; at least one hit for 26 of 52 targets (50%) in a single round; targets had no pre-existing antibody/nanobody binder in the PDB; whole workflow under two weeks; picomolar KD reported for IL7Ra, PD-L1, PDGFR-beta and Insulin R, low-nM for TNFa; at least 3-fold hit-rate improvement over the next-best method on every target compared. The report frames earlier methods as typically at or below 0.1% hit rate. Miniproteins: 68% wet-lab success (as listed in a search summary of the report). ### Inferences
- Headline hit rates cluster in three bands: about 4-22% (academic open tools, few targets: Germinal), about 10-40% (industry per-design, tens of designs: Chai-2 16%, Galux 10.5%, JAM-2 18-39%, Protenix-v2 up to 48%, GeoFlow-V3 about 15-20%), and about 1% in an independent third-party panel (Aalphabio). The spread is partly real model differences and partly definitions (KD cut-off, specificity counter-screen, epitope-level vs target-level success, assay format).
- Only a small minority of reports pair per-design hit rates with (a) CDR-level information, (b) epitope-accuracy structures (RFantibody 2 cryo-EM structures; Chai-2 5; Origin-1 cryo-EM) and (c) function (JAM GPCR agonists; Chai-2 CCR8/CXCR4 agonists; Origin-1 IL36RA antagonist). Monomeric binding is the norm; function is the exception.
- Affinity after one round is still usually nM to hundreds of nM for open methods; sub-nM claims come from companies. RFantibody needed OrthoRep maturation to reach single-digit nM.

### Gaps
- Per-design hit rates and the exact CDR-H3-only versus all-CDR design scope were not retrievable for Chai-2, JAM-2, Latent-X2, Protenix-v2; the RFantibody Nature paper's overall hit rate was not found. Exact per-target AlphaProteo percentages were not retrievable.
- Per-target Germinal affinities come from a secondary summary, not the paper's table.
- Epitope accuracy (fraction of designs hitting the intended epitope by competition or mutagenesis) was not found for any system apart from the cryo-EM cases above.
- Whether Chai-2 or Nature/Science peer-reviewed versions exist as of October 2026 was not established (only preprints found). Germinal's Nature Biotechnology listing rests on one low-confidence metadata site.
- Official AIntibody Challenge 3 (de novo loop design) leaderboard and the paper's own numbers were not accessed.

## Q3. Critical commentary: leakage, metrics, epitope control, affinity, hard targets, developability, reproducibility, evaluation standards

### Takeaway
The main credible criticisms are: in-silico confidence scores (ipTM/ipAE and even ipSAE) are only partial predictors of binding and their performance is target-dependent; headline results are mostly company-reported preprints with non-comparable "hit" definitions; first-round affinities of open methods are weak (hundreds of nM), and the best de novo EGFR binder in a community competition (82 nM) was about 70x weaker than the best optimised antibody (1.21 nM); GPCR success depends on high-quality, state-specific structures; developability and immunogenicity evidence is thin and ex vivo. Leakage is addressed by selecting targets with no PDB binders or <30% identity, but review articles still warn that benchmark overlap inflates predictive accuracy.

### Cited Findings

- **Peer-review and self-published critiques.** A Zenodo commentary (v2.0, 3 Dec 2025, author not identified in the page summary) gives a figure-by-figure critique of the RFdiffusion antibody paper: methodological gaps, over-interpretation of in-silico outputs, limited experimental depth, selective reporting and insufficient benchmarking; it also criticises RMSD-based evaluation, ambiguity in superposition strategy and absence of rotamer-level validation. Not peer reviewed; no authors' response found. — [Zenodo 17797005](https://zenodo.org/records/17797005)
- **Chai-2 critique (blog).** A Medium review argues GPCR agonism relied on near-perfect structural information and the authors' own framing requires an active-state structure with a known agonist. Secondary commentary only. A CASRAI analysis notes the Chai-2 claims had not cleared peer review. — [Medium](https://medium.com/@enginyapici/i-tried-to-poke-holes-in-chai-2s-antibody-design-paper-here-s-what-i-found-7e51f5581c7d); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/wp/?p=3221)
- **Benchmark leakage / novelty controls.** Chai-2 restricts to targets with no antibody/nanobody binder in the PDB; BoltzGen to targets with no protein >30% identity in a bound context in the PDB; Origin-1 to "zero-prior" epitopes; Protenix-v2 to "novelty-filtered" targets. A 2026 review in Antibodies warns that overlap between training datasets and commonly used benchmarks can artificially inflate reported accuracy; a Springer oncology review observes that models sometimes generate variations of known antibodies rather than novel ones. — [Chai-2 report](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.05.663018.full.pdf); [BoltzGen](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494.full.pdf); [Origin-1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.14.699389.full.pdf); [Protenix-v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.10.717613v1.full.pdf); [Antibodies 2026 review](https://doi.org/10.3390/antib15050081); [Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-025-00764-4)
- **Press-level scepticism about de novo claims.** Coverage says Absci and Generate:Biomedicines faced scrutiny with allegations that their "de novo" pipelines produced redesigned versions of existing antibodies, with some critics questioning whether de novo antibody design was possible (attribution inferred from a search summary; underlying article not opened). Baker is quoted that therapeutics need far more than binding, and the RFantibody authors say solubility, affinity and immunogenicity still need optimisation. — [GEN "Scratch that"](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/scratch-that-de-novo-antibody-design-enters-the-ai-drug-discovery-toolbox/); [Drug Target Review](https://www.drugtargetreview.com/news/190363/scientists-use-ai-to-create-antibodies-entirely-from-scratch/)
- **In-silico metrics do not reliably predict binding.** NeurIPS 2024 benchmark: log-likelihood correlates with measured affinity; no evidence pAE/ipTM suitable for ranking antibody sequence designs. Dunbrack's ipSAE preprint: ipTM is lowered by disordered/accessory regions, so trimming constructs raises ipTM with no change in the interaction; ipSAE counts only residue pairs with good PAE. Aalphabio analysis found a high false-positive rate for ipSAE in an AlphaSeq dataset of inverse-folded VHH variants. AIntibody authors conclude in-silico confidence does not reliably track experimental performance. — [NeurIPS 2024](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/102849); [ipSAE bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.10.637595.full.pdf); [Overath et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.14.670059.full.pdf); [Aalphabio "Overconfident"](https://aalphabio.substack.com/p/overconfident-what-structure-prediction); [Smithery ipSAE skill page](https://smithery.ai/skills/adaptyvbio/ipsae); [The Signal](https://buttondown.com/fjgoff/archive/the-signal-biohealth-week-of-aug-31-2026-edition-3/)
- **Epitope control.** Protenix-v2 shows 4% versus 48% hit rate for two epitopes on the same protein; JAM-2 gave VHH-Fc binders to only 30-70% of 20 user-specified epitopes on half of 10 targets; Origin-1 succeeded on 4 of 10 targets with under 100 designs each; a 2026 workflow paper says attempts to target a PD-1 region repeatedly gave low-confidence predictions. — [Protenix-v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.10.717613v1.full.pdf); [JAM-2](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/Nabla-JAM2); [Origin-1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.14.699389.full.pdf); [Frontiers/Patsnap-type review search result](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/ai-antibody-design-technology-landscape-2026/)

Hosted third-party versions exist for mBER and Germinal (Tamarind). Closed/partner access: Chai-2 (commercial; limited academic), Latent-X/X2 (web/API for selected partners), Nabla JAM, AlphaProteo, Origin-1, IsoDDE, GeoFlow-V3. — [IPD](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/11/rfantibody-in-nature); [Rosetta Commons](https://rosettacommons.org/2025/12/22/rfdiffusion3-is-now-available-in-foundry/); [MIT Jameel Clinic](https://jclinic.mit.edu/research-project/boltzgen); [EPFL](https://news.epfl.ch/news/an-open-source-ai-platform-to-democratize-protei-2/); [Chai product](https://www.chaidiscovery.com/product); [Tamarind mBER](https://docs.tamarind.bio/tools/mber)
- **Evaluation standards still immature.** Adaptyv's community paper lists lack of standardised benchmarks, standardised experimental targets and robust computational metrics; AIntibody is a one-off blinded test; Ginkgo's competition shows small heterogeneous datasets limit models and calls for standardised data; MultiAbRank (ICML 2026) criticises reconstruction-based and confidence-proxy evaluation. — [Adaptyv paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.17.648362.full.pdf); [Ginkgo/ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f1c61639-22e2-4737-b651-f18888f6b507); [MultiAbRank](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/71767)

### Inferences
- The most defensible concerns for a PhD framing: (1) cross-paper non-comparability of "hit rate"; (2) lack of independent replication of closed models; (3) first-round affinity and functional activity (agonism/blocking) rather than binding; (4) metric reliability, which opens a research niche in better ranking/validation; (5) epitope-level controllability evidence (competition, mutagenesis, structures) is sparse.
- Anchoring a project in a disordered/flexible epitope, or in joint epitope-specificity + developability optimisation, plays to an unmet area: I found no wet-lab de novo generative antibody success against intrinsically disordered targets in the 2025-2026 sources, and Sormanni-style fragment/CDR-grafting precedent exists (verify this absence before asserting it).

### Gaps
- I did not find an independent third-party reproduction of Chai-2, JAM-2, Latent-X2 or Protenix-v2.
- The leakage question for RFantibody's and Germinal's targets (e.g., whether PD-L1, IL-3, BHRF1, HA, TcdB have PDB antibody complexes) was not checked; several of those (PD-L1, influenza HA, TcdB) are known to have many PDB structures from my background knowledge, unverified here.
- The Zenodo critique's author and any reply were not identified; I did not find Derek Lowe, Nature News or Science News commentary on these papers (one search each, no hit).
- No dedicated source found on multipass membrane proteins other than GPCRs (e.g., claudins; the JAM preprint of January 2025 on Claudin-4/CXCR7 is mentioned by a third-party profile only).
- Align to Innovate benchmarks: no results found for antibody design.

## Q4. Top 10 papers to read, in suggested order (beginner entering generative antibody design)

### Takeaway
A sensible path goes from the foundational tools (RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN), to hallucination (BindCraft), to the first peer-reviewed de novo antibody paper (RFantibody), to the industry zero-shot claims (Chai-2, Nabla JAM), to the leading open antibody/binder tools (Germinal, BoltzGen), and finally to evaluation papers that temper the hype (Overath meta-analysis; AIntibody). DOIs for items 1-2 are recalled from memory and must be verified.

### Cited Findings
Reading order with reasons:

1. **Watson et al., "De novo design of protein structure and function with RFdiffusion", Nature 2023.** Foundation for RFantibody/RFD3. DOI recalled, not verified in this session: 10.1038/s41586-023-06415-8. ## Q5. What do 2024-2026 reviews and perspectives list as open questions?

### Takeaway
The recurring open questions are: scarce and biased training data; designing against truly novel ("zero-prior") epitopes; novelty versus memorisation of known antibodies; experimental validation as the new rate-limiting step; multi-property optimisation (affinity versus stability, PK, immunogenicity); benchmark leakage and the lack of standard evaluation. I could only access search-tool summaries of the reviews, so they should be read directly before quoting.

### Cited Findings
- **Frontiers in Pharmacology 2026 (fphar.2026.1773629), Springer "Revolutionizing oncology: the role of AI as an antibody design, and optimization tools" (Biomarker Research 2025), MDPI Antibodies 2026 "The Evolution of Artificial Intelligence in Antibody Design: From Structure-Based Engineering to Generative Models", PMC12063268 "Accelerating antibody discovery and optimization with high-throughput experimentation and machine learning", Drug Discovery News "Antibody discovery moves toward de novo design", Patsnap 2026 landscape (non-peer-reviewed).** — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2026.1773629/full); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-025-00764-4); [MDPI Antibodies](https://doi.org/10.3390/antib15050081); [PMC12063268](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12063268/); [Drug Discovery News](https://www.drugdiscoverynews.com/antibody-discovery-moves-toward-de-novo-design-17541); [Patsnap](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/ai-antibody-design-technology-landscape-2026/)
- Open questions as summarised by the search tool from these pieces: data scarcity (about 9000 SAbDab structures versus about 230,000 PDB structures), overfitting risk; designs against epitopes absent from training data remain weak, and a 2026 bioRxiv frames "zero-prior" epitopes as a fundamental challenge (Origin-1); models sometimes generate variations of known antibodies; experimental validation, not generation, is now the bottleneck (2026 industry landscape report); improving one property such as affinity may harm stability, pharmacokinetics or immunogenicity; training/benchmark overlap can inflate accuracy (Antibodies 2026 review). — [Nature Comms workflow paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67361-9); [Origin-1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.14.699389v2.full); [Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-025-00764-4); [Patsnap](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/ai-antibody-design-technology-landscape-2026/); [Antibodies review](https://doi.org/10.3390/antib15050081)
- **Authors' own limitation statements.** BoltzGen: high affinity alone does not establish therapeutic efficacy, additional validation ongoing. Latent-X2: animal and clinical studies ahead. RFantibody authors: solubility, affinity and immunogenicity still need optimisation. Ginkgo: datasets too small and heterogeneous for robust assay-spanning developability prediction; call for larger standardised datasets. Adaptyv: need for standardised benchmarks, experimental targets and metrics. — [BoltzGen](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494.full.pdf); [Latent-X2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20263v1); [Drug Target Review](https://www.drugtargetreview.com/news/190363/scientists-use-ai-to-create-antibodies-entirely-from-scratch/); [Ginkgo/ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f1c61639-22e2-4737-b651-f18888f6b507); [Adaptyv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.17.648362.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- Candidate PhD-level positioning angles that follow from the open questions: (a) epitope-precise and disordered/flexible-target design with explicit validation of epitope identity; (b) joint optimisation of binding, developability and immunogenicity within the generator, not as a late filter; (c) reliable metrics/rankers for antibody-antigen binding that survive prospective, blinded tests; (d) data-efficient or physics-informed methods when SAbDab-scale data are limiting; (e) function (agonism, neutralisation) rather than binding as the readout.
- Because most industry systems are closed, a project built on open tools (RFantibody, BoltzGen, Germinal, BindCraft/mBER) is more reproducible and publishable.
