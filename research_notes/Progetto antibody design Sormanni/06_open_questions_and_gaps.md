## 1. What do 2024-2026 reviews, perspectives and commentaries name as the unsolved problems in AI antibody design (grouped into themes)?

### Takeaway
Across 2024-2026 reviews and trade perspectives the same five problems recur: (i) experimental validation and functional readouts are the rate-limiting step, (ii) data scarcity, bias and absence of negative or standardised data, (iii) flexible CDR-H3 / Ab-Ag docking, (iv) multi-objective trade-offs (affinity vs developability vs immunogenicity), and (v) de novo epitope-directed design that is still early and hard to compare across studies. I found no Nature Reviews Drug Discovery, Cell Systems or Nature Biotechnology perspective on this topic. The best-evidenced "named gaps" come from the 2026 mAbs paper on the Ginkgo competition and the 2026 Nature Biotechnology AIntibody benchmark (see Section 3).

### Cited Findings

**Theme A - Validation bottleneck, dataset bias, flexible regions, interpretability, translation**
- A 2026 review lists "limited experimental validation, dataset bias, prediction of highly flexible regions, model interpretability, and clinical translation" as current challenges. The search summary did not say which of the two 2026 reviews returned is the source. Candidates: [MDPI Antibodies 15(5):81, "The Evolution of AI in Antibody Design: From Structure-Based Engineering to Generative Models"](https://doi.org/10.3390/antib15050081) and [Frontiers in Immunology 2026, 1802038](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1802038/full). Attribution unverified.
- A trade landscape piece says generative models can produce millions of candidates but wet-lab testing (SPR, ELISA, cell assays) is now the rate-limiting step — [Patsnap landscape 2026](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/ai-antibody-design-technology-landscape-2026/). This is trade/marketing content, not peer-reviewed.
- Another trade/conference summary says de novo epitope-directed binder design is "still early" with published success rates in the single-digit to low-double-digit percent range, and quotes an industry speaker arguing the bottleneck has shifted from molecule engineering to biology (choosing which interaction to target) — [Drug Discovery News](https://www.drugdiscoverynews.com/antibody-discovery-moves-toward-de-novo-design-17541), [Drug Discovery News, AI foundation models](https://www.drugdiscoverynews.com/antibody-design-with-ai-foundation-models-generative-approaches-and-the-biologics-pipeline-17359). The summary did not separate which of these two pages carried which statement.
- A monoclonal antibody review (2026) calls for large, diverse, unbiased training data and notes that databases for rare diseases and emerging pathogens are thin (same search; the specific link was not identified in the summary, see the two 2026 reviews above).

**Theme B - CDR-H3, flexibility and conformational ensembles**
- Accurate prediction of CDR-H3 conformations "remains considerably more challenging than modelling conserved framework regions" (same 2026 review set as Theme A; attribution unverified).
- ITsFlexible (Nature Machine Intelligence, 2025) classifies CDR loops as rigid or flexible with a GNN, generalises to MD, and was checked on three CDRH3 loops by cryo-EM. It gives a label, not an ensemble — [Oxford ORA record (probable match, unverified)](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:4191d86c-8daa-4607-bd77-76be55de01ee).
- Ibex is a pan-immunoglobulin predictor with apo vs holo comparisons. Its authors note that predicting inherent CDR-H3 flexibility from sequence is a distinct, open challenge — [arXiv 2507.09054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09054).
- "Cadense" (NeurIPS 2026 listing) is a generative ensemble model matching MD distributions, "seconds instead of days". Only the conference listing was seen, so the benchmark is unverified — [NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/174011).
- I found no shared community benchmark that scores CDR-H3 ensemble prediction across methods (summary of the search that returned the three items above).

**Theme C - Ab-Ag structure prediction and confidence**
- AlphaFold3 high-accuracy docking success on antibodies and nanobodies: 8.9% and 13.4% in the NeurIPS 2024 workshop version versus 11.0% and 11.4% (single seed) in the preprint, i.e. about 60% failure at single seed — [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.21.614257.full.pdf), [NeurIPS 2024 workshop page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/102512), [PMC version](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11838198/).
- A separate preprint on 70 nanobody epitope cases reports that AF3 gives highly accurate complexes above 60% of the time only when sampling 1000 seeds, and that epitope identification stays hard because of CDR3 diversity — [bioRxiv 2024.10.07.616899](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.07.616899.full.pdf).
- A CD47 case study reports an AF3 "reverse docking" failure, with the antibody docked in the opposite orientation, sometimes at high ipTM. Whether it generalises is unknown — [arXiv 2511.14676](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14676).
- An independent CASP16 assessment found AF3 only slightly ahead of AF2-based methods on complexes, with the gap vanishing under massive AF2 sampling, and that antibody/nanobody-antigen prediction "seemed to remain" a hard case — [AlphaFold3 at CASP16](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.10.648174.full.pdf).
- One CASP16 analysis reports that the kozakovvajda group exceeded 60% success on antibody-antigen targets without relying on AFM or AF3 as the primary framework. The summary did not say which linked page this came from — [candidate: PMC12154902](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12154902).
- I found no dedicated peer-reviewed benchmark calibrating ipTM against Ab-Ag docking accuracy (search summary; see Gaps).

**Theme D - Multi-objective optimisation, developability, immunogenicity**
- Optimising one property can hurt stability, immunogenicity or expression, so manufacturability constraints are increasingly built into design objectives (same 2026 mAb review set as Theme A).
- A review in Antibody Therapeutics (2025) is titled "Beyond affinity: AI-supported developability assessment and multi-objective optimization in antibody development". I saw only the title and a snippet — [Antibody Therapeutics 9(4):508](https://academic.oup.com/abt/article/9/4/508/8748294).
- Developability data are clustered by sequence identity, of unequal cluster sizes, and skewed toward rare extremes, which undermines standard cross-validation. Descriptors often disagree with each other, and the ideal datasets are siloed in industry — [3decision/Discngine blog (vendor)](https://3decision.discngine.com/blog/2024/11/19/antibody-developability-how-to-effectively-navigate-challenges-and-opportunities-in-predicting-antibody-properties), [Benchling blog (vendor)](https://www.benchling.com/blog/predicting-antibody-structure-and-developability-tools-opportunities-and-challenges). Attribution of each sub-claim to one of these pages is unverified.
- Immunogenicity: computational predictions have not replaced in vitro and animal testing, and the biological rules are poorly understood (same search set as above).
- Humanness only weakly tracks experimental immunogenicity. A multivariate Gaussian humanness model showed "only a weak negative correlation" with experimental immunogenicity — [Hu-mAb / large-scale humanization paper (PMC8760955)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8760955/). Hu-mAb itself reported a negative relationship between its score and the experimental immunogenicity of therapeutics, so the two papers disagree in emphasis. A 2024-2025 arXiv paper describes humanness as only roughly correlated with immunogenicity — [Generative Humanization (arXiv 2412.04737)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04737v2). A vendor argues humanness scores miss T-cell epitope risk in CDRs — [XtalPi (vendor, self-interested)](https://solution.xtalpi.com/en/antibody/xuperHuman).
- Structure-based immunogenicity (PITHA) reached 83% accuracy leave-one-out on 29 therapeutics with crystal structures but 65% on 23 with modelled structures — reported in the humanness/immunogenicity literature set above (the specific source link was not isolated by the summary; candidate [Frontiers in Immunology 2024 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1399438/pdf)).

**Theme E - Data scarcity, negative data, low-N, generalisation**
- Antibody affinity benchmarks: AbBiBench (184,500+ measurements, 14 antibodies, 9 antigens, 15 models) finds structure-conditioned inverse-folding models best — [arXiv 2506.04235](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.04235). A follow-up reports vanilla ESM-IF has a mean Spearman of only 0.264 between likelihood and affinity across 11 assays, raised to 0.410 by preference optimisation, and 0.115 to 0.294 on four unseen complexes — [SimBinder-IF arXiv 2512.17815](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17815v1).
- Negative data: how negatives are built can decide whether a model generalises or exploits shortcuts. Evidence is strongest in TCR work (performance drops to random on realistic negatives) — [TCR negative-bias critique, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.06.535863.full.pdf), [TCR binding review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.16594). For antibodies, one preprint studies how negative-set choice affects Ab-Ag models and notes little prior research — [bioRxiv 2024.06.17.599333](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.17.599333.full.pdf). The AVIDa-hIL6 VHH dataset treats non-significant pairs as unlabeled rather than negative — [arXiv 2306.03329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03329).
- AB-Bind includes only 27 non-binders, and the authors note that non-binders act as outliers that harm model accuracy (search summary; link not isolated, see Gaps).

**Theme F - Formats (multispecifics), intracellular use, other**
- Multispecific design: Chai-3 is reported to improve notably on multispecific generation — [IntuitionLabs (secondary)](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/generative-antibody-design-chai-3-pfizer). A 2026 arXiv paper on "disentangling multispecific antibody function with graph neural networks" exists, but I saw only the title — [arXiv 2601.23212](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.23212).
- Intrabodies: a Science Advances paper (vol. 12 issue 1, 2 Jan 2026) used AF2 + ProteinMPNN framework redesign plus live-cell screening. 19 of 26 antibodies became functional scFv intrabodies, and 18 of those 19 had failed under the standard approach — [Nanowerk](https://www.nanowerk.com/news2/biotech/newsid=68685.php), [Lab Manager](https://www.labmanager.com/ai-pipeline-converts-conventional-antibodies-into-functional-intracellular-antibodies-35006), [AZoLifeSciences](https://www.azolifesciences.com/news/20260216/Artificial-Intelligence-Enables-Stable-Intrabody-Design-in-Cells.aspx).
- A Nature Communications paper (University of Essex-led, March 2026) reports AI inverse-folding-based redesign of scFv intrabodies, 600+ sequences, with targets including tau, alpha-synuclein, SOD1 and TDP-43. Binding checks covered only a subset (p53, alpha-synuclein, SOD1). I could confirm this only from secondary sources — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/university-of-essex-study-uses-ai-to-design-intrabodies-for-cc1705dd), [BioTechniques](https://www.biotechniques.com/computational-biology/ai-designed-intrabodies-open-up-new-avenues-for-studying-gene-regulation).
- Intracellular delivery and long-term in vivo stability remain bottlenecks for computationally designed nanobodies — [Frontiers research topic](https://www.frontiersin.org/research-topics/82392/integrating-computational-design-synthetic-biology-and-delivery-platforms-in-next-generation-nanobody-technologies).
- Biosecurity: the Science/Microsoft work on AI-redesigned toxins slipping past DNA-synthesis screening was reported in October 2025. The coverage concerns protein toxins, not antibodies specifically — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/?p=3160491), [WFAE/NPR](https://www.wfae.org/science-technology/2025-10-02/ai-designs-for-dangerous-dna-can-slip-past-biosecurity-measures-study-shows). I saw only headlines.

### Inferences
- The explicitly named gaps map cleanly onto Sormanni-group strengths in three places: developability/solubility (Theme D), nativeness vs immunogenicity (Theme D), and data standardisation (Themes A/E). They map poorly onto raw structure-prediction scale (Theme C), where industry has the compute and data.
- The weak, partly contradictory evidence that humanness predicts immunogenicity is itself an open scientific question. A proposal that uses nativeness as a design objective should state what it expects nativeness to buy (expression, polyreactivity, ex vivo T-cell responses), not assume immunogenicity benefits.
- Themes not found in any reviewed source with specific content: PTMs/glycosylation, BBB penetration, in vivo PK, and active-site-level interpretability. These may be white space or simply outside the sources my queries reached.

### Gaps
- No Nature Reviews Drug Discovery, Nature Biotechnology (opinion), Cell Systems, Trends in Biotechnology, Current Opinion in Structural Biology, Briefings in Bioinformatics, Science or NMI perspective on AI antibody design surfaced. Absence in my search is not proof they do not exist.
- No source found on PTMs/glycosylation, BBB shuttles, or in vivo PK for AI-designed antibodies.
- Biosecurity: only headlines seen, nothing antibody-specific.
- Review attribution above (which 2026 review said what) is unresolved because full texts were not retrievable.

## 2. Which companies and academic labs are working on which themes (crowded vs under-explored)?

### Takeaway
De novo, zero-shot epitope-targeted binder generation (Chai, Nabla, Latent, Baker/IPD, Arc/Hie, Xaira, Absci, Generate) and lab-in-the-loop affinity optimisation (Genentech/Prescient, Aureka, Absci, Cradle-like groups) are crowded and capital-rich. Evidence for activity on disordered/aggregation-prone antibody targets, intracellular targets, selectivity-aware epitope choice, and nativeness-aware design is thin or absent in what I could retrieve, with Sormanni's own lab as the main exception.

### Cited Findings

**Companies (mostly company-reported)**
- Chai Discovery: Chai-2 reports a 16% hit rate across 52 targets with up to 20 designs each and at least one binder for 26 of 52 targets (50%) in a single round — [Chai-2 technical report mirror](https://www.rivista.ai/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/technical_report.pdf), [Chai Discovery mAb post](https://chaidiscovery.com/news/chai-2-mab). Full-length IgG: 88 IgGs, 28 antigens, 86% with zero or one flagged developability issue (thresholds from Jain et al.) — [Chai Discovery mAb post](https://chaidiscovery.com/news/chai-2-mab). Chai-3 is reported to double Chai-2's success rate. Pfizer licensed the platform in June 2026 (a Lilly deal was also reported for January 2026) — [The Pharma Letter](https://www.thepharmaletter.com/biotech-news/pfizer-signs-ai-drug-discovery-license-with-chai-discovery), [BioSpace press release](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/chai-discovery-announces-license-agreement-with-pfizer-to-accelerate-drug-discovery-with-ai), [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/generative-antibody-design-chai-3-pfizer). Funding reports conflict: one outlet reports a closed $400M Series C at $3.8B on 14 July 2026, while an earlier report said it was only in talks at about $3.4B — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/chai-discovery-pursues-400m-round-for-ai-antibody-design) (unconfirmed which is right).
- Nabla Bio: JAM-2 reportedly yields binders to 16 unseen targets (100%) with average success of 39% (VHH-Fc) and 18% (mAb), and GPCR binders against CXCR4 (11.7%) and CXCR7 (3.8%), some with agonist activity. All numbers are company-reported and come via secondary coverage — [GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/test-time-scaling-improves-speed-and-success-of-ai-based-antibody-design/), [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-research/2025/generative-antibody-design-cracks-undruggable-targets/), [HyperAI](https://hyper.ai/fr/papers/Nabla-JAM2). I found no peer-reviewed JAM-2 paper.
- Latent Labs: Latent-X2 (16 Dec 2025) reported VHH and scFv binders for 9 of 18 targets (50%), testing 4-24 designs per target, claiming developability matching approved antibodies without optimisation, and a ten-donor ex vivo immunogenicity panel on TNFL9 VHH binders. This is a preprint and press release with no animal or clinical data — [arXiv 2512.20263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20263v1), [Business Wire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20251216040546/en/Latent-Labs-Announces-Latent-X2-AI-Generated-Antibodies-With-Drug-Like-Developability-and-Low-Ex-Vivo-Immunogenicity).
- Xaira: revealed X-Design and first pipeline assets (XA-1, XA-4, targets undisclosed) in October 2026 — [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/weve-been-taking-our-time-xaira-reveals-antibody-platform-and-first-taste-pipeline). A Xaira June 2026 post defines "progressable binders" as meeting criteria beyond binding (functional activity, immunogenicity, computational developability), per a newsletter summary — [BioByte 166](https://decodingbio.substack.com/p/biobyte-166-a-deep-dive-into-progressable). The same newsletter says Xaira requires early binders at 100 nM or tighter (secondary, unverified against Xaira's post).
- Clinical-stage: Generate Biomedicines' GB-0895 (severe asthma) is reported in Phase 3, and Absci has ABS-101 and ABS-201 in Phase 1/2a — [Sovereign Magazine, Aug 2026](https://www.sovereignmagazine.com/article/aintibody-ai-antibody-design-nature-biotech-212438) (landscape-style trade source; status not independently verified).
- Absci's Origin-1 is a de novo platform. Absci's own comparison reports problems running public RFantibody code unmodified (competitor claim, see Section 4) — [Medium summary of Origin-1](https://medium.com/@enginyapici/origin-1-abscis-de-novo-antibody-design-platform-198d9defb3fa).
- Antiverse (Cardiff): $9.3M Series A in March 2026 for GPCR and ion-channel antibodies — [Yespress](https://yespress.io/antiverse). Landscape blogs also list BigHat, Isomorphic (not antibody-specific), Earendil, and others, with unverified valuations — [NewMarketPitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-drug-discovery-protein-design-startup).
- Genentech/Prescient Design: "Lab-in-the-loop" preprint with 1,800+ variants tested over four rounds on four targets (EGFR, IL-6, HER2, OSM), 3-100x better binders and about 100 pM best binders. Company-linked and not peer reviewed at the time of the summary — [bioRxiv 2025.02.19.639050](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.19.639050.full.pdf). Earlier walk-jump sampling work reported a 92% expression rate for generated antibodies — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/?p=1167682).
- Ginkgo: guided discrete diffusion for developable antibodies, derivative-free (Soft Value-based Decoding in Diffusion), with developability regressors trained on 246 clinical antibodies — [arXiv 2507.02670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02670). Ginkgo Datapoints also ran the AbDev competition (Section 3).
- Aureka Biotechnologies: won AIntibody Challenge 1 with AuraIDE (Section 3). Press release from a participant — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nature-biotechnology--aureka-wins-the-global-blinded-ai-antibody-benchmark-ai-design-surpasses-the-best-experimental-result-302860936.html).
- A-Alpha Bio (AlphaSeq assay company) publishes benchmarking commentary and argues for a controlled head-to-head test — [A-Alpha Bio Substack](https://aalphabio.substack.com/p/benchmarking-de-novo-design-models).
- Ayusuf's critique states that Nabla ran 70+ de novo antibody campaigns to build training data (blog; unverified) — [Substack](https://ayusuf.substack.com/p/developability).

**Academic labs**
- Baker/IPD: RFdiffusion-based de novo VHH and scFv design (Bennett et al., Nature 2025/2026). Initial designs had tens to hundreds of nM affinity, with maturation needed for single-digit nM. VHH binders were made to four epitopes, with cryo-EM confirming poses for influenza HA and C. difficile toxin B — [PubMed 41193805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41193805/), [IPD PDF](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Bennett-Nature-2025-RFantibody-s41586-025-09721-5.pdf), [GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/ai-designed-antibodies-achieve-atomic-precision-to-enhance-drug-discovery/). A third-party Zenodo commentary alleges methodological gaps and selective reporting (a single author's critique, not peer review) — [Zenodo 17797005](https://zenodo.org/records/17797005).
- Baker lab on disordered targets: Liu et al., Nature 644:809-817 (2025), fine-tuned RFdiffusion to design binders from target sequence alone. Binders to disordered peptides (amylin, C-peptide, VP48, BRCA1_ARATH) at about 3-100 nM, and to disordered regions in beta-strand conformations (G3BP1, IL-2RG, prion protein) at about 10-100 nM, with in-cell imaging. These are miniprotein binders, not antibodies — [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.16.603789.full.pdf), [summary of the Nature paper](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/245582).
- Arc Institute / Stanford (Hie group affiliation not confirmed in the summary): Germinal pairs AF-Multimer with IgLM. Across four targets (PD-L1, IL-3, IL-20, BHRF1) it reports a 4-22% experimental success rate with 43-101 designs per target and best binders of about 140-560 nM, with mutagenesis supporting the intended epitope — [bioRxiv 2025.09.19.677421](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421.full.pdf), [Arc Institute](https://arcinstitute.org/news/building-germinal-for-ai-designed-antibody-molecules).
- mBER (controllable de novo antibody design with million-scale experimental screening) and PXDesign (modular binder design) exist as 2025 preprints. I saw only titles — [mBER](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.26.678877.full.pdf), [PXDesign](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.15.670450.full.pdf). AgForce (antigen-conditioned generative antibody design, May 2026) — [arXiv 2605.21610](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21610) (title only).
- BoltzGen: all-atom diffusion with BoltzIF and Boltz-2 for validation, demonstrated mostly on non-antibody binders — [Tamarind summary](https://www.tamarind.bio/blog/boltzgen-validated-de-novo-binder-design-for-diverse-targets).
- Sormanni group (Cambridge): AbNatiV2 and p-AbNatiV2 (bioRxiv, late Oct 2025). Each AbNatiV2 model is trained on at least 20 million sequences, new camelid repertoires were sequenced, and p-AbNatiV2 is a cross-attention model fine-tuned on 3.7 million paired sequences — [bioRxiv 2025.10.31.685806](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf), [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/a4b75bb4-15fe-41b4-b729-02827d348695). The original AbNatiV is a VQ-VAE for VH, Vκ, Vλ and VHH nativeness — [Nature Machine Intelligence](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s42256-023-00778-3). SurfDiff (Dec 2025, Jedlitzke, Wissett, Nguyen, Ghedin, Sormanni) scores residue-level surface uniqueness/similarity to prioritise selective or broadly reactive epitopes without needing binder structures, reporting agreement with known selectivity — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.23.696170.full.pdf), [PMC12776457](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12776457/). Earlier: rational design of antibodies to epitopes in intrinsically disordered proteins (PNAS 2015), reported as targeting over 95% of residue positions in three proteins — [BLOPIG](https://www.blopig.com/blog/?p=2842). CamSol is licensed to many pharma and biotech companies — [Cambridge group listing](https://www.ch.cam.ac.uk/person/ps589), [CamSol page](https://www-vendruscolo.ch.cam.ac.uk/camsolmethod.html). The group page also lists a 2023 Nature Communications paper on automated optimisation of solubility and conformational stability of antibodies and proteins.
- A preprints.org preprint (Sept 2026, authorship unconfirmed, not peer reviewed) reports an AI solubility pipeline (AF3 hotspot analysis, RFdiffusion backbones, ProteinMPNN) giving a nanobody with CamSol 3.007 versus 0.518 for wild type, from 780 designs across 10 nanobody-antigen pairs — [preprints.org 202609.1947](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202609.1947). It shows that others are starting to attack solubility redesign with generic pipelines.
- NanoDesigner (2025) addresses nanobody CDR co-design and notes models are often constrained by reliance on experimental Ab-Ag structures — [KAUST citation listing](https://borg.kaust.edu.sa/citations/references/journal-article/rioszertuche2025/nanodesigner-resolving-complex-cdr).
- Other targeted work: Science Advances 2026 intrabody pipeline (Science Tokyo, Kyushu, Colorado State) and Essex-led Nature Communications intrabody work (Section 1, Theme F). Conformation-specific GPCR nanobodies exist as experimental (not AI-designed) work — [PMC8285524](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8285524/).

### Inferences
Rough crowdedness map based only on the evidence above (not a full census):

| Theme | Activity seen | Crowdedness |
|---|---|---|
| Zero-shot epitope-targeted de novo binders on folded antigens | Chai, Nabla, Latent, Xaira, Absci, Baker, Arc, Boltz, Ginkgo | Very high |
| Lab-in-the-loop affinity maturation | Genentech/Prescient, Aureka, Absci, others | High |
| GPCR / membrane targets | Nabla, Antiverse, Chai | High and rising |
| Inference-time developability steering (charge, hydropathy, regressor guidance) | Ginkgo, ZetaDial (title only), energy-based mAbs paper, 2023 property-guided diffusion | Moderate, growing |
| Nativeness (language-model-derived) as design objective | Germinal uses IgLM bias; Sormanni (AbNatiV2) | Low-moderate |
| Antibody-format de novo design for disordered/aggregation-prone targets | Baker (miniproteins), Sormanni 2015; no antibody generative paper found | Low (could not confirm absence) |
| Intrabody design and cryptic epitopes | Few 2026 papers (Sci. Adv., Nat. Commun.), no generative de novo work found | Low |
| Ab-Ag co-folding confidence calibration; CDR-H3 ensembles | Many groups touch it; no community benchmark | Moderate; large-lab scooping risk |
| Standardised developability/negative data | Ginkgo Datapoints, A-Alpha Bio, consortium efforts | Moderate; data-owner advantage |

- Large teams optimise hit rate on folded targets and then sell on developability claims. They publish small developability panels, not the mechanism. This leaves room for a group that can test why designs are or are not developable.

Whether they target disordered or intracellular targets is unverified.
- The Sormanni lab's current unpublished directions and funding were not verified (group page not read in full).
- Company numbers are company-reported and come via press and secondary coverage, with the exception of arXiv/bioRxiv listings.

## 3. What do community competitions and benchmarks reveal?

### Takeaway
The two most informative prospective benchmarks (Ginkgo AbDev 2025 and the AIntibody challenge) both show that models fail at predicting properties and rankings out-of-distribution, even when generation or maturation succeeds. AbDev reports Spearman correlations of only 0.31-0.39 for four of five developability assays, and AIntibody's ranking task was worse than random-clone picking. I found no antibody-specific Adaptyv competition results and no retrievable CASP17, CAPRI or Align Bio antibody results.

### Cited Findings

**Ginkgo Datapoints AbDev / GDPa1 (Sept 8 to Nov 18, 2025; mAbs 2026)**
- 113 teams from 25 countries (38 companies, 39 universities). Training set 246 clinical antibodies, blinded test set 80 antibodies, five properties: hydrophobicity, thermostability, self-association, titer, polyreactivity — [mAbs article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19420862.2026.2634216), [PubMed 41724677](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41724677/), [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f1c61639-22e2-4737-b651-f18888f6b507).
- Best test Spearman rho: hydrophobicity 0.708, thermostability 0.392, polyreactivity 0.356, self-association 0.337, titer 0.310. Cross-validation on the public set consistently exceeded held-out performance (overfitting, weak OOD generalisation). Winning submissions differed by assay. Authors cite datasets that are too small and heterogeneous and call for larger standardised data with harmonised protocols — same sources. A correction notice exists, content unseen — [PubMed 41819821](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41819821/).
- Prizes up to $60k with per-property awards — [competition announcement](https://huggingface.co/blog/ginkgo-datapoints/2025-abdev-competition) (figure from search summary). Named winning teams and their methods were not retrieved.
- The Antibody Developability Consortium (Apheris) is a federated data effort. I saw only the listing — [Apheris](https://www.apheris.com/networks/antibody-developability-consortium).

**AIntibody blinded benchmark (Specifica/IQVIA; Nature Biotechnology, Aug 2026)**
- 511 designs from 29 organisations, 14 days and up to 10 sequences per task. Tasks: (1) in silico affinity maturation, (2) affinity ranking, (3) out-of-library HCDR3 design. Publication date reported as 19 Aug or 26/27 Aug 2026 by different sources (unresolved) — [Nature Biotechnology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03238-6), [Sovereign Magazine](https://www.sovereignmagazine.com/article/aintibody-ai-antibody-design-nature-biotech-212438), [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/immunology/product-news/ai-antibody-challenge-highlights-computational-protein-design-416703), [AIntibody.org](https://www.aintibody.org/).
- Challenge 1: Aureka's AuraIDE won with a 94.7 pM binder by KinExA, about 2,000-fold over the parent, and held three of the top five places (press-release claim) — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nature-biotechnology--aureka-wins-the-global-blinded-ai-antibody-benchmark-ai-design-surpasses-the-best-experimental-result-302860936.html). A computer-designed antibody matched or slightly beat the best result from about three months of bench work — [Sovereign Magazine](https://www.sovereignmagazine.com/article/aintibody-ai-antibody-design-nature-biotech-212438), whose own subheading says field-wide AI has not yet beaten the bench.
- Challenge 2 (ranking): over 96% of sequences remained binders but only about 9.8-13.8% improved on the most abundant clone in their HCDR3 cluster, while historical random clone picking improved on the cluster control 39% of the time. The top-ranked design failed to elute from the HIC column but still won because the composite score let high affinity offset a biophysical failure; the authors say future benchmarks must fix this — summary of [Nature Biotechnology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03238-6) and associated coverage; see also [Artificial Science](https://artificialscience.org/2026/08/ai-beats-the-lab-at-antibody-design/) (I could not read this page's content).
- Challenge 3: of 168 out-of-library submissions, 69.6% bound, 33.9% reached subnanomolar affinity and 14.3% sub-100 pM. Commentary says skill did not transfer across tasks, with maturation-strong models (with one exception) worse than random at ranking and erratic at loop design — [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/immunology/product-news/ai-antibody-challenge-highlights-computational-protein-design-416703) / [Sovereign Magazine](https://www.sovereignmagazine.com/article/aintibody-ai-antibody-design-nature-biotech-212438) (the summary did not isolate which page carried which statement).

**Adaptyv Bio competitions**
- EGFR competition (2024): Cradle won with a 1.21 nM binder, 8.2-fold stronger than cetuximab. A nanobody took second place, and two designs beat the cetuximab control — [Cradle blog](https://www.cradle.bio/blog/adaptyv-protein-design-competition), [bioRxiv community paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.17.648362.full.pdf). Round 2 tested 400 designs with 378 expressed (95%) — [Adaptyv post on X](https://x.com/adaptyvbio/status/1865544715539484783). A search summary said Round 1 hit rate was almost 3% and Round 2 13%, which I could not confirm against the paper. Other coverage: [Adaptyv blog](https://www.adaptyvbio.com/blog/po104/), [Protein Optimization 102](https://adaptyvbio.substack.com/p/protein-optimization-102-lessons), [BenchBB](https://adaptyvbio.substack.com/p/introducing-benchbb-and-the-community).
- RBX1 competition: the title of an Endpoints article mentions a 2.8% hit rate on RBX1 — [Endpoints](https://endpoints.news/adaptyv-bio-reveals-latest-results-in-protein-design-competition/). A different summary says 9 of 245 submitted designs bound (about 3.7%), which does not match 2.8%. Unresolved conflict. In the same context, a Claude-driven campaign reported 28 of 90 binders and a 3.9 nM best binder versus the winner's 45 nM, from Anthropic's first-pass figures. A reviewer noted this shows binding, not function — [OncoDaily](https://oncodaily.com/technology/anthropic570775). Another summary cites 1,320 designs, 16 targets, 354 binders (26.8%), whereas the OncoDaily headline gives 15 targets (target count conflict).
- These competitions concern general binder design, mostly non-antibody. I found no 2026 Adaptyv antibody- or nanobody-specific competition results.

**CASP / CAPRI / others**
- CASP16: Ab-Ag results were described above (Section 1, Theme C). No CASP17 (2026) or CAPRI antibody results were found.
- NbBench (nanobody representation benchmark, eight tasks across nine datasets, including developability) was mentioned in one summary without a retrievable link, so unverified.

### Inferences
- Prospective, blinded, single-platform benchmarks are the only ones that expose generalisation failures. The hit rates in vendor reports are not comparable to them.
- The AIntibody result that ranking/affinity prediction among close HCDR3 variants fails (below random baseline) is the clearest evidence that low-N affinity prediction is unsolved, while directed maturation works because it exploits library structure and experimental feedback.
- AbDev shows polyreactivity and self-association, the two properties most connected to CamSol-type physics and charge/hydrophobicity patches, are among the worst predicted. This is a concrete, measurable target.
- Composite scores that allow one property to offset a failure (AIntibody Challenge 2) are a design flaw for any multi-objective benchmark a PhD project might build. Hard biophysical gates and Pareto reporting are the alternative.

### Gaps
- Could not read the AIntibody Nature Biotechnology paper or the mAbs AbDev paper in full. Winning AbDev methods and team names are unknown.
- No CASP17, CAPRI, Align to Innovate, or antibody-specific Adaptyv 2026 results found.
- RBX1 hit-rate discrepancy and target-count discrepancy above remain unresolved.

## 4. What are the main critiques of recent AI-antibody claims?

### Takeaway
The critiques that recur are: (1) hit-rate numbers are not comparable (different targets, epitopes, assays, hit definitions), (2) target and epitope selection bias, (3) developability claims based on small panels and lenient thresholds, (4) affinity often micromolar to low-nanomolar and measured without functional assays, (5) benchmark fairness and leakage disputes, and (6) lack of independent replication. Most critiques are from competitors, bloggers or single authors, not peer-reviewed papers, and should be weighed accordingly.

### Cited Findings
- Hit rates differ with framework, target and epitope, proposal strategy, scoring funnel, assay format and even the definition of "hit"; ELISA positivity vs SPR-confirmed binding make rates from 0.1% to 39% hard to compare — [A-Alpha Bio benchmarking post](https://aalphabio.substack.com/p/benchmarking-de-novo-design-models), [Agent-Guided nanobody preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.13.717816v1.full) (attribution of the 0.1-39% statement to this preprint is by position in the result list and not certain).
- Epitopes differ in difficulty, and Chai-2 and JAM-2 were tested on different epitopes, so common-basis comparison is nearly impossible — [A-Alpha Bio, "Building antibodies blindfolded"](https://aalphabio.substack.com/p/building-antibodies-blindfolded-the). The same sources call for a controlled head-to-head benchmark on identical targets, and for measuring both affinity and specificity.
- One analysis across 10 challenging cancer targets found pass rates varying about 60-fold (0.3% to 19.8%) between targets — [Antibody Research Hub](https://inventcures.github.io/antibody-research/) (aggregator; attribution by position).
- Micromolar affinities raise therapeutic-relevance questions, since therapeutic antibodies typically need below 10 nM — [Comparative analysis PDF](https://inventcures.github.io/files/v7_denovo_antibody_design_comparison.pdf) (aggregator).
- Absci reports that running public RFantibody code unmodified yielded broken loop chains and that the release includes native PDB loop coordinates that could leak structure into a "de novo" benchmark. It also alleges some reported hits gave SPR signals not confirmed by BLI and a weaker hit confirmed without visible data. Absci is a competitor, so this is an allegation, not settled — [Medium summary of Origin-1](https://medium.com/@enginyapici/origin-1-abscis-de-novo-antibody-design-platform-198d9defb3fa).
- Zenodo commentary on the Bennett et al. Nature paper alleges over-interpretation of in silico outputs, limited experimental depth and selective reporting. One author, not peer reviewed — [Zenodo 17797005](https://zenodo.org/records/17797005).
- Chai-2 developability claims: the updated preprint tests 88 full-length IgGs over 28 antigens, and passing allows one flagged issue (lenient). A Substack critic says developability results are "mostly speculative" because it is unclear whether any developability data were used in training or scoring, and says Chai-2 claims are not peer reviewed. I found no independent replication — [Chai Discovery mAb post](https://chaidiscovery.com/news/chai-2-mab), [Ayusuf Substack](https://ayusuf.substack.com/p/developability), [CASRAI listing](https://casrai.org/wp/tag/ai-biology-foundation-models/).
- Chai-2 statistics: per-target hit counting. The original preprint tested up to 20 designs per target, and results reflect a single round without iterative lab optimisation — [Chai-2 technical report mirror](https://www.rivista.ai/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/technical_report.pdf).
- Latent-X2: no animal or clinical data, a ten-donor ex vivo panel only, unreviewed — [arXiv 2512.20263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20263v1).
- Nabla JAM-2: binding in cells does not equal therapeutic effect, and no peer-reviewed paper was found — [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-research/2025/generative-antibody-design-cracks-undruggable-targets/).
- Binding is not function. A reviewer of the Claude/Adaptyv RBX1 run says it demonstrated binding, not biological function — [OncoDaily](https://oncodaily.com/technology/anthropic570775). Xaira's own "progressable binder" framing concedes that SPR/BLI affinity can be ambiguous (e.g. non-specific plate sticking) — [BioByte 166](https://decodingbio.substack.com/p/biobyte-166-a-deep-dive-into-progressable).
- Benchmark design flaws: in AIntibody, the composite score let a design that failed HIC win; the authors say future benchmarks must address this (Section 3).
- Co-folding confidence: ipTM above 0.8 is used as a "confident" heuristic in one study, but "reverse docking" can score high — [arXiv 2511.14676](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14676). No calibrated threshold was found.

### Inferences
- The strongest, most checkable critiques are the benchmark-design ones from the AIntibody authors themselves and the AbDev generalisation gap, because they come from the benchmark organisers and are peer-reviewed. Competitor allegations (Absci on RFantibody) should be treated as hypotheses.
- A recurring, unexploited opportunity is a head-to-head, same-epitope, same-assay comparison of open tools (RFantibody, Germinal, BoltzGen, mBER, others) with hard biophysical gates and specificity readouts. Company papers cannot be that neutral comparator.
- Developability for de novo designs has a credibility gap: the claim is "drug-like without optimisation", the evidence is small panels with lenient thresholds. A mechanistic study (which sequence or structural features of de novo CDRs cause polyreactivity or aggregation, and does nativeness/CamSol predict them) would be publishable and is within the group's skill set.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed critique paper (Nature/Science correspondence, Cell Systems) specifically on hit-rate reporting, benchmark leakage or selection bias in AI antibody papers was found. Most are blogs, vendors or preprints.
- Whether Chai-2 or JAM-2 have been independently replicated: not found.

## 5. Candidate directions for a Sormanni-group PhD: evidence for and against, novelty, feasibility, risk

### Takeaway
On the evidence available, the best-balanced thesis would be built around (a)+(b)+(f): nativeness- and solubility-aware generative design applied to hard targets that large teams are not targeting (disordered, aggregation-prone, intracellular), with a built-in controlled evaluation/benchmark component to de-risk. Pure affinity-maturation (d) and generic co-folding work (e) are the most exposed to large-team competition. These are inferences from sparse and mostly secondary evidence, not established facts.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Nativeness/developability-guided generative design**
- Precedents: Ginkgo's guided discrete diffusion with derivative-free guidance, developability regressors trained on 246 clinical antibodies — [arXiv 2507.02670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02670). Energy-based generative models for mAbs (mAbs 2025) generate candidates on Pareto fronts across solubility, humanness and affinity — [mAbs](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19420862.2025.2584935). Black-box guidance in a shared latent space co-designs CDRs for affinity and developability — [arXiv 2508.11424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11424). Property-guided DDPM using hydropathy as a solubility proxy — [bioRxiv 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.11.22.568230.full.pdf). ZetaDial (Sept 2026) dials net charge of protein binders at inference time for developability (title only seen) — [arXiv 2609.05451](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05451). My search found no single paper combining diffusion guidance, explicit multi-objective Pareto optimisation and humanness (an absence observation from the search summary, with no single URL; it is not proof of absence).
- Against/limits: Humanness weakly correlated with immunogenicity ([PMC8760955](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8760955/), [arXiv 2412.04737](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04737v2)). Industry already claims drug-like developability without optimisation (Latent-X2, Chai-2, Section 2). Developability predictors trained on small sets generalise poorly (AbDev, Section 3), so a reward built on them can be gamed.
- Tool basis: AbNatiV2 models trained on at least 20M sequences each, and p-AbNatiV2 on 3.7M pairs — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf). Germinal biases designs toward natural-like sequences using IgLM, which shows nativeness-like priors are already being used in de novo pipelines — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421.full.pdf).

**(b) Epitope-targeted design for disordered / aggregation-prone / conformational-state targets**
- Baker's sequence-only binder design to IDPs/IDRs, miniproteins at 3-100 nM, with in-cell engagement ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.16.603789.full.pdf), [summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/245582)). Sormanni 2015 designed antibodies against IDP epitopes ([BLOPIG](https://www.blopig.com/blog/?p=2842)). SurfDiff supports epitope selection by selectivity or breadth ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.23.696170.full.pdf)).
- Experimental conformation-selective nanobodies (GPCR states) exist but without generative design ([PMC8285524](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8285524/)). No AI-designed state-selective GPCR nanobody paper was found in my search.
- Against/limits: AF3-class co-folding is weak even on folded Ab-Ag pairs (8.9-13.4% single-seed high-accuracy docking, [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.21.614257.full.pdf)), and Germinal/RFantibody validation is on folded epitopes.

**(c) Intracellular / low-complexity / cryptic-epitope targets**
- Science Advances 2026 intrabody conversion (19/26), Essex Nat. Commun. 2026 intrabodies against tau, alpha-synuclein, SOD1, TDP-43 (Section 1, Theme F). Delivery and stability remain open ([Frontiers research topic](https://www.frontiersin.org/research-topics/82392/integrating-computational-design-synthetic-biology-and-delivery-platforms-in-next-generation-nanobody-technologies)). The Baker IDP binders showed in-cell engagement ([summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/245582)). No generative cryptic-epitope antibody work found.

**(d) Low-data affinity maturation with PLMs and active learning**
- Genentech lab-in-the-loop ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.19.639050.full.pdf)); AIntibody Challenge 1 win by Aureka and Challenge 2 failure of ranking ([Section 3 sources](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03238-6)); AbBiBench and SimBinder-IF show weak zero-shot likelihood-affinity correlations ([arXiv 2506.04235](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.04235), [arXiv 2512.17815](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17815v1)). Active-learning and wet-lab throughput are concentrated in industry.

**(e) Ensemble-aware CDR-H3 / co-folding confidence calibration**
- AF3 docking and reverse-docking failures ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.21.614257.full.pdf), [arXiv 2511.14676](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14676)); sampling dependence (60%+ at 1000 seeds, [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.07.616899.full.pdf)); CASP16 results ([AF3 at CASP16](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.10.648174.full.pdf)); ITsFlexible, Ibex, Cadense as partial solutions (Section 1, Theme B). No ipTM calibration benchmark for Ab-Ag found.

**(f) Benchmarks and datasets with wet-lab validation**
- AbDev (Section 3) explicitly calls for larger standardised datasets. Critics call for head-to-head benchmarks with affinity and specificity ([A-Alpha Bio](https://aalphabio.substack.com/p/building-antibodies-blindfolded-the)). AIntibody authors say composite scoring needs fixing. Negative data are scarce (Section 1, Theme E). Industrial and federated efforts exist ([Apheris](https://www.apheris.com/networks/antibody-developability-consortium)).

**(g) Other directions with literature support**
- Self-association/polyreactivity prediction: worst-predicted AbDev properties (rho 0.337 and 0.356), see Section 3.
- Epitope selectivity and variant breadth as a design constraint: SurfDiff and the call to measure specificity in benchmarks.
- Multispecific/format-aware design: Chai-3 emphasis and the multispecific GNN paper (title only).
- Ex vivo immunogenicity testing of de novo designs: Latent-X2's ten-donor panel was called "the first immunogenicity assessment of an AI-generated antibody" by its authors ([arXiv 2512.20263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20263v1)).

### Inferences

Qualitative scoring is my judgement from the evidence above. "Crowding" is within the retrieved sources only.

**Pitches, related work and risks**

**(a) Nativeness- and developability-guided generative design**
- Pitch: use AbNatiV2/p-AbNatiV2 and CamSol as differentiable or derivative-free guidance in open diffusion/flow/LM design pipelines, reporting Pareto fronts rather than composite scores, and test experimentally whether guidance actually improves expression, polyreactivity, self-association and ex vivo T-cell responses compared with unguided designs, using the same epitopes and assays. The novel contribution is the controlled, wet-lab-validated ablation of what nativeness buys, which no company report provides.
- Related work: Ginkgo guided diffusion (arXiv 2507.02670), energy-based Pareto mAbs paper, 2023 property-guided DDPM, black-box latent guidance (arXiv 2508.11424), ZetaDial (2609.05451), Latent-X2/Chai-2 developability claims, Germinal's IgLM bias.
- Risks: guidance with weak oracles can be reward-hacked (AbDev); the effect of nativeness on immunogenicity is contested; generators may already produce drug-like sequences, so the delta may be small; guidance code for AbNatiV2 differentiability must be verified (I could not confirm differentiability from sources, only that AbNatiV is a VQ-VAE).

**(b) Epitope-targeted design for disordered, aggregation-prone and conformation-specific targets**
- Pitch: extend Sormanni's IDP-epitope approach with modern generative models. Sample ensembles for the target (not a single structure), choose epitopes by ensemble accessibility and selectivity (SurfDiff), generate nanobody/antibody CDRs conditioned on those ensembles, and filter with CamSol and AbNatiV2 for solubility and nativeness. Validate against aggregation-prone targets (for example alpha-synuclein or amyloid species, as examples to choose from) with oligomer-versus-monomer or polymorph selectivity assays. The gap is that antibody-format generative design for disordered or state-specific epitopes was not found, while Baker's miniproteins show that binders to IDRs are achievable.
- Related work: Liu et al. Nature 2025 (bioRxiv 2024.07.16.603789), Sormanni 2015 PNAS, SurfDiff, conformation-specific GPCR nanobodies (PMC8285524), Essex intrabodies (tau, alpha-synuclein, SOD1, TDP-43).
- Risks: no reliable structural ground truth; co-folding confidence is uninformative on disordered targets; epitope validation (mapping) is hard; assay development for aggregates is slow; Baker could extend the IDP work to VHH scaffolds; low hit rate may dominate the PhD.

**(c) Intracellular and cryptic-epitope nanobody/antibody design**
- Pitch: design intrabody-compatible nanobodies/scFvs for intracellular, aggregation-prone or low-complexity proteins, using solubility- and stability-aware generation (CamSol and framework redesign) and live-cell readouts, with cryptic or transiently exposed epitopes defined from ensembles. The unmet need is that existing AI intrabody papers redesign frameworks of existing binders rather than generating new binders; the 2026 Science Advances work converted 19 of 26 antibodies but did not generate new paratopes.
- Related work: Science Advances 2026 pipeline, Essex Nature Communications 2026, Baker IDP binder in-cell engagement.
- Risks: intracellular function needs cell assays and target biology; delivery is not solved (but not needed if the aim is intracellular expression); cryptic epitopes are hard to define computationally; mostly mid-sized evidence base.

**(d) Generalisable low-data affinity maturation with PLMs and active learning**
- Pitch: build a PLM plus physics-informed prior that predicts affinity changes within HCDR3 clusters or from tens of measurements, evaluated prospectively. The measured gap is the AIntibody ranking failure and AbBiBench's weak zero-shot correlations.
- Related work: Genentech lab-in-the-loop, AIntibody Challenge 1 and 2, AbBiBench, SimBinder-IF.
- Risks: heavily crowded by well-resourced teams (Genentech, Aureka, Absci, others); wet-lab throughput; prospective validation would need partners; a negative result is likely and less publishable.

**(e) Ensemble-aware CDR-H3 and Ab-Ag co-folding confidence calibration**
- Pitch: build a calibration and uncertainty layer for open co-folding models (AF3-class, Boltz, Chai-1) on antibody-antigen targets, using sampling statistics, ensemble disagreement and ITsFlexible-like flexibility labels, and test whether it improves in silico filtering of generated designs on external experimental data (for example AIntibody, Germinal, RFantibody data).
- Related work: AF3 docking papers, CD47 reverse docking, CASP16, ITsFlexible, Ibex, Cadense.
- Risks: model internals and compute dominated by large labs; exact experimental ground truth for ensembles is scarce; I found no evidence that calibration improves experimental hit rates; high scooping risk from Genentech, Isomorphic and open-source co-folding teams.

**(f) Benchmark and dataset creation with wet-lab validation**
- Pitch: run a neutral, prospective, same-epitope, same-assay comparison of open design tools, with hard biophysical gates (HIC, polyreactivity, self-association, expression), specificity panels, and public negative data, designed to avoid the AIntibody composite-score flaw. Release as a living benchmark (analogous to AbDev but for generated designs).
- Related work: AbDev, AIntibody, Adaptyv BenchBB and competitions, A-Alpha Bio proposals, Apheris consortium.
- Risks: assay cost and partner dependence; data-labeller risk; Ginkgo, Adaptyv and A-Alpha Bio hold assay platforms; benchmark papers age quickly; community adoption is uncertain.

**(g) Other directions**
- (g1) Physics-informed self-association/polyreactivity prediction. Pitch: AbDev shows self-association (rho 0.337) and polyreactivity (0.356) are poorly predicted; combine CamSol-style intrinsic solubility, charge/patch features and language-model embeddings, train on harmonised public data, and evaluate on a prospective blinded set. Risk: public datasets are small (246 training antibodies), so a ceiling may be data-limited, and winners differed per assay.
- (g2) Selectivity- and breadth-constrained design. Pitch: design binders whose epitopes are selected for paralog selectivity or cross-variant breadth using SurfDiff, then test against off-target panels, filling the specificity gap that critics say benchmarks omit. Risk: needs off-target panels and is partly dependent on target choice.
- (g3) Ex vivo immunogenicity and nativeness. Pitch: test whether AbNatiV2-based nativeness predicts ex vivo T-cell/DC responses for de novo designs, building on Latent-X2's first panel. Risk: donor panel logistics, small samples, and the established weak correlation between humanness and immunogenicity.

**Overall positioning (inference)**
- Lowest competitor exposure and strongest group fit: (b), (c), (g2), (g1).
- Best de-risking components: (a) and (f) provide publishable intermediate outputs even if (b) hit rates are low.
- Most exposed: (d) and (e).
- A coherent thesis could be framed as "Developability- and nativeness-aware generative design of antibodies for hard targets, with an open validated benchmark", where years 1-2 deliver (a)+(g1) and benchmark scaffolding, years 2-4 deliver (b) or (c) wet-lab validation.
