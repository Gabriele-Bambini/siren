## 1. What are the minimum concepts to master (architecture, V(D)J/SHM/repertoires, numbering and CDRs, binding, discovery platforms, humanisation, developability, landscape)?

### Takeaway
Antibody design is the engineering of a Y-shaped, modular protein whose hypervariable CDR loops (above all CDR-H3, generated by V(D)J junction diversity) determine binding; the practical vocabulary splits into six blocks: architecture and formats, how nature generates and matures diversity, numbering/CDR definitions, binding physics, how therapeutics are discovered and humanised, and developability. I could source only some of this in-session (Janeway chapter, numbering review, repertoire-scale statistics, developability benchmarks, tool papers); the rest is standard background that the report writer should treat as textbook knowledge to be checked against Janeway chapters 3-4 and the reviews in Key Question 2.

### Cited Findings
- Janeway's Immunobiology (5th edition, 2001) is on NCBI Bookshelf and its antibody-structure chapter describes the molecule as Y-shaped with three roughly equal parts joined by a flexible hinge: two arms carrying the variable regions that bind antigen and a stem of constant region; antibodies are the secreted form of the B-cell receptor, differing only at the heavy-chain C-terminus; structure was determined by X-ray crystallography — [NCBI Bookshelf NBK27144, via search summary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK27144/)
- Papain cleaves IgG into two Fab fragments and one Fc fragment (MIT HST.176 course page, per search summary) — [MIT OCW HST.176 "antibodies" PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-176-cellular-and-molecular-immunology-fall-2005/cb1b962b6c8b5811a4e9e3545bc0f463_antibodies.pdf)
- The many antibody databases use different numbering conventions and CDR definitions, and variable CDRH3 length makes comparing sequences and identifying antigen-binding residues harder; the review compares existing numbering schemes and CDR definitions and summarises strategies to number residues and identify CDRs (Dondelinger et al., Front Immunol 2018; 9:2278) — [Frontiers (DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2018.02278)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.02278/full), [PMC6198058](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6198058/)
- ANARCI supports these numbering schemes: IMGT (all antigen receptors), Chothia (H/L only), Kabat (H/L only), Martin/Enhanced Chothia (H/L only), AHo (all antigen receptors), Wolfguy (H/L only); species supported: human, mouse, rat, rabbit, pig, rhesus (H, kappa; lambda not listed for rhesus) — numbering of other species (README example: llama VHH) may work but the species label may be wrong — [ANARCI GitHub README](https://github.com/oxpig/ANARCI)
- Repertoire scale and individuality: Briney et al. (Nature 566:393-397, 2019) sequenced ~3 billion heavy-chain sequences from 10 people; repertoires are largely individual-specific (pairs of people shared on average ~0.95% of clonotypes; only ~0.022% of clonotypes shared by all ten, still above chance); the authors extrapolate a baseline human repertoire of up to ~10^18 distinct antibodies (an extrapolation from blood B cells of 10 donors, which the authors caveat) — [Scripps press release](https://www.scripps.edu/news-events/news/20190123-burton-antibodies/), [PMC6411386](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6411386), [Nature DOI 10.1038/s41586-019-0879-y (as shown in IDEAS/RePEc URL)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v566y2019i7744d10.1038_s41586-019-0879-y.html)
- Canonical CDR clusters: RosettaAntibodyDesign grafts CDR structures from the canonical clusters of North et al. 2011, then does sequence design using each cluster's amino-acid profiles and flexible-backbone sampling; it uses the North/Dunbrack CDR definition and requires an AHo-numbered input antibody — [Adolf-Bryfogle et al., PLoS Comput Biol 14(4):e1006112, 2018 (DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006112), via search summary](https://doaj.org/article/25c7583215634d658b727758fbfe0bdf); [RosettaCommons RAbD manual](https://docs.rosettacommons.org/docs/latest/application_documentation/antibody/RosettaAntibodyDesign); [AntibodyDesignMover docs](https://docs.rosettacommons.org/docs/latest/scripting_documentation/RosettaScripts/Movers/movers_pages/antibodies/AntibodyDesignMover)
- Developability benchmark: Jain et al. (PNAS 114(5):944-949, 2017) built isotype-matched IgG1s from the sequences of 137 antibodies in advanced clinical stages (48 approved), expressed them in mammalian cells and ran a dozen biophysical assays, to define empirical "drug-like" limits (framed as a Lipinski-style rule for antibodies) — [Adimab summary](https://adimab.com/?p=7540), [PMC5293111](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5293111), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1616408114
- Therapeutic Antibody Profiler (Raybould et al., PNAS, 14 Feb 2019): five computational developability guidelines based on total CDR length, extent and strength of surface hydrophobic patches, positive charge and negative charge in the CDRs, and asymmetry of net heavy/light surface charge; thresholds derived from homology models of post-Phase-I clinical-stage antibodies versus a snapshot of the human antibody gene repertoire; input is variable-domain sequence — [PMC6410772](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6410772/), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1810576116
- CamSol (Sormanni, Aprile, Vendruscolo, J Mol Biol 427:478-490, 2015): computational design of mutants with enhanced solubility; screens tens of thousands of mutations; tested on a single-domain antibody against amyloid-beta; has a fast sequence-based predictor plus a structure-corrected variant; academic users directed to the web server — [CamSol method page](https://www-vendruscolo.ch.cam.ac.uk/camsolmethod.html), DOI 10.1016/j.jmb.2014.09.026 (DOI per search summary)
- Humanisation and humanness: BioPhi's Sapiens (deep model trained on OAS; benchmark of 177 antibodies, results comparable to human experts) and OASis (humanness scored by splitting a sequence into overlapping 9-mer peptides and checking their frequency in OAS; separates human from non-human sequences and correlates with clinical immunogenicity); a review notes OAS non-human sequences are almost all murine, which may limit generalisation — [BioPhi bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.08.08.455394.full.pdf), [BioPhi GitHub (MIT licence; DOI 10.1080/19420862.2021.2020203)](https://github.com/Merck/BioPhi), [DOAJ record, mAbs 2022](https://doaj.org/article/1cfab3de8e254294ae2cd13c6a106121)
- AbNatiV (Ramon et al., Nat Mach Intell 6(1):74-91, 2024; DOI 10.1038/s42256-023-00778-3): VQ-VAE trained on native repertoires; separate models for human VH, VK, VL and camelid VHH; scores humanness and VHH-nativeness with a residue-level profile; in wet-lab tests on two nanobodies its humanised variants kept binding and stability at least as good as wild type, unlike conventional methods tested; an AbNatiV2 preprint (bioRxiv, Oct 2025) adds paired VH-VL nativeness and pairing likelihood — [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.28.538712.full.pdf), [Heidelberg library record with DOI](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1883486513), [AbNatiV2 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf), [PyPI (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 per summary)](https://pypi.org/project/abnativ)
- Sormanni's 2018 review frames computational design as "third generation" antibody discovery (after in vivo = first, in vitro display = second) and introduces "Modular", a method for designing antibodies in a modular way — [Chem Soc Rev 47:9137-9157, DOI 10.1039/C8CS00523K](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2018/cs/c8cs00523k)
- De novo design milestone: Bennett et al., Nature 649(8095):183-193 (Jan 2026; online Nov 2025), DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09721-5: fine-tuned RFdiffusion generates VHHs, scFvs and full antibodies, screened by yeast display; initial designs had tens to hundreds of nM Kd, OrthoRep affinity maturation reached single-digit nM; cryo-EM structures of HA and TcdB VHH complexes (PDB 9NFU, 9NH7) confirmed binding poses and CDR conformations — [PubMed 41193805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41193805/), [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103.full.pdf), [RCSB 9NFU](https://rcsb.org/structure/9nfu)
- Therapeutic landscape (what can be sourced): the annual "Antibodies to Watch" article series started in 2010 (mAbs / The Antibody Society); since 2021 Silvia Crescioli co-authors; the 2025 edition covered 18 candidates for which marketing applications might be submitted by end of 2025, including bi/multispecifics denecimig, sonelokimab, erfonrilimab, anbenitamab; a 2026 webinar/article edition exists (Crescioli and Reichert) — [Antibody Society 2025 page](https://www.antibodysociety.org/antibody-therapeutic/antibodies-to-watch-in-2025-is-now-online/), [BioTechniques webinar 2026](https://www.biotechniques.com/webinars/antibodies-to-watch-in-2026), [webinar series page](https://www.antibodysociety.org/learningcenter/antibodies-to-watch-webinar-series/)
- Thera-SAbDab tracks all antibody- and nanobody-related therapeutics recognised by the WHO and links them to SAbDab structures; 461 unique molecules as of 5 Aug 2019 (the live count is higher; synchronised weekly with SAbDab) — [Thera-SAbDab, NAR 48(D1):D383-D388](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6943036/)

### Inferences
**All items in this section are standard background knowledge (not sourced in this session) unless a link is given. Treat as a checklist of concepts; cross-check with Janeway ch. 3-4 [NBK27144] and with the references flagged [U-n] in Key Question 2 Gaps.**

**A. Architecture and formats**
- IgG (about 150 kDa) = 2 identical heavy chains (VH-CH1-hinge-CH2-CH3) + 2 identical light chains (VL-CL; kappa or lambda), joined by inter-chain disulfides; two identical antigen-binding sites; subclasses IgG1-4 differ in hinge, effector function and half-life. Other isotypes: IgM, IgA, IgE, IgD.
- Fab = VH-CH1 + VL-CL (antigen-binding, ~50 kDa); Fc = CH2-CH3 (effector functions via FcgammaR and complement; half-life via FcRn recycling); Fv = VH+VL only (the part almost all computational work models); scFv = VH and VL joined by a flexible linker (~25 kDa; common in display and CAR-T).
- Nanobody/VHH = single variable domain (~15 kDa) of camelid heavy-chain-only antibodies (HCAbs, discovered 1993 [U-3]); no VL, so a single domain carries three CDRs, frequently with a longer CDR-H3 that can reach into clefts; hydrophilic residues at the former VH/VL interface (FR2 hallmark residues) give solubility. Shark VNAR is a distinct single-domain scaffold. Key review: Muyldermans [U-10]; [FEBS J 2021 guide to nanobody generation and design, DOI 10.1111/febs.15515 (per search summary)](https://x-mol.com/paper/1293273001769967616).
- Why Fv: binding is mostly decided by six CDR loops (H1-H3, L1-L3), so models typically take VH+VL sequences only.

**B. How nature makes and tunes antibodies**
- V(D)J recombination in developing B cells (RAG1/2): heavy chain = one V + one D + one J gene segment (IGHV/IGHD/IGHJ); light chain = V + J (IGKV/IGKJ or IGLV/IGLJ); junctional diversity (random exonuclease trimming, TdT-mediated N-nucleotide addition, P-nucleotides) creates the CDR3, which is why CDR-H3 is by far the most diverse and least germline-templated loop. CDR1 and CDR2 are encoded by the V gene (germline). Reference: [U-28]; Janeway chapter "The generation of lymphocyte antigen receptors" on NCBI Bookshelf (same book as NBK27144).
- Germline genes: curated in IMGT (https://www.imgt.org; not fetched in this session). The number of functional human IGHV genes is roughly 40-50 (approximate; verify in IMGT). Germline usage biases and allelic variation matter for "humanness" scoring.
- Somatic hypermutation (SHM, AID-driven, ~10^-3 per base per division, mainly in germinal centres) plus selection = affinity maturation. Mutations concentrate in CDRs, but framework mutations also occur and can matter for stability and affinity. Key review: Victora and Nussenzweig, Annu Rev Immunol 2022 [U-26]. Consequence for ML: natural sequences are "germline-biased" (most are near-germline), which distorts language models — the stated motivation of AbLang2 ([README](https://github.com/oxpig/AbLang2)).
- Repertoire = the set of B-cell receptors in an individual (Rep-seq/NGS). Sizes: see Briney finding above. Clonotype/clonal family = sequences sharing V/J genes and a similar CDR3 (common ancestor). Paired (VH+VL from one cell) vs unpaired data is a central data-quality issue: OAS holds billions of unpaired but few paired chains (see Key Question 4).

**C. Framework vs CDRs, numbering, CDR-H3**
- Framework regions (FR1-4) form the conserved beta-sandwich (immunoglobulin fold); CDRs are loops at the tip. Chothia canonical structures [U-5] (limited loop conformations for CDR-H1, H2, L1-L3 predicted from length and key residues); CDR-H3 has no small set of canonical conformations (very diverse in length, sequence, and conformation, with a "kinked" or "extended" base geometry), and is also the centre of the paratope — which is why structure prediction and design of CDR-H3 remain the hard part. Updated clustering: North et al. 2011 [U-9] (used by Rosetta RAbD and PyIgClassify).
- Numbering schemes (all assign position labels so that equivalent residues align across antibodies): Kabat (1991 sequence-variability-based; CDR-H1 insertion positions at 35A/B; CDR-H3 95-102) [U-30]; Chothia (structure-based numbering moving the H1 insertion position to 31-32; shorter CDR-H1 loop definition) [U-5, U-6]; IMGT (gap-based, uniform for V domains of Ig and TCR; "IMGT unique numbering", CDR1 27-38, CDR2 56-65, CDR3 105-117) [U-7]; Martin (enhanced Chothia, structure-aligned; used in abYsis/AbDb); AHo (Honegger and Plückthun; structure-based, 149 positions, equal-length alignment) [U-8]. CDR definitions (Kabat, Chothia, IMGT, Contact, AbM, North) differ by a few residues per loop, so "CDR" depends on the definition — always state numbering AND CDR definition. Beginner-friendly overview: [Dondelinger et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.02278/full) and [Benchling blog "Antibody numbering schemes and CDR definitions"](https://www.benchling.com/blog/antibody-numbering) (found in search results; content not read).
- Tools that number: ANARCI/ANARCII (OPIG), abnumber (wraps ANARCI), IMGT/DomainGapAlign, abYsis.

**D. Binding physics and "paratope/epitope"**
- Paratope = antibody residues contacting antigen (typically CDR residues plus a few framework residues); epitope = antigen residues contacted (linear vs conformational). Affinity = Kd = koff/kon (nM-pM typical for therapeutic antibodies; Kd of <1 nM is a common target; avidity = multivalent apparent affinity (two arms)). Measurement: SPR/BLI (kinetics), ITC (thermodynamics), ELISA/flow cytometry (relative), deep mutational scanning (DMS) with display + NGS.
- Specificity = binding the intended target/epitope only; polyreactivity/polyspecificity = non-specific stickiness to unrelated molecules (e.g., DNA, insulin, detergents, baculovirus particles), usually associated with hydrophobic and/or positively charged paratope patches and associated with fast clearance in vivo. Affinity maturation can raise polyreactivity ([U-21] Makowski et al. on co-optimising affinity and specificity; verify).
- Why ΔΔG prediction is hard: mutations at interface change energy by small amounts compared with model error; benchmarks SKEMPI 2.0 and AB-Bind (see Key Question 4).

**E. Discovering and making therapeutic antibodies**
- Hybridoma (Köhler and Milstein 1975) [U-1]: immunise mouse, fuse B cells with myeloma; murine antibodies need humanisation. Immunisation of animals (mouse, rat, rabbit, llama/alpaca for VHH, chicken) remains a mainstay.
- Transgenic mice with human Ig loci (e.g., Lonberg [U-11]) yield fully human antibodies without humanisation.
- In vitro display: phage display (McCafferty 1990 [U-2]; 2018 Nobel to Smith and Winter), yeast display (Boder and Wittrup 1997 [U-4]; allows FACS selection with quantitative sorting), also mammalian, ribosome, mRNA display; libraries from human donors (naive), synthetic/designed (e.g., CDR randomisation) or immune libraries. Review: Bradbury et al. 2011 [U-12].
- Single B-cell cloning from convalescent/vaccinated donors (e.g., COVID-19 antibodies; CoV-AbDab records these).
- Third generation = computational design (see Sormanni 2018 and Bennett 2026 above).
- Humanisation: CDR grafting (put murine CDRs on a human framework), back-mutations of key framework residues, resurfacing, and now ML (Sapiens/BioPhi, AbNatiV). Goal: reduce immunogenicity (anti-drug antibodies, ADA) while keeping affinity and stability. Humanisation does not eliminate immunogenicity [U-13].
- Formats and modalities beyond IgG: bispecifics, ADCs, Fc-engineered antibodies (half-life extension, effector tuning), nanobody-based drugs; many clinical candidates in the Antibodies to Watch series are bispecifics/ADCs (see cited finding above).

**F. Developability (the central "second objective" in ML antibody design)**
- Aggregation/solubility (hydrophobic patches, low net charge, CDR-H3 and light-chain contributions; CamSol is a solubility predictor), viscosity at high concentration (>100 mg/mL for subcutaneous injection) driven by self-association and charge asymmetry, conformational and colloidal stability (Tm, Tagg, aggregation onset), expression titre (CHO/HEK), polyreactivity (see above), self-association (AC-SINS, CIC assays), hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC) retention as a hydrophobicity proxy, immunogenicity (T-cell epitopes, humanness), PK/clearance (FcRn, charge, polyreactivity), and chemical liabilities: deamidation (Asn-Gly, Asn-Ser), isomerisation (Asp-Gly, Asp-Ser), oxidation (Met, Trp), N-glycosylation motifs (N-X-S/T) in CDRs, free cysteines, and unpaired disulfides; reference: Lu et al. 2019 [U-14].
- Empirical benchmarks: Jain 2017 (137 clinical-stage antibodies; cited above) and TAP (five guidelines; cited above). The ML-relevant limitation: these datasets are small (hundreds of molecules) and mostly IgG1, while assay noise is considerable; there is little public data on developability for novel designs.

**G. Clinical and commercial landscape (partial)**
- Hundreds of antibody therapeutics exist in WHO INN lists (Thera-SAbDab: 461 in 2019, cited above); approved antibodies number in the hundreds worldwide (exact current number NOT verified — see Gaps). Typical drug classes: oncology (PD-1/PD-L1, HER2, CD20), immunology (TNF, IL-17, IL-23, IL-4R alpha), neurology (anti-amyloid), infectious disease. The market is dominated by a small number of blockbuster antibodies (not verified here).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
**Glossary (background knowledge; verify nuances against Janeway and the reviews in Key Question 2).**

*Molecules and formats*
1. **Immunoglobulin (Ig)/antibody** — secreted B-cell-receptor protein that binds antigen; five isotypes (IgG, IgM, IgA, IgE, IgD).
2. **IgG** — the dominant therapeutic isotype (~150 kDa; subclasses IgG1-4), two heavy + two light chains.
3. **Heavy chain (H) / light chain (L; kappa or lambda)** — the two polypeptide types; the VH/VL pair forms the binding site.
4. **Fab / Fc / Fv** — antigen-binding fragment (VH-CH1 + VL-CL) / constant crystallisable fragment (CH2-CH3, effector function and FcRn binding) / variable fragment only (VH+VL).
5. **scFv** — single-chain Fv: VH and VL joined by a linker; common in display libraries and CAR-T.
6. **VHH / nanobody** — single variable domain from camelid heavy-chain-only antibodies (~15 kDa); VNAR is the shark equivalent.
7. **Bispecific / ADC** — antibody binding two targets / antibody-drug conjugate carrying a cytotoxic payload.
8. **Hinge, CH1-CH3, CL** — constant-domain components; hinge flexibility affects Fab orientation and subclass behaviour.

*Structure and numbering*
9. **CDR (complementarity-determining region)** — the six hypervariable loops (H1-H3, L1-L3) forming most of the paratope.
10. **Framework region (FR)** — the conserved beta-sandwich scaffold between CDRs.
11. **CDR-H3** — the most diverse, longest-range-variable loop, built from V-D-J junction; hardest to predict and design.
12. **Canonical structure/CDR cluster** — a limited set of loop conformations (Chothia; North/Dunbrack clusters) for non-H3 CDRs.
13. **Numbering scheme (Kabat, Chothia, IMGT, Martin, AHo)** — conventions that give equivalent residues the same position label; differ in insertions and CDR boundaries.
14. **CDR definition (Kabat/Chothia/IMGT/Contact/AbM/North)** — separate from numbering; decides which residues count as CDR.
15. **VH/VL orientation (packing angle)** — relative orientation of the two domains; influences the shape of the binding site.
16. **Paratope / epitope** — antibody-side / antigen-side contact residues; epitope can be linear or conformational; "epitope-specific design" targets a chosen epitope.

*Immunology and repertoires*
17. **V(D)J recombination** — somatic assembly of V, D, J gene segments (RAG-mediated) producing the receptor repertoire.
18. **Germline gene (IGHV/IGKV/IGLV)** — inherited V/D/J segments catalogued in IMGT; "germline identity" = similarity to the nearest gene.
19. **Junctional diversity / N-additions** — random nucleotide insertions/deletions at V-D-J junctions that create CDR3 diversity.
20. **Somatic hypermutation (SHM) and AID** — targeted point mutation of rearranged V genes by activation-induced deaminase.
21. **Affinity maturation / germinal centre** — iterative SHM + selection in germinal centres that raises affinity.
22. **Clonotype / clonal family / lineage** — sequences derived from the same recombination event, often grouped by V/J and CDR3.
23. **Repertoire / Rep-seq / NGS** — the set of receptors in an individual and its high-throughput sequencing.
24. **Paired vs unpaired sequencing** — whether VH and VL come from the same cell; most OAS data are unpaired.
25. **Naive vs memory/plasma B cell** — antigen-inexperienced (near-germline) vs antigen-selected (mutated).

*Binding and measurement*
26. **Affinity (Kd), kon, koff** — equilibrium dissociation constant = koff/kon; lower Kd = tighter binding.
27. **Avidity** — combined strength of multivalent binding (e.g., two Fab arms).
28. **Specificity** — binding only the intended target/epitope.
29. **Polyreactivity/polyspecificity (non-specific binding)** — stickiness to unrelated molecules; developability and PK risk.
30. **SPR / BLI / ELISA / DMS** — surface plasmon resonance and bio-layer interferometry (kinetics), enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (relative binding), deep mutational scanning (display + NGS fitness maps).
31. **ddG (binding free energy change)** — effect of a mutation on binding energy; SKEMPI/AB-Bind benchmarks.

*Discovery and engineering*
32. **Hybridoma** — fused B cell/myeloma line producing a monoclonal antibody (Köhler and Milstein).
33. **Phage / yeast display** — in vitro selection libraries displayed on phage or yeast surface; yeast allows quantitative FACS sorting.
34. **Transgenic (humanised) mice** — mice carrying human Ig loci that yield fully human antibodies.
35. **Humanisation** — converting non-human (usually murine) antibodies to human-like sequences by CDR grafting plus back-mutations or ML.
36. **Humanness / nativeness** — how human-like (OASis, Sapiens) / how natural-repertoire-like (AbNatiV) a sequence is; used as an immunogenicity/developability proxy.
37. **Affinity maturation in vitro** — mutagenesis plus selection to improve Kd (display, OrthoRep in Bennett 2026).
38. **De novo design vs optimisation** — creating binders from scratch vs improving existing ones.

*Developability and clinical*
39. **Developability** — properties determining whether a candidate can be manufactured, formulated, stored and dosed (expression, solubility, stability, viscosity, immunogenicity).
40. **Aggregation / solubility / viscosity** — colloidal behaviours at high concentration; CamSol predicts intrinsic solubility.
41. **Self-association / hydrophobic patches / charge asymmetry** — properties captured by TAP-style metrics and linked to viscosity and clearance.
42. **Chemical liabilities** — deamidation (NG, NS), isomerisation (DG), oxidation (M, W), N-glycosylation sites (N-X-S/T) in variable regions.
43. **Immunogenicity / ADA** — immune response against the drug (anti-drug antibodies).
44. **Fc effector function (ADCC/CDC) and FcRn** — immune-cell/complement killing and recycling-mediated half-life.
45. **Clinical-stage antibody set** — the Jain 2017 137-antibody benchmark; "post-Phase-I" sets underpin TAP thresholds.

*ML vocabulary specific to the field*
46. **Antibody language model (AbLang2, AntiBERTy, IgLM)** — protein language models trained on antibody sequences (OAS) to score or generate variants; "germline bias" is a known failure mode.
47. **Inverse folding (AntiFold) / diffusion (RFdiffusion)** — structure-to-sequence and generative backbone design methods used for antibody design.
48. **Structure prediction (IgFold, ABodyBuilder2) / docking** — predicting Fv structure from sequence / predicting antibody-antigen complex pose; CDR-H3 and docking remain the main error sources.
