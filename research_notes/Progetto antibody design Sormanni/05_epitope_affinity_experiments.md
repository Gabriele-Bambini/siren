## 1. Epitope-targeted / rational design methods, success rates, and antibodies against disordered and aggregation-prone proteins

### Takeaway
Sormanni's group pioneered sequence-based, epitope-by-epitope design of antibodies against intrinsically disordered targets (complementary-peptide grafting into CDRs, 2015) and used it to make conformation/oligomer-selective binders for Aβ42 and α-synuclein (2017, 2020, 2022). The 2025-26 generative methods (RFantibody, Chai-2, Germinal, BoltzGen, JAM, Origin-1) now do epitope-conditioned de novo design with reported per-target success of roughly 4-22% (Germinal) up to ~50% of targets (Chai-2) but all validated targets I could find were structured proteins with mostly modest initial affinities. I found no published generative de novo antibody work on a disordered target, and the field of conformational, oligomer-specific, PTM-specific or cryptic epitopes remains largely unsolved.

### Cited Findings

**Sormanni-lab rational design (disordered / aggregation-prone targets)**
- Founding paper: Sormanni, Aprile, Vendruscolo, "Rational design of antibodies targeting specific epitopes within intrinsically disordered proteins", PNAS 112(32):9902-9907 (2015), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1422401112. The method designs complementary peptides for a chosen disordered epitope and grafts them onto an antibody scaffold; it was tested on three disordered proteins/peptides linked to neurodegenerative and systemic misfolding disease — [PubMed/PMC summary via search](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538631), [PNAS PDF](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.1422401112)
- The three targets were α-synuclein, Aβ42 and IAPP, using a sequence-based design built into a single-domain antibody scaffold; the abstract itself states that raising antibodies against specific epitopes is laborious and difficult "in particular if these epitopes are not effective antigens" — [PubMed 26216991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26216991)
- A secondary blog describes the 2015 design as a "cascade" method that searches the PDB for beta-strand-forming peptides complementary to the epitope, grafted into single-domain scaffolds (blog; less authoritative than the paper) — [BLOPIG blog](https://www.blopig.com/blog/?p=2842)
- Later summaries state the designed binders inhibited α-synuclein aggregation and that the approach was applied to multiple antibodies scanning along Aβ — [PubMed Central record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538631)
- Aprile, Sormanni, Perni, Arosio, Linse, Knowles, Dobson, Vendruscolo, Sci. Adv. 3(6):e1700488 (2017): a panel of designed antibodies against different linear epitopes spanning Aβ42; screening found two antibodies acting on primary vs secondary nucleation (one delays onset, one stops aggregate multiplication); both reduced toxicity in an Aβ42-overexpressing C. elegans model — [PMC5479649](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5479649/), [PubMed 28691099](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/28691099)
- Aprile, Sormanni et al., PNAS 117:13509-13518 (2020), "Rational design of a conformation-specific antibody for the quantification of Aβ oligomers": two-step "antigen scanning" then "epitope mining"; DesAbs designed against residues 29-36 of Aβ42; lead DesAb-O binds oligomers more selectively than monomers or fibrils; detects/quantifies Aβ oligomers in vitro, in C. elegans and in mouse hippocampal tissue — [PMC7306997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7306997), [UCL Discovery PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10106995/1/13509.full.pdf)
- Kulenkampff, Emin, Staats et al., Chem. Sci. 13(46):13815-13828 (2022): panel of nine single-domain epitope-specific designed antibodies against α-synuclein used to examine soluble α-synuclein aggregates (size, morphology) in serum and CSF of Parkinson's patients; a companion preprint says they bind monomers and fibrils with low affinity, suggesting they recognise intermediate aggregate species — [Chem. Sci. HTML](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2022/sc/d2sc00066k), [PMC9710209](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9710209), [Imperial repository entry](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/74fd260f-7d34-4f49-b6ea-8ad44146822f)
- Recent Sormanni-group computational preprints surfaced on his page: SurfDiff (Jedlitzke, Wissett, Nguyen, Ghedin, Sormanni; bioRxiv Dec 2025, DOI 10.64898/2025.12.23.696170) — protein surface comparison by local structural alignment + neighbourhood analysis giving per-residue uniqueness/similarity and a "discriminability" score that rewards conservation across intended targets and penalises similarity to off-targets; authors report it tracks measured selectivity for small molecules, peptides, antibodies and mimetics and correlates with binding/neutralisation data across pathogen variants — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.23.696170.full.pdf), [PMC12776457](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12776457/)
- Also from the Sormanni publication list: AbNatiV2 (Ramon et al., bioRxiv Oct 2025; antibody/nanobody nativeness and pairing likelihood for design) and "Improving nanobody structure prediction with self-distillation" (bioRxiv Dec 2025), whose introduction states that significant progress has been made towards de novo epitope-specific nanobody design with generative models — [AbNatiV2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806v2), [nanobody self-distillation](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.01.691162v1.full)
- Group profile: Sormanni is a Royal Society University Research Fellow in the Yusuf Hamied Department of Chemistry (Cambridge), also a member of the Cambridge Engineering Biology IRC; the group builds data-driven methods for rational antibody design, solubility/stability prediction, combines method development with in vitro experiments, aims at targets hard to reach conventionally, and works with pharma and academic groups — [Eng. Bio. profile](https://www.engbio.cam.ac.uk/node/8411), [Chemistry profile](https://www.ch.cam.ac.uk/person/ps589)

**Generative / epitope-conditioned structure-based design (2023-2026)**
- RFantibody (Bennett, Watson, ... Baker), "Atomically accurate de novo design of antibodies with RFdiffusion", Nature (online Nov 2025; Jan 2026 issue), DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09721-5: fine-tuned RFdiffusion + yeast display screening; designs VHHs, scFvs and full antibodies to user-specified epitopes; VHH binders tested against four disease-relevant epitopes; cryo-EM confirmed binding pose for influenza haemagglutinin and C. difficile toxin B binders; initial affinities tens to hundreds of nM, OrthoRep affinity maturation took some to single-digit nM; Baker said the designs lack clinical-grade affinity and are a proof of concept; code free for academic and commercial use — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5), [PubMed 41193805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41193805/), [IPD news](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/11/rfantibody-in-nature), [GEN summary](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/ai-designed-antibodies-achieve-atomic-precision-to-enhance-drug-discovery/)
- Chai-2 (Chai Discovery), "Zero-shot antibody design in a 24-well plate" (bioRxiv, Jul 2025, not peer-reviewed in sources seen): report states ~16% hit rate, <=20 antibodies/nanobodies tested per target, 52 targets with no pre-existing antibody/nanobody binder in the PDB; successful binders for 26 of 52 targets (~50%); a secondary write-up gives 15.5% average (20.0% VHH, 13.7% scFv); picomolar KD for IL7Ra, PD-L1, PDGFRb, Insulin R and low-nM for TNFa; authors claim >=3-fold better hit rate than next-best method (Chai marketing claims ~100-fold vs prior computational methods) — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.05.663018.full.pdf), [Chai page](https://www.chaidiscovery.com/news/introducing-chai-2), [BiopharmaTrend (secondary)](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/news/chai-discovery-releases-foundation-model-for-zero-shot-antibody-design-with-1620-hit-rates-1309/)
- Germinal (Arc-affiliated preprint; bioRxiv Sep 2025, v3 / PMC12485712): epitope-targeted de novo antibodies/nanobodies; on four diverse targets experimental success 4-22% testing only 43-101 designs per antigen; validated nanobodies express in mammalian cells with nM affinities; preprint estimates ~1.2-1.5% for RFdiffusion + AF3 filtering (modelling assumption, not measured head-to-head) — [bioRxiv v3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421v3.full), [PMC12485712](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485712/)
- BoltzGen (MIT; bioRxiv Nov 2025): nanobody designs, 15 per target, gave nM binders for 6/9 novel targets and 4/5 benchmark targets; weakest on viral-target nanobodies (e.g. 1/7 designs for Penguinpox, 7/7 for haemagglutinin with at best ~2 uM); a later version notes BoltzGen v0 had 0 hits on both of those targets — [BoltzGen bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494v2.full), [Tamarind summary (secondary)](https://www.tamarind.bio/blog/boltzgen-validated-de-novo-binder-design-for-diverse-targets)
- mBER: described as an open-source antibody-format binder-design framework doing VHH design via backpropagation through AlphaFold-Multimer; no wet-lab hit rates found; a vendor blog's claim of 10-20% hit rates across Boltzgen/Germinal/RFantibody/mBER is unverified — [Tamarind 2026 overview (secondary)](https://www.tamarind.bio/blog/a-comprehensive-introduction-to-ai-for-proteins)
- tFold system (Wu, Zhao, Wu et al., Nat. Commun. 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-67361-9): structure-prediction (tFold-Ab/Ag) + virtual screening + de novo epitope-specific design; designs against influenza HA, PD-1, PD-L1, SARS-CoV-2 RBD validated by phage display + SPR with nM affinity (abstract only) — [Twist listing](https://twistbioscience.com/resources/publication/de-novo-design-epitope-specific-antibodies-structure-driven-computational)
- Absci zero-shot design (Shanehsazzadeh et al., bioRxiv 2023, v1-v3): trastuzumab/HER2 model system; designed HCDR3 (later all three heavy-chain CDRs) with HER2 structure + framework, training excluded HER2 binders and homologs; >1 million designed variants screened, 421 binders characterised by SPR, 3 bound tighter than trastuzumab; low-nM binders; also tested against VEGF-A, Omicron RBD and one undisclosed target (press); company-issued, not peer-reviewed in sources seen — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.08.523187.full.pdf), [Absci poster](https://www.absci.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Design-of-Functional-Developable-and-Potent-Antibodies-Using-Generative-AI.pdf), [GEN Edge](https://genengnews.com/artificial-intelligence/zero-shot-moonshot-absci-uses-ai-platform-to-create-and-validate-de-novo-antibodies)
- Origin-1 (Absci, bioRxiv Jan 2026): generative platform for de novo antibodies against novel epitopes; cryo-EM confirmed designed complexes; targets not GPCRs (details not retrieved) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.14.699389v2.full)
- Membrane protein / GPCR: JAM preprint (bioRxiv May 2025) reports hundreds of de novo VHHs to CXCR4 and CXCR7, best binders pM to low-nM, a subset acting as CXCR7 agonists ("first computationally designed antibody GPCR agonists" per authors), and >300 further agonists generated by "experiment-guided steering" with extra inference-time compute, one with EC50 near the natural ligand SDF1a; their earlier single GPCR design had weak affinity and no function — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.28.656709v1.full); a review says GPCR antibody design has converged on structure-based generative models — [PMC12819051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12819051/)

**Why oligomer/conformation-specific targets are hard (stated in sources)**
- For many misfolding-disease proteins the native ensemble is intrinsically disordered, making conformation-selective design harder; oligomers are transient and often a minor fraction relative to monomer and fibril — [PMC3949727 (generic oligomer-specific antibody design)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3949727/), [PMC7306997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7306997)
- Alternative strategies noted: avidity-based oligomer selectivity via divalent IgG (up to 1500-fold stronger binding than monovalent format, 2014 method) and conformationally constrained peptide immunogens — same search results as above ([PubMed 25130729](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25130729/))

### Inferences
- Sormanni-type methods are sequence-based and epitope-by-epitope, so they handle disordered epitopes with no structure; all 2025-26 generative methods I found were validated on structured targets (HA, toxin B, PD-L1, RBD, GPCRs, etc.). In my limited search I found no de novo generative antibody paper on an IDP/amyloid target, which suggests the niche between "sequence-based complementary design" and "structure-based diffusion design" is open. This is an absence-of-evidence inference from a limited search, not a proven gap.
- Hit-rate numbers are not comparable across papers (different targets, assay stringency, number of designs, definitions of "binder", number of targets). Chai-2's ~50% of targets and Germinal's 4-22% per target answer different questions.
- Initial de novo affinities (tens-hundreds nM for RFantibody, nM for BoltzGen/Germinal, pM-nM for Chai-2 selected targets) mostly need affinity maturation for clinical use; designed epitope specificity is typically validated by cryo-EM only for a handful of designs.
- The clinical record (prasinezumab missed primary endpoint, anti-tau class without aggregate benefit, trontinemab shuttle in Phase 3) shows that target engagement of a given epitope/conformer, and brain exposure, matter as much as binding; this is why conformer-selective epitope control and BBB delivery are both unsolved levers.

## 2. Binding-affinity / ΔΔG prediction: state of the art, failure modes, and datasets

### Takeaway
Antibody-antigen ΔΔG prediction does not currently generalise: published deep models look good (e.g. Pearson up to 0.87) under random or leaky splits but overfit the few hundred experimental points, FoldX reaches only r≈0.34 on AB-Bind, and the 2025 AbDesign benchmark found ML tools generalise poorly to new antibodies. Zero-shot protein language model and inverse-folding scores are the practical alternative, with inverse folding best on AbBiBench, and the community consensus is that far larger, more diverse and standardised datasets are needed.

### Cited Findings
- AB-Bind contains 1101 mutants with experimentally determined binding free-energy changes across 32 complexes; current affinity datasets are small and often not standardised between molecules — [AbDesign mAbs 2025 (via search summary)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19420862.2025.2567319)
- SKEMPI 2.0 antibody-antigen single-point mutations come from fewer than 50 complexes — [Hummer et al., Nat. Comput. Sci. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00823-8)
- Hummer, Schneider, Chinery, Deane, Nat. Comput. Sci. 5:635-647 (Jul 2025), DOI 10.1038/s43588-025-00823-8: Graphinity (equivariant GNN on antibody-antigen structures) reaches test Pearson up to 0.87 on experimental ΔΔG, but appears to overfit the few hundred experimental points and is not robust to train-test cut-offs; authors generated ~1 million FoldX ΔΔG values and >20,000 Rosetta Flex ddG values to probe data needs; conclusion: current experimental data are insufficient, orders of magnitude more likely needed; diversity (CDR sequence identity, substitution types, mutated positions) matters as much as size; FoldX correlates only weakly with AB-Bind (r≈0.34) — [PMC12374841](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12374841/), [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:cd85076f-5a05-4a93-ac39-0260b614aa1e), [bioRxiv 2023 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.05.17.541222.full.pdf); a secondary blog quotes r=0.89, the abstract figure 0.87 is the one to trust
- Random train-test splits on small AB-Bind data produce high correlations that are overfitting; mutation-level vs complex-based CV splits are needed — [search summary pointing to Nat. Comput. Sci. paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00823-8)
- AbDesign (mAbs 17(1):2567319, Oct 2025; bioRxiv Jun 2025): new dataset of 7 antigens x 2 antibodies each, varied CDR-H3 mutations measured by ELISA, each parent complex with a crystal structure; ML models perform poorly on it; physics-based FoldX is comparatively good at identifying mutants that retain binding; published models used inconsistent train/test partitions on SKEMPI v2 (one model's split ambiguous), so a "train-only" SKEMPI subset was built to make leakage explicit — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.09.658639v1.full), [mAbs](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19420862.2025.2567319)
- AbDist (mAbs 2026): a lightweight distance-based affinity model proposed as an interpretable benchmark; notes SKEMPI-derived subsets are not independent so cross-subset evaluation still leaks — [mAbs](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19420862.2026.2644655)
- Other 2025-26 ΔΔG items that surfaced (titles only, not opened): "Predicting mutational effects on protein binding from folding energy" (arXiv 2507.05502) and CIR-DDG (arXiv 2608.27530, residual correction of antibody-antigen affinity changes) — [arXiv 2507.05502](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05502), [arXiv 2608.27530](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27530)
- AbBiBench (Zhao et al., arXiv 2506.04235, Jun 2025): v1 = 155,853 mutated heavy-chain antibodies, nine antigens, 14 models; v2 = >184,500 measurements covering heavy- and light-chain mutations, 15 models, plus in vitro ELISA of generated antibodies; scores designs by how a protein model rates the whole antibody-antigen complex; structure-conditioned inverse-folding models performed best for both affinity correlation and generation; data on Hugging Face, code on GitHub (MSBMI-SAFE) — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04235v2), [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04235)
- Zero-shot language-model maturation: Hie et al., Nat. Biotechnol. (DOI 10.1038/s41587-023-01763-2): general protein LMs, with no antigen/structure/binding input, improved affinity up to 7-fold for four highly mature antibodies and up to 160-fold for three unmatured antibodies across seven antibodies, two rounds, <=20 variants each — [PMC10869273](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10869273/)
- Mason et al., Nat. Biomed. Eng. 5:600-612 (2021), DOI 10.1038/s41551-021-00699-9: ~1x10^4 trastuzumab variants made by CRISPR-HDR in mammalian cells, screened for HER2 binding and deep-sequenced; networks applied to ~10^8 in silico variants predicted ~10^6 HER2-specific; 30 random variants all retained binding — [ETH record](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/58188776-da46-4107-8c45-ba84680d2f23), [DOI link](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/s41551-021-00699-9)
- Absolut! (Robert, Akbar, Pavlović, Widrich ... Greiff; Nat. Comput. Sci. 2(12):845-865, Dec 2022, DOI 10.1038/s43588-022-00372-4): lattice-based synthetic antibody-antigen structures with known paratope/epitope/affinity; ranking of methods on Absolut! data matched ranking on experimental data for both sequence- and structure-based tasks; but Absolut! cannot produce atomic-resolution complexes and is used as a black-box oracle (e.g. AntBO) — [FHNW record](https://irf.fhnw.ch/entities/publication/76b992ca-f966-4ff7-9131-c268b166d4bd), [AntBO arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.12570)
- Developability prediction shows the same generalisation problem: Ginkgo AbDev competition (Sep 8-Nov 18 2025; 113 teams, 25 countries; GDPa1 train set of 246 clinical antibodies, blinded test of 80): best Spearman on test was 0.708 hydrophobicity, 0.392 thermostability, 0.356 polyreactivity, 0.337 self-association, 0.310 titer; CV scores consistently exceeded held-out test performance; conclusion: datasets too small/heterogeneous, need larger standardised data (mAbs 2026) — [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f1c61639-22e2-4737-b651-f18888f6b507), [Hugging Face announcement](https://huggingface.co/blog/ginkgo-datapoints/2025-abdev-competition), [EPMC mirror](https://www.omicsdi.org/dataset/biostudies-literature/S-EPMC12928636)

- AlphaSeq (A-Alpha Bio; yeast-mating assay read out by NGS barcodes, up to ~1M interactions per experiment): Sci. Data 2022 SARS-CoV-2 antibody dataset — three phage-display seed sequences, variants designed in silico, 104,972 variants built into the library, affinity values from 37 pM to 22 mM; subsets released on Hugging Face (log10 Kd score; binders usually VHH or scFv) including data tied to the 2025 AlphaBind model paper (mAbs); exact licence/location not confirmed — [Sci. Data 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41597-022-01779-4), [HF README](https://huggingface.co/spaces/aalphabio/README/blob/main/README.md), [AlphaBind mAbs 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19420862.2025.2534626), [Owl Posting AlphaSeq (secondary)](https://www.owlposting.com/i/147105147/alphaseq)
- ANDD, "A Unified Dataset for Antibody and Nanobody Design" (Sci. Data, Feb 2026): compiled from 15 sources; 48,683 antibody/nanobody sequences, 9,557 affinity values (Zenodo lists 9,569); largely aggregated from databases and patents, and per the search summary about half the affinity values are predicted rather than measured (not verified in the paper) — [Sci. Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-06878-0), [Zenodo ANDD](https://zenodo.org/records/16894086)
- AbCDR-Binding (Zenodo, Feb 2026): six curated affinity datasets including one with N=11,052 single and combinatorial mutants binding fluorescein (mutant series of few parents, not diverse antibodies) — [Zenodo 18762978](https://zenodo.org/records/18762978)
- A 2026 high-throughput ML-aided antibody discovery study for cell-surface antigens reports SPR kinetics for 335 verified antibodies, characterisation of 486 antibodies and releases its sequencing data — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405471226001274)
- Adaptyv EGFR competition (2024 round; bioRxiv Apr 2025): >1,800 designs submitted over two rounds, 601 characterised for expression and binding; best optimised binder 1.21 nM (Cradle; vs 9.94 nM for a scFv cetuximab control and 5.18 nM runner-up per Cradle's blog), best de novo 82 nM; the community paper also lists other open datasets such as the AIntibody challenge — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.17.648362.full.pdf), [Cradle blog](https://cradle.bio/blog/adaptyv2), [BenchBB post](https://adaptyvbio.substack.com/p/introducing-benchbb-and-the-community)
- Secondary and unverified: an oncodaily report says Adaptyv ran binding assays for a 2026 AI binder-design campaign by Anthropic (published 18 Aug 2026; 354 binders, 15 targets), with two labs agreeing on 89% of 1,235 designs measured at both sites (Cohen's kappa 0.71) — I did not find the primary publications — [OncoDaily (secondary)](https://oncodaily.com/technology/anthropic570775)

### Inferences
- The consistent message across Hummer 2025, AbDesign 2025, AbDev 2026 and AbDist 2026 is that evaluation protocol (held-out complex/antibody, deduplicated by CDR identity) matters more than architecture; for a student project, any new predictor must be benchmarked with complex-level or antibody-cluster-level splits, and ideally on a new wet-lab set.
- A realistic baseline stack is FoldX/Flex ddG (best at "does it still bind"), PLM/inverse-folding zero-shot scores (AbBiBench, Hie 2024), and simple distance baselines (AbDist); beating them on out-of-distribution antibodies is currently an unsolved task.
- The datasets that exist are mostly (i) deep mutational series around one or a few parents (trastuzumab, SARS-CoV-2 seeds, fluorescein), (ii) compilations with heterogeneous assays (ANDD), or (iii) developability readouts on ~10^2 clinical antibodies. What is missing is a large set of structurally diverse antibody-antigen pairs with affinity from one standardised assay, including negatives (non-binders) and epitope-resolved labels.
- Predicted-vs-measured correlations in the binding literature mix tasks (ranking mutants of one antibody vs ranking unrelated antibodies); a Spearman of 0.3-0.4 on developability and r≈0.34 for FoldX on AB-Bind suggest realistic expectations for cross-antibody generalisation are low.

### Gaps
- Koenig et al. 2017 (Genentech), Warszawski et al. 2019, Flex ddG (Barlow 2018), SKEMPI 2.0 (Jankauskaite 2019) primary papers: not retrieved in this session; SKEMPI 2.0 total size not confirmed; the Nature Comput. Sci. paper's own AB-Bind/SKEMPI numbers beyond those above were not read.
- "Tsuchiya/Rosace" dataset named in the brief with a question mark: I could not identify or confirm any antibody-affinity dataset under that name; treat as unverified.
- CoV-AbDab, SAbDab, OAS documentation and sizes: not retrieved this session (only a Nat. Commun. snippet saying SAbDab has ~9,000 structures vs ~230,000 PDB entries).
- Quantitative per-model Spearman values on AbBiBench and CHIMERA-Bench were not retrieved.
- No source found for open 2024-2026 datasets with measured affinity for >10^4 diverse antibodies from a single assay; the nearest are AlphaSeq (10^5 variants of a few seeds) and AbCDR-Binding (11,052 mutants of one parent). Ginkgo/Adaptyv antibody-specific binding datasets beyond the AbDev developability set were not found.

## 3. Experimental methods, costs, Cambridge ecosystem and open data

### Takeaway
Library size and format set what a computational student can learn from an experiment: phage ~10^9-10^10, yeast below ~10^9, ribosome/mRNA display up to ~10^13-10^15 (vendor/secondary figures), with yeast display + FACS/NGS and AlphaSeq-style mating assays producing most large quantitative datasets and ELISA/SPR/BLI giving the validation numbers. In Cambridge the natural partners are the Centre for Misfolding Diseases / Chemistry of Health complex, the Sormanni group itself, and the Cambridge Antibody Technology/AstraZeneca lineage, but I could not confirm current facility-level contacts.

### Cited Findings
- Display-technology library sizes: phage libraries limited by bacterial transformation to ~10^9-10^10 independent sequences; yeast display typically below ~10^9 and limited by cell-surface capacity; mRNA display reaching >10^13 molecules (about 10,000-fold phage complexity per one thesis chapter); ribosome display listed as <10^15, but complexes are fragile and mRNA display works best for small proteins — [Creative Biolabs (vendor)](https://www.creative-biolabs.com/phage-display-vs-ribosome-display.html), [RSC Mol. BioSyst. 2016 review](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2016/mb/c6mb00219f), [Caltech thesis](https://thesis.library.caltech.edu/2099/3/2_Chapter2.pdf), [Nano Convergence table](https://nanoconvergencejournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40580-017-0103-4/tables/1)
- Yeast surface display with NGS readout is the format used for RFantibody screening (and affinity maturation with OrthoRep) — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5), [IPD news](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/11/rfantibody-in-nature)
- Mammalian-cell CRISPR-HDR display of ~10^4 trastuzumab variants with deep sequencing was used to generate training data (Mason 2021) — [ETH record](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/58188776-da46-4107-8c45-ba84680d2f23)
- AlphaSeq yeast-mating assay: interaction strength proportional to mating efficiency, paired barcodes read by NGS, up to ~1M interactions per experiment, yields log10 Kd-scale scores; exclusively licensed by A-Alpha Bio from a 2017 PNAS method — [Owl Posting (secondary)](https://www.owlposting.com/i/147105147/alphaseq), [HF README](https://huggingface.co/spaces/aalphabio/README/blob/main/README.md)
- Cross-lab concordance matters: Anthropic-campaign report (secondary) cites 89% agreement and kappa 0.71 between two labs, and the AbDev competition found CV-to-test drops, both pointing to assay noise and batch effects as limiting factors — [OncoDaily (secondary)](https://oncodaily.com/technology/anthropic570775), [Hugging Face AbDev](https://huggingface.co/blog/ginkgo-datapoints/2025-abdev-competition)
- Pricing (third-party only): Adaptyv Bio list price reported from $149 per protein including two replicates at standard delivery, kinetics (KD, kon, koff) against catalogue targets, ~3 weeks turnaround; I found no confirmation on Adaptyv's own site and no separate BLI vs SPR price — [AIwiki summary (third-party)](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/adaptyv_bio), [Adaptyv skills doc](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@saibolla/ada@0.1.3/skills/adaptyv/reference/experiments.md)
- Validation formats seen in the literature: ELISA (AbBiBench v2, AbDesign), SPR after phage display (tFold), SPR of 421 binders (Absci), yeast display titration plus cryo-EM for a few designs (RFantibody, Origin-1) — links in sections 1-2
- Cambridge ecosystem: the Centre for Misfolding Diseases is housed in the Chemistry of Health building, funded by £17.6M from the UK Research Partnership Investment Fund plus contributions from Elan Pharmaceuticals and AstraZeneca; the page (written at the building's opening) lists co-directors Dobson, Knowles and Vendruscolo, and Wren Therapeutics as first incubator spin-out — [Cambridge news](https://www.cam.ac.uk/news/new-research-facility-for-neurodegenerative-disorders-opened-in-cambridge), [Cambridge Network](https://www.cambridgenetwork.co.uk/news/new-research-facility-neurodegenerative-disorders-opens-cambridge)
- Sormanni is based in the Yusuf Hamied Department of Chemistry, with a CMD profile and a link to a group website; the group states it works with pharma and other academic groups — [CMD profile](https://www.cmd.ch.cam.ac.uk/person/ps589), [Eng. Bio. profile](https://www.engbio.cam.ac.uk/node/8411)
- Cambridge Antibody Technology (CAT): founded 1989 by David Chiswell and Greg Winter with John McCafferty and the MRC, began at the MRC labs in Cambridge; discovered adalimumab (D2E7, later Humira) and was behind belimumab; acquired by AstraZeneca in 2006 (£702m) and combined with MedImmune into AstraZeneca's biologics R&D; Winter shared the 2018 Chemistry Nobel for phage display of antibodies — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Antibody_Technology), [ON Helix](https://onenucleus.com/blog/cambridge-biotech-back-future) (secondary sources)
- Cambridge-linked α-synuclein nanobody work (Dobson/Crick-affiliated groups) and Aprile-Sormanni-Vendruscolo-Knowles-Dobson papers show a track record of in-house expression, aggregation kinetics and C. elegans assays for these targets — [Crick record](https://www.crick.ac.uk/research/publications/nanobodies-raised-against-monomeric-a-synuclein-inhibit-fibril-formation-and-destabilize-toxic-oligomeric-species), [PMC5479649](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5479649/)

### Inferences
- Because the Aβ/αSyn designs from Sormanni's lab were validated by aggregation kinetics, oligomer detection assays and C. elegans (not only affinity), a student's computational designs against IDPs will need kinetic/biophysical aggregation assays and conformer-resolved binding, which are available in the CMD/Knowles-Vendruscolo ecosystem but are not standard antibody-company assays.
- For a computational student with no wet lab, a pragmatic route is: (i) public data (AbBiBench, ANDD, AlphaSeq HF, AbDev), (ii) outsourced BLI (Adaptyv-type service at ~$100-ish per protein per third-party price, unverified) for small design sets, (iii) collaboration within the Sormanni group/CMD for IDP-specific assays.
- The CAT/AstraZeneca lineage means industrial antibody-discovery know-how is geographically close, but whether students can access it depends on collaborations that I could not verify.

### Gaps
- No sourced cost figures for DNA synthesis (oligo pools/gene fragments), yeast/phage library construction, FACS sorting, NGS, cell-free expression, or full-IgG production; only the third-party Adaptyv number above.
- Single-cell BCR sequencing, cell-free expression, ribosome/mRNA display protocols, DMS/Tite-Seq methodology: no primary sources retrieved in this session (background knowledge only, deliberately not written as findings).
- Current Cambridge facilities and contacts not confirmed: AstraZeneca Cambridge (Granta Park/Biologics Engineering) antibody discovery group, MRC LMB (Winter/McCafferty heritage), Kaminski lab (Chemical Engineering), Cambridge Institute for Medical Research/UK DRI, Sanger, CPI, Cambridge Biophysics/protein production facilities, UK DRI Cambridge: the search returned nothing on them. The CMD leadership page is outdated (written at opening); current directors should be checked on the CMD site.
- Whether Aprile's lab (listed in an Imperial College repository) is currently a collaborator: not verified.
- Whether there is a 2025-26 open dataset with >10^4 diverse antibodies and measured affinity AND developability: none found.

## 4. Top 15 most important papers (with what they show and the limitations reported)

### Takeaway
The fifteen below combine the Sormanni lineage (1-4), the 2025-26 generative-design wave (5-9, 15), the benchmarks that show generalisation failures (10-13) and zero-shot/ML maturation work (14). For papers I could not read, the limitations column reflects only what appeared in search abstracts/summaries and is marked.

### Cited Findings
1. Sormanni, Aprile, Vendruscolo (2015) PNAS 112:9902, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1422401112 — first rational design of antibodies to specific epitopes in IDPs (Aβ42, α-syn, IAPP) via complementary peptides grafted into a single-domain scaffold. Limitations stated: antibody generation to specific epitopes is laborious, especially for poor antigens (motivation); further limitations not retrieved — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.1422401112), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26216991)
2. Aprile et al. (2017) Sci. Adv. 3:e1700488 — antibody scanning panel across Aβ42; two antibodies selectively inhibit primary or secondary nucleation, reduce toxicity in C. elegans; method proposed for small, broad-coverage antibody libraries. Limitations not retrieved — [PMC5479649](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5479649/)
3. Aprile et al. (2020) PNAS 117:13509, "Rational design of a conformation-specific antibody for the quantification of Aβ oligomers" — antigen scanning + epitope mining gives DesAb-O, oligomer-selective, works in vitro, in worms and mouse tissue. Limitations not retrieved — [PMC7306997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7306997)
4. Kulenkampff et al. (2022) Chem. ## 5. Open problems named by reviews and perspectives (2023-2026) for specificity, affinity and epitope control

### Takeaway
Reviews and benchmark papers converge on four open problems: controlling the epitope (screens return antibodies to unintended sites), data scarcity and leakage (SAbDab ~9,000 structures vs ~230,000 in PDB; affinity sets with <50 complexes), weak de novo affinity needing maturation, and scarce developability data. Brain delivery and conformer-selective targeting are open for neurodegeneration, but I found no 2023-2026 review specific to disordered/amyloid targets in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Epitope control: a 2025 Frontiers in Immunology review says isolating antibodies that bind a precisely defined epitope is a major hurdle because campaigns can return antibodies to unintended epitopes with reduced or absent therapeutic effect — [Front. Immunol. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2025.1571371/full) (attribution of this statement among the three Frontiers pages returned is uncertain)
- A 2025 mAbs review states that discovering epitope-specific antibodies that target therapeutically relevant regions remains challenging — [mAbs 2025 review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19420862.2025.2528902)
- Data scarcity / overfitting: a 2025 Nat. Commun. paper says epitope-specific de novo design is hard because of data scarcity and the structure of antibody-antigen interactions, noting ~9,000 structures in SAbDab vs ~230,000 in the PDB, which increases overfitting risk — [Nat. Commun. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67361-9)
- Developability data: a 2025 Frontiers review states there is "a great scarcity of data on developability points", so many methods enumerate binders then filter computationally — [Front. Immunol. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2025.1571371/full)
- Affinity/conformation: a Frontiers review warns AI-generated de novo sequences may reduce conformational flexibility, potentially compromising affinity and functionality — [Front. Drug Discov. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/drug-discovery/articles/10.3389/fddsv.2025.1628789/full) (attribution uncertain)
- Benchmark papers' own diagnoses: Hummer 2025 (data volume and diversity), AbDesign 2025 (inconsistent splits, poor generalisation, need for diverse consistent datasets), AbDev 2026 (small heterogeneous datasets, CV-test gap) — see section 2 links
- Membrane targets: GPCR antibody design has converged on structure-based generative models; JAM's results are preprint-level — [PMC12819051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12819051/), [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.28.656709v1.full)
- Brain delivery: TfR1-shuttle bispecifics (trontinemab) are in Phase 3 but only early data are public — see section 1 links
- Additional 2025-26 reviews surfaced but not read: Frontiers Immunology 2026 (fimmu.2026.1802038), Springer "Revolutionizing oncology: the role of AI as an antibody design and optimization tool", Drug Discovery News "Can better training data fix AI antibody design?" — [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1802038/full), [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-025-00764-4), [DDN](https://www.drugdiscoverynews.com/can-better-training-data-fix-ai-antibody-design-17211)

### Inferences
- Synthesis: the unsolved problem is no longer "can a model make a binder" but "can it make a binder to a chosen epitope or conformer, with predictable specificity, with data we can trust". Specificity (off-target and conformer selectivity) is far less benchmarked than affinity; SurfDiff-style surface-discriminability scoring is one 2025 attempt to quantify selectivity a priori.
- For neurodegeneration, the combination of disordered/ensemble epitopes, low-abundance oligomers, PTMs, and BBB transport multiplies the difficulty; no source I retrieved shows a computational method solving more than one of these simultaneously.

### Gaps
- No 2023-2026 review specific to antibody design against IDPs/amyloids or on cryptic epitopes and PTM-specific antibodies was retrieved; the reviews above are general AI-antibody-design reviews.
- The actual text of the 2025-26 reviews (limitation/future-work paragraphs) was not read; only search summaries.
- No source retrieved on specificity/polyreactivity prediction benchmarks for de novo designs, nor on how designed epitope accuracy is measured across methods (beyond CHIMERA-Bench's described protocol).
