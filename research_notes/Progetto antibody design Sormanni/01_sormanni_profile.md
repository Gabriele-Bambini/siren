## 3. The 5-8 most important papers: problem, method, results, stated limitations, future work

### Takeaway
Seven papers are covered below at the depth the sources allow. Method and results are taken from abstracts and search summaries. **Stated limitations and future work could not be retrieved for any of them (no discussion section was read).** Only limitations implied by abstracts are given, and they are labelled.

### Cited Findings
**A. CamSol (J Mol Biol 2015)**
- Problem: poor protein solubility limits the development of proteins and antibodies as drugs; screen variants by sequence. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25451785/)
- Method: a sequence-based predictor gives a score per residue (its effect on overall solubility), forming an "intrinsic solubility profile"; the overall score ranks variants by solubility, supporting rational design of more soluble mutants. — [Scholar lookup / abstract fragments](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?title=The+CamSol+method+of+rational+design+of+protein+mutants+with+enhanced+solubility&author=P+Sormanni&publication_year=2015&journal=J+Mol+Biol&pages=478-490&doi=10.1016/j.jmb.2014.09.026&pmid=25451785)
- Server and licensing: [CamSol method page](https://www-vendruscolo.ch.cam.ac.uk/camsolmethod.html); [Cambridge Enterprise](https://licensing.enterprise.cam.ac.uk/product/camsol-method)

**B. CamSol on antibody libraries (Sci Rep 2017)**
- Problem: selecting soluble lead antibodies from a library. Result: fast sequence-only prediction; predicted and measured solubility highly correlated; thousands of sequences in under a minute on a laptop. — [Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-07800-w)

**C. Rational design of antibodies against disordered epitopes (PNAS 2015)**
- Problem: antibodies against intrinsically disordered proteins (no stable structure to dock against) are hard to obtain by conventional discovery.
- Method (as stated in the abstract): sequence-based design of one or more peptides complementary to a selected disordered epitope, then grafting of those peptides onto an antibody scaffold, in the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs). The authors state it allows antibodies "against virtually any target epitope within intrinsically disordered peptides and proteins or within disordered regions in structured proteins".
- Results: six single-domain antibodies designed against different epitopes in three disease-related disordered proteins and peptides (alpha-synuclein, Abeta42, IAPP).
- Source for all three: [PNAS summary via Altmetric/ResearchGate/Semantic Scholar results](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280536216_Rational_design_of_antibodies_targeting_specific_epitopes_within_intrinsically_disordered_proteins); [full PDF](https://www-vendruscolo.ch.cam.ac.uk/sormanni2015pnas.pdf); [blog commentary](https://www.blopig.com/blog/2016/02/designing-antibodies-targeting-disordered-epitopes/)
- Follow-up: the epitope-directed approach was later applied to Abeta oligomers. — [PNAS Abeta paper](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1919464117)

**D. Third generation antibody discovery methods (Chem Soc Rev review)**
- Frames computational rational design as the next generation after animal immunisation (first) and library display methods (second). The framing is inferred from the title only; abstract not retrieved. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328167878_Third_generation_antibody_discovery_methods_In_silico_rational_design)

**E. Fragment-based design for structured epitopes (Sci Adv 2022)**
- Problem: extend epitope-targeted design from disordered to structured epitopes.
- Method: build antibody binding loops (CDRs) by combining fragments from other proteins, then graft them onto antibody scaffolds.
- Results: six single-domain antibodies against three antigens, including the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain; all designs stable; nanomolar affinities to the intended targets "without in vitro affinity maturation"; similar predictions whether the input is a crystal structure or a computer-generated model, so a high-resolution structure is not strictly required; the pipeline "readily runs on a laptop".
- Source: [Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abp9540); [PMC open access](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9651861/); [news-medical summary of the preprint](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20210308/Researchers-present-fragment-based-computational-design-of-antibodies-that-target-structured-epitopes.aspx)

**F. AbNatiV (Nat Mach Intell 2024)**
- Problem: judge whether an antibody or nanobody sequence looks "native" (like natural repertoires) for hit selection, humanisation and engineering, with an interpretable score linked to immunogenicity likelihood (via search summary).
- Method: a transformer-based VQ-VAE (vector-quantised variational autoencoder) that scores how far a sequence sits from the distribution of native antibodies; gives a sequence-level score and a residue-level profile that guides which positions to mutate (via search summary).
- Availability: download and web server; licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial). — [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.28.538712.full.pdf); [Cambridge Enterprise](https://licensing.enterprise.cam.ac.uk/product/abnativ-method)
- Limitation implied by the follow-up paper: version 1 "operated on unpaired sequences, limiting applicability to conventional VH-VL antibodies". — [AbNatiV2 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf)

**G. AbNatiV2 and p-AbNatiV2 (bioRxiv Oct 2025; mAbs 2026)**
- Problem: AbNatiV1 handled unpaired chains only; nanobody classification and CDR-grafting sensitivity could improve.
- Method: new camelid repertoires sequenced; an enhanced architecture with models each trained on at least 20 million sequences; p-AbNatiV2, a cross-attention model fine-tuned on 3.7 million paired human sequences, providing residue- and sequence-level humanness for VH/VL pairs and learning pairing likelihood through noise-contrastive training.
- Results: improved nanobody nativeness classification on held-out and diverse test sets; more robust detection of nativeness changes upon CDR grafting; on held-out tests the native pair scores higher in 74% of cases, "substantially outperforming recent pairing models"; the native pair ranks in the top 5 of 51 candidate pairs in over 35% of cases.
- Source: [mAbs](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2646361); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf); [mAbs PDF](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/19420862.2026.2646361) (all via search summary)
- Note: a top-5-of-51 rate of only "over 35%" shows that pairing prediction is far from solved.

### Inferences
- Beginner explanation (general background, not claims from the papers): a VQ-VAE compresses a sequence into a small set of learned "codes" and reconstructs it; sequences that look like natural antibodies reconstruct well, unusual ones reconstruct badly, and that reconstruction error is the nativeness score. Residues that reconstruct badly point to positions worth mutating.
- Beginner explanation of grafting: antibodies keep a stable framework and vary six loops (CDRs) that touch the target. Design copies or assembles loop sequences or fragments known or predicted to bind a given patch (epitope) and inserts them in the framework.
- The 2022 paper's "no affinity maturation" result and the AbNatiV2 pairing result define where the group sees headroom: affinity, and VH/VL pairing.

## 6. Software, web servers, and datasets

### Takeaway
Verified tools: CamSol (server plus Cambridge Enterprise licence) and AbNatiV/AbNatiV2 (Python package, Imperial GitLab code, web server). The lab has no public GitHub repositories under "Sormanni-Lab".

### Cited Findings
- AbNatiV2 source code, new trained models and paired humanisation pipeline: Imperial Computing GitLab, `gitlab.doc.ic.ac.uk/sormanni-lab/abnativ`. — [AbNatiV2 paper summary](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2646361) (repo not opened)

- CamSol: method page at the Vendruscolo lab and a Cambridge Enterprise licence entry. — [CamSol page](https://www-vendruscolo.ch.cam.ac.uk/camsolmethod.html); [Cambridge Enterprise](https://licensing.enterprise.cam.ac.uk/product/camsol-method)

### Inferences
