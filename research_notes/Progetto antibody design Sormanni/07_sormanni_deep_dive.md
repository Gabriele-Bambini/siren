## 2. Content of the 2025-2026 papers and preprints (method, numbers, validation, limitations)

### Takeaway
The group's recent output is (i) sequence-only "nativeness" and pairing models (AbNatiV2/p-AbNatiV2), (ii) a structure-based epitope-selectivity profiler (SurfDiff), (iii) data-efficient nanobody structure predictors trained with filtered self-distillation (NOT a nativeness paper), and (iv) a PLM-plus-biophysics polyreactivity classifier. I could reach only abstract-level text, so the authors' own limitations and future-work paragraphs are mostly NOT verified. The one limitation text I did see is the AbNatiV2 introduction's account of why AbNatiV1 needed updating.

### Cited Findings

**A. AbNatiV2 and p-AbNatiV2 (mAbs 2026; bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.10.31.685806)**
- Title: "Deep learning assessment of nativeness and pairing likelihood for antibody and nanobody design with AbNatiV2" (Ramon, Frassetto et al., Sormanni group); posted Oct 2025, published in mAbs 2026. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf); [mAbs DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2026.2646361)
- Beginner explanation of "nativeness": a score of how likely a sequence is to belong to natural immune repertoires, used for hit selection, humanisation and guiding de novo design. The model is a VQ-VAE (vector-quantised variational autoencoder: a neural network that compresses a sequence into a small set of discrete "codebook" codes and reconstructs it; sequences unlike the training data reconstruct badly, which gives a low nativeness score) trained with masked unsupervised learning on natural repertoires. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf); [Nature Machine Intelligence (AbNatiV1)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-023-00778-3)
- Stated reason for the update (limitations of AbNatiV1, from the preprint intro): the original "operated on unpaired sequences, limiting applicability to conventional VH-VL antibodies", and "its performance on nanobody nativeness was constrained by the limited number and diversity of nanobody repertoires available at the time." — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf)
- What was done: the authors sequenced new camelid repertoires, curated recent datasets, and built an ensemble of models each trained on at least 20 million sequences; AbNatiV2 improves nanobody nativeness classification on held-out and diverse test sets and detects nativeness changes after CDR grafting more reliably. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf)
- p-AbNatiV2 is a cross-attention pairing model fine-tuned on 3.7 million paired sequences, assessing VH/VL pairs jointly; the stated concern is that accounting for heavy/light-chain interactions when mutating risks compromising functionality and developability. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf)
- A Cambridge thesis describes p-AbNatiV2 as a paired VQ-VAE built on the unpaired AbNatiV2 models to assess humanness of paired sequences and predict pairing compatibility. — [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/a4b75bb4-15fe-41b4-b729-02827d348695)
- Numbers from a third-party summary of the paper: architecture modernised with rotary positional embeddings, gated attention and SwiGLU layers plus a focal reconstruction loss; human models trained on about 19.7M VH and 21.2M VL sequences from OAS; VHH classification versus "artificial" PSSM-generated sequences PR-AUC 0.984 (AbNatiV2) vs 0.961 (AbNatiV1); with CDRs grafted on a universal VHH framework AbNatiV2 flags nativeness loss in 90% of grafts vs 64% for AbNatiV1; p-AbNatiV2 identifies native VH/VL pairs 74% of the time on held-out tests. — [Omnicuris summary](https://www.omnicuris.com/medshots/daily_updates/abnativ2-deep-learning-antibody-design-nativeness); [Biology+AI Daily on X](https://x.com/BiologyAIDaily/status/2042231399558811803) **[UNVERIFIED: secondary summaries, not checked against the paper]**
- Wet-lab validation in AbNatiV2: not found in any snippet. The wet-lab validation I could confirm is in AbNatiV1 (below).

**B. AbNatiV (Nat Mach Intell 2024, vol 6, pp 74-91; doi 10.1038/s42256-023-00778-3)**
- VQ-VAE with four models: human VH, human V-kappa, human V-lambda and camelid VHH. It gives a residue-level nativeness profile that highlights regions with liabilities. Laboratory experiments show AbNatiV-humanised nanobodies "retain binding and stability at par or better than their wild type". Software and webserver are available. — [Nature Machine Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-023-00778-3)
- Humanisation: for nanobodies a dual-control strategy raises humanness without decreasing VHH-nativeness; for paired VH/VL it directly raises both VH-humanness and VL-humanness. — [Imperial GitLab (README as summarised)](https://gitlab.doc.ic.ac.uk/sormanni-lab/abnativ)
- The only author-stated limitation I can attribute is the one that AbNatiV2 states about AbNatiV1 (above). The AbNatiV1 paper's own discussion was not retrieved. The earlier work reportedly notes that a bigger and more diverse VHH training set could help; that is a prior-version remark, not an AbNatiV2 limitation. — [bioRxiv AbNatiV v2 PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.28.538712v2.full.pdf)

**C. SurfDiff (bioRxiv, 23 Dec 2025, doi 10.64898/2025.12.23.696170; PMC12776457)**
- Title: "SurfDiff: protein surface profiling for selective or broadly reactive epitope prioritisation in binder and immunogen design"; authors B. Jedlitzke, O. Wissett, T.H. Nguyen, E. Ghedin, P. Sormanni. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.23.696170.full.pdf); [PMC12776457](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12776457/)
- Problem: which epitopes on a target are best suited to the desired selectivity profile is "substantially under-investigated". The aim is a framework with single-residue resolution that supports one-to-one and one-to-many protein comparisons and quantifies design objectives: unique epitopes for selective binding, shared epitopes for broad binding, and intermediate cases. — [PMC12776457](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12776457/)
- Method (beginner version): it aligns small local patches of protein surfaces in 3D, compares each residue's physicochemical and spatial neighbourhood across proteins, and produces a residue-level uniqueness (or similarity) score. A "discriminability score" is high where a residue is conserved across the desired targets and dissimilar to undesired off-targets. It is target-only: it does not need the binder. — [PMC12776457](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12776457/)
- Validation: the target-only scores "reliably predict known experimental selectivity" for diverse binders (small molecules, peptides, antibodies, antibody mimetics) across viral antigens, cytokine isoforms, GPCR subtypes and serum albumin. This is retrospective benchmarking against literature data. No new wet-lab experiments were reported in what I saw. — [PMC12776457](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12776457/)
- Headline numbers (AUCs, correlations) and authors' limitations/future work: not retrieved (the PMC snippet cut off).

**D. Nanobody structure prediction with self-distillation (bioRxiv Dec 2025, doi 10.64898/2025.12.01.691162)**
- Correction to the brief: this preprint is about nanobody STRUCTURE prediction, not nativeness. Title: "Improving nanobody structure prediction with self-distillation". Authors: Ali, Greenig, Jaskolowski, Crnogaj, Smorodina, Zhao, Greiff, Sormanni. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.01.691162v1)
- Method (beginner version): self-distillation means using a trained predictor to label unlabelled sequences (VHH sequences with no experimental structures), then training on those synthetic structures. To keep synthetic structures plausible they built NanoKink, a sequence-based classifier of kinked vs extended H-CDR3 conformation, and filter by it. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.01.691162v1)
- Results: under a fixed training compute budget, the model trained on filtered synthetic data beats baseline models and NanobodyBuilder2 on a curated benchmark (lower mean H-CDR3 RMSD, fewer structural violations) and is competitive with AlphaFold3 at about two orders of magnitude lower per-structure inference time. No wet-lab component was mentioned. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.01.691162v1)
- Related preprint: "Disulphide and sequence-encoded conformational priors guide nanobody structure prediction" introduces NbForge, a lightweight folding model with blueprint- and disulphide-aware inductive biases and filtered self-distillation, sub-second inference, matching large predictors' coordinate accuracy. — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400785182_Disulphide_and_sequence-encoded_conformational_priors_guide_nanobody_structure_prediction) **[UNVERIFIED: abstract-level, authors not seen]**
- Authors' limitations/future work for both: not retrieved. The Sormanni group site's publication list is the place to find them. — [group publications page](https://www-sormanni.ch.cam.ac.uk/publications/all)

**E. Antibody non-specificity (polyreactivity) with PLMs and biophysics (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.04.28.650927; mAbs 2026, 18)**
- Title: "Prediction of Antibody Non-Specificity using Protein Language Models and Biophysical Parameters"; authors Sakhnini, Beltrame, Fulle, Sormanni and colleagues; the Cambridge group lists it as mAbs 2026, 18. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.28.650927.full.pdf); [Cambridge group page](https://www.cmd.ch.cam.ac.uk/person/ps589)
- Method (beginner version): "non-specificity" means an antibody sticks to unrelated molecules, which predicts poor pharmacokinetics. Two feature families are used: (i) sequence embeddings from protein language models (PLMs; large networks pre-trained on millions of sequences whose internal vectors summarise a sequence) and (ii) a large set of sequence-based biophysical descriptors; simple classifiers (e.g., logistic regression) are trained on these. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.28.650927.full.pdf)
- Data and results: trained on human and mouse antibody data from Boughter et al. (2020), tested on Jain et al. (2017), Shehata et al. (2019) and Harvey et al. (2022). Non-specificity is best predicted from the heavy variable domain and heavy-chain CDRs. The best PLM model (heavy-domain ESM-1v with logistic regression) reached 10-fold cross-validation accuracy of up to 71%. Isoelectric point emerged as a key driver. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.28.650927.full.pdf) (preprint abstract; the journal version may differ)
- Authors' limitations/future work and any wet-lab validation: not retrieved. The modest 71% accuracy is itself an indication of headroom.

**F. Sci Adv 2022: fragment-based design for structured epitopes**
- Citation as reported: Aguilar Rangel, Bedwell, Costanzi, Taylor, Russo, Bernardes, Ricagno, Frydman, Vendruscolo, Sormanni, Science Advances 8(45), eabp9540 (11 Nov 2022), doi 10.1126/sciadv.abp9540. — [Unimi repository record](https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/945481); bioRxiv preprint [PDF](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.02.433360v2.full.pdf)

Complementary peptides are assembled from beta-strand-facing fragments harvested from the PDB that match any subsequence of the target epitope of at least three residues. — [Unimi repository record](https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/945481)
- Validation: six single-domain antibodies were designed against different epitopes on three antigens, including the SARS-CoV-2 spike RBD, and tested experimentally. Affinity figures were not retrieved. — [Unimi repository record](https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/945481)

### Inferences
- A common pattern in the recent work: (1) a sequence-only model trained on very large natural repertoires (AbNatiV2, nanobody structure models) and (2) cheap property predictors (polyreactivity, solubility via CamSol) used as filters or scores. This matches the PhD advert's "repertoire-scale foundation models" and "multi-objective optimisation".
- The authors' own stated gap for AbNatiV1 (unpaired only; limited nanobody diversity) was addressed by AbNatiV2. A natural open gap is that nativeness is a proxy and not a measured property: no AbNatiV2 wet-lab study was seen. Pairing nativeness or polyreactivity scores with experimental feedback is therefore a plausible proposal angle, but it is my inference and not a statement from the authors.
- The polyreactivity model's ~71% accuracy and its reliance on small public datasets (Boughter, Jain, Shehata, Harvey) suggest more and better experimental non-specificity data (e.g., from display selections) would help. Again an inference.
- SurfDiff is target-only and retrospective, so a natural next step is binder design that uses its discriminability score as an objective, and wet-lab tests of its predicted selective epitopes. This is an inference; the authors' own future work was not retrieved.
- The "self-distillation" paper is about structure prediction, so a proposal that cites it as a nativeness method would be wrong.

## 4. AbNatiV2 code: licence, models, inputs, usability as a guidance signal or reward

### Takeaway
AbNatiV is a PyTorch/Lightning package on PyPI (latest 2.0.8 per the stats page) under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so non-commercial and share-alike only. It provides nativeness scores and humanisation tools; whether it exposes gradients is not documented in anything I saw. Using it as a differentiable guidance signal is technically plausible but unverified, and a black-box reward or filter is the safer assumption.

### Cited Findings
- PyPI package `abnativ`: latest version 2.0.8; licence Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0); summary "AbNatiV: a VQ-VAE-based assessment of the nativeness of antibodies". — [PyPI Stats page](https://pypistats.org/packages/abnativ); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/abnativ)
- The PyPI listing says the software is not to be used for commercial purposes. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/abnativ)
- The Imperial GitLab repository (sormanni-lab/abnativ) shows the same CC BY-NC-SA licence; a Cambridge GitLab mirror also exists. — [Imperial GitLab](https://gitlab.doc.ic.ac.uk/sormanni-lab/abnativ); [Cambridge GitLab](https://gitlab.developers.cam.ac.uk/ch/sormanni/abnativ)
- Listed dependencies include biopython, einops, freesasa, immunebuilder, matplotlib, mlflow, numpy, pandas, paretoset, pytorch-lightning, pyyaml, scipy, seaborn, torch, tqdm, wandb (also brewer2mpl, chroma, protein-topmodel, python-parallel). — [PyPI Stats page](https://pypistats.org/packages/abnativ)
- README as summarised: VQ-VAE producing an interpretable nativeness score and a residue-level nativeness profile; default scores from four training sets (human VH, V-kappa, V-lambda and camelid VHH); humanisation for nanobodies (dual-control: raise humanness without lowering VHH-nativeness) and for paired VH/VL. — [Imperial GitLab](https://gitlab.doc.ic.ac.uk/sormanni-lab/abnativ); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/abnativ)
- The AbNatiV2 preprint describes unpaired AbNatiV2 models and the paired p-AbNatiV2; whether the public repository already ships the v2 weights was not confirmed in snippets, although the PyPI version 2.0.x suggests it does. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685806.full.pdf); [PyPI Stats page](https://pypistats.org/packages/abnativ)
- The package list includes `paretoset`, which suggests Pareto-front (multi-objective) selection utilities, but the README text on this was not seen. — [PyPI Stats page](https://pypistats.org/packages/abnativ) **[inference from a dependency name only]**

### Inferences
- Because the model is implemented in PyTorch, gradients are available in principle for the continuous parts of the network, but sequence input is discrete and the VQ step needs a straight-through estimator (or a relaxed/soft one-hot input) to pass gradients. Whether the released code supports this was not verified. A safe design is to treat AbNatiV2 as a black-box score: use it as a reward in reinforcement-learning or best-of-N sampling, a reranking filter on generated candidates, or a regulariser in a discrete search such as MCMC or evolutionary search.
- The "residue-level nativeness profile" is a ready-made per-position signal, which could target mutations to low-nativeness positions. It is the most natural interface for guidance without gradients.
- CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 means a derivative tool that wraps it must also be released under the same licence and cannot be commercial. This is fine for a PhD but matters for any industry plan.
